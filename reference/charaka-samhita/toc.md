# Charaka Samhita (चरकसंहिता)

- Author / compiler: Agniveśa's tantra, revised (pratisaṃskṛta) by Caraka, as the colophons say ("agniveśakṛte tantre carakapratisaṃskṛte"). The colophons of these chapters also mention Dṛḍhabala's completion ("dṛḍhabalasaṃpūrite", mostly with "aprāpte"): 6.9-6.13, 6.15-6.18, 6.20, 6.22, 6.25-6.30, 7.1-7.12, 8.1-8.12. Tradition: Ayurveda (inferred).
- Subject and purpose of the book: a classical treatise of Ayurveda (internal medicine). Each chapter opens "athāto X vyākhyāsyāmaḥ" ("now we shall expound X"), so every chapter announces its own topic (column Purpose, text itself).
- Base edition: Yādavaśarman Trivikramātmaja Ācārya, 3rd ed. Nirnaya Sagar Press, Bombay 1941, with Cakrapāṇidatta's Āyurvedadīpikā (commentary NOT included in the e-text); e-text by Yamashita, Muroya, Yamanaka, Madhu K. P. (Kyoto), TEI by Dominik Wujastyk, as published by SARIT (source S1 in sources.md).
- Mapping: part = sthāna, chapter = adhyāya, verse = verse or prose paragraph as numbered in the edition (IDs PP.CC.V). In chapters 6.1 and 6.2 (Rasāyana, Vājīkaraṇa) the edition numbers verses inside four pādas; the pāda is the first part of V: 06.01.2.15 = pāda 2, verse 15. In Sūtrasthāna 1 a verse inserted in parentheses after verse 66 is 66.1 (and so on). Unnumbered prose paragraphs are P1, P2, ... in the chapter; each chapter's colophon is its last P line.
- Totals: 8 parts, 120 chapters, 10716 verse units (numbered verses and P paragraphs) extracted; traditional count unknown (the text states none).

Columns Sanskrit name: from the chapter's colophon (final -o written as -aḥ, avagraha mark dropped), converted to Devanagari. English name: literal rendering written by the extractor, (inferred), not from a source. Purpose: the chapter's own opening formula.
