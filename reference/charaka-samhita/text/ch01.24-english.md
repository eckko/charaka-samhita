01.24.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on blood and regimen (Vidhiśoṇitīya).
01.24.2 | Thus said the venerable Ātreya.
01.24.3a | In embodied beings, blood produced by following the regimen (vidhi) is pure, the regimen of place, time and habitual suitability (okasātmya) that has been set forth.
01.24.3b | ~
01.24.4a | For that pure blood endows living beings with strength, complexion, happiness and life-span; for (life) follows the blood.
01.24.4b | ~
01.24.5a | By wines that are spoiled, (taken) in excess, sharp and hot, and by other (drinks) of that kind; likewise by excess of salt and alkalis (kṣāra), by sour things and by pungent things too;
01.24.5b | ~
01.24.6a | by the use of kulattha, māṣa, niṣpāva and sesame oil; by (the use) of piṇḍālu, mūlaka and other green vegetables of all kinds,
01.24.6b | ~
01.24.7a | from the use (of the flesh) of water-born, marsh and burrow-dwelling animals and of the prasaha (animals that seize by force); (from the use) of curd, sour things, whey (mastu) and śukta, and of surā and sauvīraka;
01.24.7b | ~
01.24.8a | by eating incompatible, decomposed and putrid things; and of those who sleep by day after eating liquid, unctuous and heavy things;
01.24.8b | ~
01.24.9a | of those who eat to excess, and likewise of those who resort to anger, and to the sun and fire; from suppressing the urge to vomit, and from not letting blood at the (proper) time;
01.24.9b | ~
01.24.10a | By fatigue, injury and heat, likewise by indigestion (ajīrṇa) and eating on top of undigested food (adhyaśana), and from the nature of the Śarad season, the blood becomes vitiated.
01.24.10b | ~
01.24.11a | Then diseases born of blood, of various kinds, arise: mukhapāka (inflammation of the mouth), redness of the eyes, putrid odour of the nose and mouth,
01.24.11b | ~
01.24.12a | gulma, upakuśa, vīsarpa, raktapitta, pramīlaka, vidradhi (abscess), raktameha, pradara, vātaśoṇita,
01.24.12b | ~
01.24.13a | discoloration, weakness of the digestive fire (agni), thirst, heaviness of the body, burning heat, extreme weakness, loss of taste (aruci) and pain in the head,
01.24.13b | ~
01.24.14a | burning (vidāha) of food and drink, bitter and sour belching, exhaustion (klama), abundance of anger, confusion of the intellect, saltiness in the mouth,
01.24.14b | ~
01.24.15a | sweating, foul smell of the body, intoxication (mada), tremor, loss of voice, excess of drowsiness and sleep, and excessive seeing of darkness,
01.24.15b | ~
01.24.16a | itching (kaṇḍū), arus, koṭha, eruptions (piḍakā), kuṣṭha, carmadala and the like: all these disorders are to be known as based on blood.
01.24.16b | ~
01.24.17a | Those diseases which, though curable and properly treated with cold, hot, unctuous, dry and other (measures), are not cured, one should recognise as born of blood.
01.24.17b | ~
01.24.18a | In diseases of the blood one should carry out the treatment that removes raktapitta, and purgation, fasting and letting out of blood.
01.24.18b | ~
01.24.19a | One should let out the person's blood either according to the measure of strength and doṣa, or by (the standard of) the purity of the blood, or having carefully examined the āśaya (site).
01.24.19b | ~
01.24.20a | (Blood vitiated) by Vāta is reddish in appearance, non-slimy, frothy and thin; by Pitta the blood is yellow and dark and, because of heat, clots slowly.
01.24.20b | ~
01.24.21a | Vitiated by Kapha, it is slightly pale, slimy, stringy and compact; from a combination (of two doṣas) it has combined signs, and from all three together (sānnipātika) it has the signs of all three.
01.24.21b | ~
01.24.22a | Know as pure the blood that has the lustre of refined gold (tapanīya) and of the indragopa, that resembles the lotus and lac-dye (alaktaka), and that has the colour of the guñjā fruit.
01.24.22b | ~
01.24.23a | When the blood has been let out, food and drink that is neither too hot nor too cold, light and stimulating to digestion is beneficial; for then the body has unsteady blood, and the digestive fire (agni) is to be protected especially.
01.24.23b | ~
01.24.24a | The man whose complexion and senses are clear, who desires the objects of the senses, whose digester (paktṛ) and urges are unobstructed, who is endowed with happiness and possessed of contentment and strength, him they call a man of pure blood.
01.24.24b | ~
01.24.25a | But when the aggravated malas, singly or all together, the channels that carry blood and those that carry rasa and consciousness (saṃjñā),
01.24.25b | ~
01.24.26a | of a person habituated to unclean food, whose self is covered by rajas and delusion (moha), having obstructed (them), settle (there), then diseases arise:
01.24.26b | ~
01.24.27a | mada (intoxication), mūrcchāya (fainting) and saṃnyāsa (collapse). The discerning one should know that among these the strength increases successively in cause, signs and alleviation.
01.24.27b | ~
01.24.28a | When Vāta (vāyu) enters the weakened seat of the mind, then, agitating the mind, it confuses the person's consciousness.
01.24.28b | ~
01.24.29a | In the same way Pitta, and likewise Kapha, agitating the mind of men, brings consciousness into confusion; and the distinction here will be stated.
01.24.29b | ~
01.24.30a | One whose speech is halting, profuse and hurried, whose movements are unsteady and stumbling, and whose appearance is dry, dusky and reddish, one should know to be seized by the mada of Vāta.
01.24.30b | ~
01.24.31a | One whose speech is angry and harsh, who is fond of fighting and quarrels, and whose appearance is red, yellow or dark, one should know to be seized by the mada of Pitta.
01.24.31b | ~
01.24.32a | One whose speech is scanty and incoherent, who is given to drowsiness and sloth, pale, and intent on brooding, should be known as possessed by the mada (intoxication) of Kapha.
01.24.32b | ~
01.24.33a | In mada caused by sannipāta (all three doṣas combined) all these forms [appear]. Mada that has the appearance of liquor-mada arises and subsides quickly.
01.24.33b | ~
01.24.34a | That which is declared to be caused by liquor, that born of poison and that which arises from blood: all these madas do not occur without the triad of Vāta, Pitta and Kapha.
01.24.34b | ~
01.24.35a | Seeing the sky blue or black or red, he enters darkness and comes to again quickly.
01.24.35b | ~
01.24.36a | In mūrcchāya (fainting) arising from Vāta there are trembling, body-ache, severe pain of the heart, emaciation and a dusky-reddish complexion.
01.24.36b | ~
01.24.37a | Seeing the sky red or green-coloured or else yellow, he enters darkness and comes to again sweating.
01.24.37b | ~
01.24.38a | In mūrcchāya arising from Pitta, he is thirsty and burning with heat, his eyes are red, yellow and unsteady, his stools are loose, and he looks yellowish.
01.24.38b | ~
01.24.39a | Seeing the sky like a cloud, or covered by masses of darkness, he enters darkness and comes to again after a long time.
01.24.39b | ~
01.24.40a | In mūrcchāya arising from Kapha, he has heavy limbs, covered just as if by a wet hide, and he has watering of the mouth and nausea.
01.24.40b | ~
01.24.41a | [Mūrcchāya] from sannipāta has all the features and comes on like apasmāra; it fells the person quickly, but without the loathsome movements.
01.24.41b | ~
01.24.42a | When the doṣas of embodied beings have run their course, mada and mūrcchāya subside indeed by themselves; saṃnyāsa (collapse) does not [subside] without medicines.
01.24.42b | ~
01.24.43a | The very strong malas, lodged in the seats of life (prāṇa), seize the activity of speech, body and mind and cast the weak person down [into saṃnyāsa].
01.24.43b | ~
01.24.44a | That man, cast down by saṃnyāsa, become like a log and resembling the dead, is quickly parted from life, except [through] treatments that bear immediate fruit.
01.24.44b | ~
01.24.45a | As a wise man would quickly seize a vessel sinking in deep water before it has reached the bottom, so [should one seize] one afflicted by saṃnyāsa.
01.24.45b | ~
01.24.46a | Collyria (añjana), pressed-juice nasal drops (avapīḍa), medicated smokings, insufflations (pradhamana), pricking with needles, burning, and pressing between the nails are approved.
01.24.46b | ~
01.24.47a | Plucking the head-hair and body-hair, biting with the teeth also, and rubbing with ātmaguptā are beneficial for rousing him.
01.24.47b | ~
01.24.48a | Various sharp liquors, compounded and mixed with plenty of pungent [substances], should be poured repeatedly into his mouth.
01.24.48b | ~
01.24.49a | Likewise one should give the juice of mātuluṅga combined with mahauṣadha, and likewise sauvarcala salt mixed with liquor, sour liquids and kāñjika (sour fermented liquid).
01.24.49b | ~
01.24.50a | combined with hiṅgu and ūṣaṇa, until consciousness is awakened. When his consciousness is awakened, one should attend to him with light foods and
01.24.50b | ~
01.24.51a | with astonishing things, with reminders, indeed with things pleasant to hear, with clear sounds of songs and musical instruments, and with varied sights,
01.24.51b | ~
01.24.52a | with laxative purgation and vomiting (ullekhana), with medicated smokings, collyria and holding of mouthfuls (kavalagraha), with lettings of blood, and also with exercise and rubbing.
01.24.52b | ~
01.24.53a | When he has regained consciousness, the intelligent [physician] should treat the after-effect (anubandha), for his mind must be protected from the causes of loss of consciousness (pralaya).
01.24.53b | ~
01.24.54a | In mūrcchāyas and madas, for those who have received oleation and sweating, one should perform the five procedures according to the doṣa and according to strength.
01.24.54b | ~
01.24.55a | The administration of the twenty-eight-drug [preparation], likewise of the bitter ghee, or likewise of the great Ṣaṭpala (six-pala) [ghee], is praised.
01.24.55b | ~
01.24.56a | or the administration of triphalā with ghee, honey (kṣaudra) and sugar, or the administration of śilājatu, or also the administration of milk,
01.24.56b | ~
01.24.57a | or the administration of pippalīs or of citraka with milk; the administration of rejuvenatives (rasāyana) or of kaumbha ghee is praised.
01.24.57b | ~
01.24.58a | By letting of blood, and by attending to the scriptures, the good and also the strong-minded, the mada and mūrcchāya of embodied beings are allayed.
01.24.58b | ~
01.24.59a | Here, the two verses: Pure and impure blood, its causes, the diseases born of vitiation of blood, and the medicine for those diseases,
01.24.59b | ~
01.24.59c | ~
01.24.60a | the causes, signs and medicine of mada, mūrcchāya and saṃnyāsa: all this has been set forth in the chapter Vidhiśoṇitaka.
01.24.60b | ~
01.24.P1 | Thus, in the treatise composed by Agniveśa and redacted by Caraka, in the Śloka section (Ślokasthāna), the twenty-fourth chapter named Vidhiśoṇitīya.
01.24.P2 | Completed: the tetrad on planning (yojanā).
