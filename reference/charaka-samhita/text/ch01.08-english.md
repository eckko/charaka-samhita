01.08.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter called "Indriyopakramaṇīya" (On the Management of the Senses).
01.08.2 | Thus spoke the venerable Ātreya.
01.08.3 | Here, indeed, there are five senses, five sense-substances, five seats of the senses, five sense-objects and five sense-cognitions: so it has been said in the topic of the senses.
01.08.4 | But the mind is beyond the senses; it is called sattva, and some call it cetas. Its activity depends on the availability of its object and of the self, and it is the cause of the activity of the senses.
01.08.5 | Because of the variation of its own object, of the sense-objects and of resolve (saṅkalpa), and because of association with the qualities rajas, tamas and sattva, the mind (sattva) in one person is manifold; yet there is no plurality, for one thing does not operate at one time in many. Therefore the activity of all the senses is not simultaneous.
01.08.6 | With whichever quality the mind (sattva) constantly accompanies a person, the sages designate that mind as being of just that quality, because of its predominant persistence.
01.08.7 | The senses, led by the mind, are able to grasp objects.
01.08.8 | Among these, sight (cakṣus), hearing (śrotra), smell (ghrāṇa), taste (rasana) and touch (sparśana): these are the five senses.
01.08.9 | The five sense-substances are space (kha), air (vāyu), fire (jyotis), water (āpas) and earth (bhū).
01.08.10 | The five seats of the senses are the two eyes, the two ears, the two nostrils, the tongue and the skin.
01.08.11 | The five sense-objects are sound, touch, form (rūpa), taste (rasa) and smell.
01.08.12 | The five sense-cognitions are visual cognition and the rest; they, moreover, are born of the contact of sense, sense-object, mind (sattva) and self, and are momentary and determinate in nature. This is the set of five pentads.
01.08.13 | The mind, the object of the mind, the intellect and the self: this is the collection of the substances and qualities pertaining to the self (adhyātma), and the cause of engaging in and refraining from what is good and bad; and the action (karma) that rests on substance is what is called kriyā.
01.08.14 | Among these, although the senses, which are known by inference, consist of an aggregate of modifications of the five great elements, fire (tejas) is predominantly present in sight, space in hearing, earth in smell, water in taste and air (anila) in touch.
01.08.P1 | There, whatever nature a sense has, it predominantly grasps an object of just that same nature, because of its nature and because of its capacity (vibhutva).
01.08.15 | From excessive, deficient and wrong contact with its objects, the sense, together with the mind, undergoing disorder, leads to the impairment of its own cognition; but from fitting contact, regaining its normal state, it nourishes its own cognition.
01.08.16 | But of the mind, the object is what is to be thought (cintya).
01.08.P2 | There, for the mind and for mental cognition, those same proper, excessive, deficient and wrong contacts are the causes of the normal state and of disorder.
01.08.17 | There, for the non-affliction of the senses, together with the mind, while they are unafflicted, effort should be made toward their normal state by these means, namely: by wholesome (sātmya) contact with sense-objects; by properly carrying out actions after examining them well again and again with the intellect; and by resorting to what is opposite to the qualities of place, time and oneself.
01.08.P3 | Therefore everyone who wishes to do what is good for himself should always practise the whole of good conduct (sadvṛtta), keeping to mindfulness (smṛti).
01.08.18 | For one who practises it accomplishes two aims at once: health and mastery of the senses. That good conduct (sadvṛtta) we shall teach in full, O Agniveśa. It is as follows. One should honour gods, cows, brahmins, teachers, elders, adepts (siddha) and preceptors; tend the fire; wear approved herbs; perform ablution at the two times; frequently keep the channels of waste (malāyana) and the feet clean; have the hair, beard, body-hair and nails cut three times a fortnight; always wear unsoiled clothes, and be cheerful (sumanas) and fragrant; be well-dressed, with well-kept hair, regularly oiling the head, ears, nose and feet, taking medicated smoke (dhūma), the first to speak, pleasant-faced, a helper in difficulties, one who offers oblations, who sacrifices, who gives, who pays homage to crossroads, who presents offerings (bali), who honours guests, who gives rice-balls (piṇḍa) to the ancestors, who at the right time speaks what is beneficial, measured, sweet and meaningful, self-controlled, righteous (dharmātman), emulous in the means but not envious of the result, free from worry, fearless, modest, intelligent, highly energetic, skilful, forbearing, righteous (dhārmika), a believer (āstika), and one who serves those senior in discipline, intellect, learning, lineage and age, the adepts and the preceptors; one should walk about with an umbrella, a staff, a headdress and footwear, looking a yoke's length ahead; be given to auspicious practices; avoid dirty rags, bones, thorns, impure things, hair, husks, rubbish heaps, ashes, potsherds, and bathing and offering grounds; stop exercise before fatigue; be a kinsman to all living beings; be a pacifier of the angry, a comforter of the frightened, a helper of the distressed, true to one's word, given chiefly to conciliation (sāman), tolerant of others' harsh words, a destroyer of intolerance, one who sees the merit of tranquillity, and a destroyer of the causes of attachment and aversion.
01.08.19 | One should not speak untruth; not take another's property; not desire another's wife, nor another's wealth; not take pleasure in enmity; not do evil; not be evil even towards an evil person; not speak of others' faults; not seek to learn another's secret; not sit with the unrighteous, nor with those hated by the king, nor with the insane, nor with outcastes, nor with killers of embryos, nor with the mean, nor with the wicked; not mount faulty vehicles; not sit on a hard seat of knee height; not resort to a bed that is uncovered, without a pillow, narrow or uneven; not roam on rough mountain-tops; not climb a tree; not plunge into water with a fierce current; not sit in the shade of a river-bank; not move about near an outbreak of fire; not laugh loudly; not release wind (māruta) noisily; not give way to a yawn, a sneeze or laughter with the mouth uncovered; not pick the nose; not grind the teeth; not click the nails; not strike bones; not scratch the ground; not cut grass; not crush a clod; not move the limbs awkwardly; not look at the luminaries, or at what is undesirable, impure or inauspicious; not utter "hum" at a corpse; not step on the shadow of a shrine (caitya), a banner, a teacher, a venerable person or an inauspicious thing; not frequent at night temples, shrines, courtyards, crossroads, groves, cremation grounds or slaughter-places; not enter alone an empty house, nor a forest; not associate with women, friends or servants of evil conduct; not oppose superiors; not keep company with inferiors; not take pleasure in crookedness; not rely on an ignoble person; not cause fear; not indulge in rashness, or in excessive sleep, waking, bathing, drinking and eating; not remain long with the knees raised; not approach vicious animals (vyāla), nor fanged nor horned ones; one should avoid the east wind (purovāta), sun, dew and strong wind; not start a quarrel; not sit by the fire when not composed, nor when soiled after eating (ucchiṣṭa); nor warm oneself having put it below; not bathe without fatigue having gone, without washing the face, or naked; not touch the head with the bathing cloth; not strike the ends of the hair; after bathing, not wear those same two garments; not go out without touching gems, ghee, venerable and auspicious things, and flowers; not pass venerable and auspicious things keeping them on the left (apasavyam), nor others keeping them on the right.
01.08.20 | One should not take food without a gem in the hand, unbathed, in soiled clothes, without japa (recitation), without offering oblations to the deities, without making an offering to the ancestors, without giving to teachers, guests and dependants, without a pleasant fragrance, without a garland, with hands, feet and face unwashed, with an unclean mouth, facing north, distracted, with attendants who are not devoted, ill-mannered, impure or hungry, in impure vessels, in an improper place, at an improper time, in a crowded place, without giving the first portion to the fire, unsprinkled with sprinkling-water, unconsecrated with mantras, while reviling it, food that is reviled, or food served by an unfriendly person. One should not take stale food, except meat, greens (haritaka), dried vegetables, fruits and eatables (bhakṣya); one should not eat leaving no remainder, except curd, honey, salt, roasted barley-flour (saktu) and ghee (sarpis); one should not eat curd at night; one should not eat saktu alone, nor at night, nor after a meal, nor in large amount, nor twice, nor with water in between, nor bite it with the teeth (dvija) before eating.
01.08.21 | One should not sneeze, eat or lie down while not upright; one should not, when an urge (vega) is present, be engaged in other work; one should not discharge spittle, faeces or urine facing the wind, fire, water, the moon, the sun, the twice-born (dvija) or teachers; one should not urinate on the road, nor in a populated place, nor at mealtime; one should not release phlegm (śleṣman) or nasal mucus during japa, oblations, study, offerings (bali) or auspicious rites.
01.08.22 | One should not despise a woman, nor trust her excessively, nor tell her a secret, nor give her authority.
01.08.P4 | One should not engage in sexual intercourse (vyavāya) with a woman who is menstruating, sick, impure, inauspicious, of disagreeable appearance, conduct and manners, unskilled, uncompliant, unwilling, desiring another, another's wife, or of another kind (anyayoni); nor in what is not the yoni; nor in shrines, courtyards, crossroads, groves, cremation grounds, slaughter-places, water, or the abodes of herbs, the twice-born, teachers and gods; nor at the two twilights; nor among guests (atithiṣu); nor when impure, without having taken medicine, without having formed the intention, without arousal having arisen, without having eaten, when overfed, in an uneven position, pressed by urine or faeces, afflicted by fatigue, exercise, fasting or exhaustion, or in a place that is not private.
01.08.23 | One should not speak ill of the good or of teachers; one should not, while impure, perform abhicāra rites, worship of shrines (caitya) and venerable persons, or study.
01.08.24 | One should not practise study during unseasonal lightning, when the quarters are lit up, during a conflagration, an earthquake, a great festival, a fall of meteors, or the approach of a great planet (mahāgraha), on a moonless lunar day, or at the two twilights; nor otherwise than from the teacher's mouth; nor with omissions, excessively, wearily, out of tune, with unsteady words, too fast, slowly, too feebly, or in tones too high or too low.
01.08.25 | One should not abandon an agreement (samaya); not break an observance (niyama); not wander at night or in an improper place; not engage in eating, study, women or sleep at the twilights; not make friends with children, the old, the greedy, fools, the troubled or the impotent (klība); not be fond of indulgence in wine, gambling or prostitutes; not reveal a secret; not despise anyone; not be arrogant, unskilled, discourteous or envious; not speak ill of brahmins; not raise a stick against cows; not insult elders, teachers, assemblies or kings; not talk too much; not cast out kinsmen, the devoted, companions in hardship or those who know one's secrets.
01.08.26 | One should not be unsteady, nor over-elated in mind (sattva); not one who does not support his servants; not one who does not trust his own people; not happy alone; not of distressing disposition, conduct and behaviour; not trusting everyone, nor suspecting everyone, nor deliberating all the time.
01.08.27 | One should not let the time for action pass; not embark on what has not been examined; not be under the control of the senses; not let the fickle mind wander; not put an excessive burden on the intellect and the senses; not be too dilatory; not give way to anger and joy; not dwell in grief; not show pride in success nor dejection in failure; one should constantly remember one's nature (prakṛti); be convinced of the power of causes and always engaged in undertaking causes; not rest content thinking "it is done"; not give up vigour; not dwell on censure.
01.08.28 | One should not, while impure, offer oblations into the fire with fine ghee (ājya), unbroken rice (akṣata), sesame, kuśa grass and mustard, invoking blessings on oneself: "May Agni not depart from my body; may Vāyu establish my vital breaths (prāṇa); may Viṣṇu establish my strength; may Indra my vigour; may the auspicious waters enter me." With "āpo hi ṣṭhā" one should touch water; having wiped the lips twice and sprinkled the feet, one should touch with water the orifices (kha) in the head, oneself (ātman), the heart and the head.
01.08.29 | And one should be devoted to celibacy (brahmacarya), knowledge, giving, friendliness, compassion, joy, equanimity (upekṣā) and tranquillity.
