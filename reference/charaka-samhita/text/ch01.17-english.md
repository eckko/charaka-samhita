01.17.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter named 'How many in the head' (Kiyantaḥśirasīya).
01.17.2 | Thus indeed spoke the venerable Ātreya.
01.17.3a | How many diseases are declared in the head and in the heart of embodied beings? And how many diseases of Vāta (anila) and the others are born of variation in measure?
01.17.3b | ~
01.17.4a | How many depletions (kṣaya) are enumerated, and how many boils (piḍakā), O sinless one? And of how many kinds is the course of the doṣas stated to be, O destroyer of doṣas?

01.17.5b | ~

01.17.6b | ~

01.17.23b | ~

01.17.31b | ~

01.17.37b | ~
01.17.38a | From the moistness (saṃkleda), worms arise in him whose self is afflicted; born in one part of the vital point, they creep about and devour.

01.17.41b | ~
01.17.42a | In combination (saṃsarga, of two doṣas) nine: of them six by the increase of one, three with equal (doṣas); and three separately, by those (doṣas) increased: (thus) twenty-five diseases.
01.17.42b | ~
01.17.43a | Just as with increased doṣas, so with decreased doṣas there would be twenty-five; and another gradation (vikalpa), made by increase and decrease, will be taught.
01.17.43b | ~
01.17.44a | Increase of one, balance of one and decrease of one; increase of a pair and decrease of one; increase of one and decrease of two.
01.17.44b | ~
01.17.45a | When, in the decrease of Kapha (śleṣman), Vāta (māruta) takes Pitta that is in its normal state from its seat and spreads wherever in the limbs,
01.17.45b | ~
01.17.46a | then in that part of his body, here and there, there occur shifting splitting pain and burning, fatigue, and weakness as well.
01.17.46b | ~
01.17.47a | When, Pitta being decreased, the strong Vāta (vāyu) drags Kapha that is in its normal state, it then produces pain (śūla) with coldness, stiffness and heaviness.
01.17.47b | ~
01.17.48a | When, in the decrease of Kapha, Pitta obstructs Vāta (anila) that is in its normal state, then burning and pain arise in him.
01.17.48b | ~
01.17.49a | For when, in the decrease of Vāta, Pitta obstructs Kapha (śleṣman) that is balanced, it then produces fever with drowsiness and heaviness.
01.17.49b | ~
01.17.50a | For when, Pitta being decreased, increased Kapha (śleṣman) obstructs Vāta (samīraṇa), it then produces chill, heaviness and pain.
01.17.50b | ~

01.17.51b | ~

01.17.52b | ~

01.17.53b | ~

01.17.54b | ~

01.17.62b | ~

01.17.101 | Now, for knowing the distinction between the curability and incurability of these vidradhīs, we shall teach the distinction of signs caused by location. There, in a vidradhī arising in the chief vital point (marma): jolting of the heart, tamaka, stupor (pramoha), cough and dyspnoea; in one arising in the kloman: thirst, dryness of the mouth and catching of the throat; in one arising in the liver: dyspnoea; in one arising in the spleen: obstruction of breathing; in one arising in the kukṣi: pain in the kukṣi, the sides and between the shoulders; in one arising in the kidney: stiffness of the back and waist; in one arising in the navel: hiccup; in one arising in the groin: weakness of the thighs; and in one arising in the bladder: difficult and foul-smelling urine and faeces.
01.17.102 | When they have ripened and burst, in those arising above the discharge flows from the mouth, in those arising below from the anus, but in those arising in the navel from both.
01.17.103 | Of these, those arising in the heart, navel and bladder when fully ripened, and the sānnipātikī one, are for death; the rest, however, subside on reaching a skilful physician who treats quickly.
