01.20.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on the great diseases (Mahāroga).
01.20.2 | Thus said the venerable Ātreya.
01.20.3 | There are four diseases: those caused by the exogenous (āgantu), by Vāta, by Pitta and by Kapha (śleṣman). Of all these four diseases the state of being a disease is of one kind, because pain is common to them. Again, their origin (prakṛti) is twofold, by the division into exogenous (āgantu) and endogenous (nija); and their seat (adhiṣṭhāna) is twofold, by the distinction of mind and body. But the disorders are innumerable, because the distinctions of origin, seat, sign (liṅga), source (āyatana) and gradation (vikalpa) are innumerable.
01.20.4 | The gateways (mukha) of the exogenous disease are nails, teeth, falling, sorcery (abhicāra), curse (abhiśāpa), possession (abhiṣaṅga), a blow (abhighāta), piercing, binding, wrapping, pressing, rope, burning, weapon, thunderbolt, affliction by spirits (bhūta) and the like; but the gateway of the endogenous disease is the imbalance of Vāta, Pitta and Kapha.
01.20.5 | For both of them, the exogenous and the endogenous, the impelling cause (preraṇa) is unsuitable contact of the senses with their objects, offence of intellect (prajñāparādha) and transformation (pariṇāma).
01.20.6 | But all these four diseases, when greatly increased, become bound up with one another, and yet they do not fall into confusion with one another.
01.20.7 | For the exogenous disease, arising first with pain, afterwards brings about an imbalance of Vāta, Pitta and Kapha; but in the endogenous disease Vāta, Pitta and Kapha first fall into imbalance and afterwards produce pain.
01.20.8 | Of these three doṣas, the division of their sites in the body will now be taught. Namely: the bladder (basti), the receptacle of faeces, the waist, the two thighs, the two feet, the bones and the pakvāśaya are the sites of Vāta; among these too the pakvāśaya is especially the site of Vāta. Sweat, rasa, lasīkā, blood and the āmāśaya are the sites of Pitta; among these too the āmāśaya is especially the site of Pitta. The chest, the head, the neck, the joints, the āmāśaya and fat (medas) are the sites of Kapha; among these too the chest is especially the site of Kapha.
01.20.9 | But Vāta, Pitta and Kapha, which move through the whole body, do good and bad things in the whole body, as they are aggravated or unaggravated: when in their normal state, the good things such as growth, strength, complexion and clarity; but when they have undergone morbid change, the bad things that are called disorders.
01.20.10 | Among these, the disorders are common (sāmānyaja, 'arising in common') and specific (nānātmaja, 'arising from separate natures').
01.20.P1 | Among these, the common ones (sāmānyaja) have been expounded earlier in the Aṣṭodarīya; but the specific ones (nānātmaja) we shall expound in this chapter.
01.20.P2 | Namely: eighty Vāta disorders, forty Pitta disorders and twenty Kapha (śleṣman) disorders.
01.20.11 | Among these, we shall first expound the Vāta disorders.
01.20.P3 | Namely: splitting of the nails, vipādikā, pain in the feet, slipping of the feet, numbness of the feet, vātakhuḍḍatā, stiffness of the ankles, cramping of the calves, gṛdhrasī, splitting pain in the knees, loosening of the knees, stiffness of the thighs, weakness of the thighs, lameness, falling of the anus, pain in the anus, jerking of the testicles, stiffness of the penis, distension (ānāha) in the groins, splitting pain in the hips, breaking up of the stool, udāvarta, limping, being hunchbacked, being dwarfed, stiffness of the trika, stiffness of the back, crushing pain in the sides, griping of the belly, stupor of the heart, racing of the heart, grating pain in the chest, constriction of the chest, pricking pain in the chest, wasting of the arms, stiffness of the neck, manyāstambha, impairment of the throat, splitting pain in the jaw, splitting of the lips, splitting pain in the eyes, splitting pain in the teeth, looseness of the teeth, dumbness, obstruction of speech, an astringent taste in the mouth, dryness of the mouth, inability to perceive taste, loss of smell, pain in the ear, hearing sounds that are not there, hearing only what is loud, deafness, stiffness of the eyelids, contraction of the eyelids, timira, pain in the eyes, distortion of the eyes, distortion of the eyebrows, splitting pain in the temples, splitting pain in the forehead, headache, cracking of the scalp, ardita, disease of one limb, disease of all the limbs, pakṣavadha (striking down of one side), ākṣepaka (convulsion), daṇḍaka (stick-like rigidity), darkness (tamas), giddiness, trembling, yawning, hiccup, dejection, excessive prattle, dryness, harshness, a dusky and reddish appearance, sleeplessness and unsteadiness of mind. Thus the eighty Vāta disorders, the most evident among the innumerable Vāta disorders, have been expounded.
01.20.12 | In all these Vāta disorders that have been stated, and in others not stated, this is the unchanging own form (ātmarūpa) of Vāta (vāyu), and this is the own characteristic of its action, perceiving which, or a part of it, the skilled, freed from doubt, determine it to be a Vāta disorder alone. Namely: dryness, coldness, lightness, non-sliminess (vaiśadya), motion, formlessness and instability are the own forms of Vāta. And because Vāta is of this kind, this is the own characteristic of its action as it enters one part of the body or another. Namely: loosening (sraṃsa), slipping (bhraṃsa), spreading, obstruction, breaking, weakness, tingling, thirst, tremor, twisting, shaking, pricking, pain, movement and the like; and likewise roughness, harshness, non-sliminess, porousness, reddish colour, an astringent and tasteless mouth, drying, pain, numbness, contraction, stiffening, limping and the like are the actions of Vāta. A disorder accompanied by these one should determine to be a Vāta disorder alone.
01.20.13 | One should treat it with measures that are sweet, sour, salty, unctuous and hot, with Vāta-removing measures such as oleation, sweating, decoction enema (āsthāpana), unctuous enema (anuvāsana), nasal therapy (nastaḥkarma), food, oil massage, rubbing with unguents (utsādana) and affusion (pariṣeka), having taken measure and time as the standard. Among these, physicians consider decoction enema and unctuous enema the most important of the measures for Vāta in every case; for it enters the pakvāśaya from the very start and cuts the entire morbid root of Vāta. When Vāta there is overcome, the Vāta disorders lying within the body also become calm, just as, when the root of a tree is cut, the destruction of the trunk, branches, shoots, flowers, fruits, leaves and the like is certain: so it is here.
01.20.14 | From here onwards we shall expound the forty Pitta disorders: heat (oṣa), scorching (ploṣa), burning (dāha), feverish heat (davathu), a sensation of fumes (dhūmaka), sourness (amlaka), vidāha, internal burning, burning in the shoulders, excess of heat, excessive sweating, body odour, splitting of the body, moistness of the blood, moistness of the flesh, burning of the skin, splitting of the skin, bursting of the skin, red rashes (koṭha), red blisters, raktapitta, red circular patches, greenness, turmeric-yellowness, nīlikā, kakṣā, kāmalā, a bitter taste in the mouth, a smell of blood in the mouth, foul smell of the mouth, excess of thirst, lack of satisfaction, inflammation (pāka) of the mouth, of the throat, of the eyes, of the anus and of the penis, jīvādāna, entering into darkness, and a green and yellow colour of the eyes, urine and faeces. Thus the forty Pitta disorders, the most evident among the innumerable Pitta disorders, have been expounded.
01.20.15 | In all these Pitta disorders that have been stated, and in others not stated, this is the unchanging own form (ātmarūpa) of Pitta, and this is the own characteristic of its action, perceiving which, or a part of it, the skilled, freed from doubt, determine it to be a Pitta disorder alone. Namely: heat, sharpness, liquidity, not excessive unctuousness, a colour other than white and reddish, a raw-flesh smell (visra), the two tastes pungent and sour, and a flowing quality (saratva) are the own forms of Pitta. And because Pitta is of this kind, this is the own characteristic of its action as it enters one part of the body or another. Namely: burning, heat, inflammation (pāka), sweating, moistness, putrefaction (kotha), itching, discharge, redness, and the producing of smell, colour and taste each according to its own, are the actions of Pitta. A disorder accompanied by these one should determine to be a Pitta disorder alone.
01.20.16 | One should treat it with measures that are sweet, bitter, astringent and cold, with Pitta-removing measures such as oleation, purgation (vireka), plasters (pradeha), affusion (pariṣeka) and oil massage, having taken measure and time as the standard. But physicians consider purgation (virecana) the most important of all measures for Pitta; for it enters the āmāśaya from the very start and draws out the entire morbid root of Pitta. When Pitta there is overcome, the Pitta disorders lying within the body also become calm, just as, when the fire is removed, the whole fire-house becomes cool: so it is here.
01.20.17 | And from here onwards we shall expound the twenty Kapha (śleṣman) disorders. Namely: a feeling of fullness (tṛpti), drowsiness, excess of sleep, staimitya (sluggish heaviness), heaviness of the body, sloth, sweetness in the mouth, flow from the mouth, bringing up phlegm, excess of waste (mala), balāsaka, lack of digestion, coating of the heart, coating of the throat, build-up in the vessels (dhamanī), galagaṇḍa, excessive corpulence, coldness of the digestive fire (agni), udarda, a white appearance, and whiteness of the urine, eyes and faeces. Thus the twenty Kapha disorders, the most evident among the innumerable Kapha disorders, have been expounded.
01.20.18 | In all these Kapha disorders that have been stated, and in others not stated, this is the unchanging own form (ātmarūpa) of Kapha (śleṣman), and this is the own characteristic of its action, perceiving which, or a part of it, the skilled, freed from doubt, determine it to be a Kapha disorder alone. Namely: unctuousness, coldness, whiteness, heaviness, sweetness, firmness, sliminess and smoothness (mārtsnya) are the own forms of Kapha. And because Kapha is of this kind, this is the own characteristic of its action as it enters one part of the body or another. Namely: whiteness, coldness, itching, firmness, heaviness, unctuousness, numbness, moistness, coating (upadeha), binding, sweetness and slowness in acting are the actions of Kapha. A disorder accompanied by these one should determine to be a Kapha disorder alone.
01.20.19 | One should treat it with measures that are pungent, bitter, astringent, sharp, hot and dry, with Kapha-removing measures such as sweating, emesis (vamana), evacuation of the head (śirovirecana) and exercise, having taken measure and time as the standard. But physicians consider emesis the most important of all measures for Kapha; for it enters the āmāśaya from the very start and throws upwards the entire morbid root of Kapha lodged in the chest. When Kapha there is overcome, the Kapha disorders lying within the body also become calm, just as, when the dyke of a field is broken, śāli rice, barley (yava), ṣaṣṭika rice and the like, no longer moistened by the water, dry up: so it is here.
