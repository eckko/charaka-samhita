01.07.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter called "Navegāndhāraṇīya" (On Not Suppressing the Urges).
01.07.2 | Thus spoke the venerable Ātreya.
01.07.3a | The wise one should not suppress the urges of urine and faeces when they have arisen, nor of semen, nor of flatus (vāta), nor of vomiting, nor of sneezing;
01.07.3b | ~
01.07.4a | nor of belching, nor of yawning, nor the urges of hunger and thirst, nor of tears, nor of sleep, nor of breathing due to exertion.
01.07.4b | ~
01.07.5a | Hear from me, as I tell them one by one for the sake of treatment, the diseases that arise in one who suppresses these urges when they have arisen.
01.07.5b | ~
01.07.6a | Pain in the bladder and penis, difficult urination (mūtrakṛcchra), headache, bending of the body (vināma) and distension of the groins would be the signs when urine is suppressed.
01.07.6b | ~
01.07.7a | When urine is obstructed one should apply sweating, immersion baths, oil massage, the avapīḍaka of ghee and the threefold enema therapy (basti).
01.07.7b | ~
01.07.8a | Pain in the colon (pakvāśaya) and head, non-passage of flatus and faeces, cramping of the calves and distension (ādhmāna) would occur when faeces is suppressed.
01.07.8b | ~
01.07.9a | When faeces is obstructed, sweating, oil massage, immersion baths, suppositories (varti), enema therapy and food and drink that is pramāthin are beneficial.
01.07.9b | ~
01.07.10a | When semen is obstructed there would be pain in the penis and testicles, body-ache, distress in the heart, and obstructed urine as well.
01.07.10b | ~
01.07.11a | There, oil massage, immersion bath, wine (madirā), cocks (caraṇāyudha), śāli rice, milk, decoction enema (nirūha) and sexual intercourse are recommended.
01.07.11b | ~
01.07.12a | Retention of faeces, urine and flatus, distension, pain, exhaustion and other diseases born of Vāta in the abdomen would arise from suppression of flatus (vāta).
01.07.12b | ~
01.07.13a | There, the procedure of oleation and sweating, suppositories (varti), foods, drinks, enemas and whatever moves Vāta downward (vātānulomana) are recommended.
01.07.13b | ~
01.07.14a | Itching (kaṇḍū), rashes (koṭha), loss of taste (aruci), facial blemishes (vyaṅga), swelling (śotha), pallor disease (pāṇḍu), fever (jvara), kuṣṭha, nausea (hṛllāsa) and spreading inflammation (vīsarpa) are diseases born of suppressing vomiting.
01.07.14b | ~
01.07.15a | Here, emesis after eating, medicated smoking (dhūma), lightening (laṅghana), bloodletting, dry food and drink, exercise and purgation are recommended.
01.07.15b | ~
01.07.16a | Stiffness of the neck (manyāstambha), headache, ardita and ardhāvabhedaka, and weakness of the senses would arise from suppression of sneezing.
01.07.16b | ~
01.07.17a | There, oil massage of the region above the clavicles, sweating, medicated smoking together with nasal therapy (nāvana), Vāta-destroying food and ghee taken after meals are beneficial.
01.07.17b | ~
01.07.18a | Hiccup, dyspnoea (śvāsa), loss of taste, tremor and constriction of the heart and chest arise from suppression of belching; there the medicine is the same as for hiccup.
01.07.18b | ~
01.07.19a | Bending (vināma), convulsions, contractions, numbness, tremor and shaking arise from suppression of yawning; there, all Vāta-destroying medicine.
01.07.19b | ~
01.07.20a | Emaciation, weakness, discoloration, body-ache, loss of taste and giddiness arise from suppression of the urge of hunger; there, unctuous, hot and light food.
01.07.20b | ~
01.07.21a | Dryness of the throat and mouth, deafness, fatigue, lassitude (sāda) and distress in the heart arise from suppression of thirst; there a cold refreshing nourishment (tarpaṇa) is prescribed.
01.07.21b | ~
01.07.22a | Coryza (pratiśyāya), eye disease, heart disease, loss of taste and giddiness arise from suppression of tears; there, sleep, wine and pleasant talk.
01.07.22b | ~
01.07.23a | Yawning, body-ache, drowsiness (tandrā), disease of the head and heaviness of the eyes arise from suppression of sleep; there, sleep and massages (saṃvāhana).
01.07.23b | ~
01.07.24a | Gulma, heart disease and fainting (saṃmoha) arise from suppression of breathing due to exertion; there, rest and Vāta-destroying measures are beneficial.
01.07.24b | ~
01.07.25a | Wishing that these diseases born of the suppression of urges, which have been described, should not arise, one should not suppress these urges.
01.07.25b | ~
01.07.26a | But one who seeks his good after death and here should restrain these urges: those of rash and censured acts of mind, speech and body.
01.07.26b | ~
01.07.27a | The intelligent one should restrain the urges of greed, grief, fear, anger and pride, and of shamelessness, envy, excessive passion and covetousness (abhidhyā).
01.07.27b | ~
01.07.28a | One should restrain the urge, once it has arisen, toward speech that is harsh, excessive, slanderous, untrue or untimely.
01.07.28b | ~
01.07.29a | Whatever bodily activity there is that involves harm to others, such as enjoyment of women, theft, violence and the like, one should restrain its urges.
01.07.29b | ~
01.07.30a | Because his acts of mind, speech and body are free of sin, a man of good repute, happy, enjoys and accumulates dharma, artha (wealth) and kāma (pleasure).
01.07.30b | ~
01.07.31a | Bodily activity which is aimed at steadiness and increases strength is known as bodily exercise (vyāyāma); one should practise it in due measure.
01.07.31b | ~
01.07.32a | Lightness, capacity for work, steadiness, tolerance of hardship, reduction of the doṣas and increase of the digestive fire (agni) arise from exercise.
01.07.32b | ~
01.07.33a | Fatigue, exhaustion, wasting, thirst, raktapitta, pratāmaka, cough, fever and vomiting arise from excessive exercise.
01.07.33b | ~
01.07.33.1a | Onset of sweating, increase of breathing, lightness of the limbs, and obstruction of the heart and so on: this is the sign of exercise.
01.07.33.1b | ~
01.07.34a | The intelligent one should not indulge excessively in exercise, laughter, talking, travelling on foot (adhvan), sexual intercourse (grāmyadharma) and staying awake at night, even when accustomed to them.
01.07.34b | ~
01.07.35a | He who practises these and others of this kind beyond measure suddenly perishes, like a lion dragging an elephant.
01.07.35b | ~
01.07.35.1a | Those who are greatly emaciated by excess of sexual intercourse, load-bearing, travel and work, and those men too who are overcome by anger, grief, fear and exertion,
01.07.35.1b | ~
01.07.35.2a | children, the old, the windy (pravāta), those who talk much and loudly, and those who are hungry and thirsty: they should avoid exercise.
01.07.35.2b | ~
01.07.36a | A wise man should gradually desist from the unwholesome to which he is habituated, and should take up the wholesome gradually; and the sequence is taught here.
01.07.36b | ~
01.07.37a | In the adding and reducing of those two (the unwholesome and the wholesome), the sequence should be by quarter-parts; and then, after that, with an interval of one, then of two, and likewise of three.
01.07.37b | ~
01.07.38a | Doṣas (faults) reduced gradually and good qualities increased gradually, being so, go to non-recurrence and become unshakable.
01.07.38b | ~
01.07.39a | Some men, from conception onward, have Pitta, Vāta (anila) and Kapha in balance; some are seen to be Vāta-predominant (vātala), some Pitta-predominant (pittala), and likewise Kapha-predominant (śleṣmala).
01.07.39b | ~
01.07.40a | Of them, the former are not sick; the Vāta-predominant and the rest are always sick; for their bodily constitution is called 'following of the doṣas' (doṣānuśayitā).
01.07.40b | ~
01.07.41a | For them, a rule of the regimen of the healthy (svasthavṛtta) of opposite qualities is wholesome; for one of balanced dhātus, suitability (sātmya) with all tastes in equal measure is praised.
01.07.41b | ~
01.07.42a | The orifices, two below and seven in the head, and the openings of sweat: these channels of waste (malāyana) are afflicted by wastes that are vitiated and exceed their measure.
01.07.42b | ~
01.07.43a | One should know increase of waste from the heaviness of the channels of waste, depletion of waste from their lightness, and also from excessive obstruction or discharge.
01.07.43b | ~
01.07.44a | Having determined those diseases by the signs of the doṣas, one should treat the curable ones with what is opposed to the disease and its cause, considering measure and time.
01.07.44b | ~
01.07.45a | For those who are not sick but whose regimen of the healthy (svasthavṛtta) is irregular, these and other diseases arise; therefore one should be devoted to the regimen of the healthy.
01.07.45b | ~
01.07.46a | In the month that is first of Mādhava, again in the first of Nabhasya, and in the first of Sahasya, one should have the accumulation of doṣas removed.
01.07.46b | ~
01.07.47a | For those whose bodies have been oleated and sweated, (removal) upward and downward, regularly; then the wise one should perform enema therapy (bastikarma) and nasal therapy (nasyakarma).
01.07.47b | ~
01.07.48a | After this, the knower of time should employ, in due order and as suitable, proven rejuvenatives (rasāyana) and aphrodisiac applications (yoga).
01.07.48b | ~
01.07.49a | Thus, the dhātus remaining in their natural state, diseases do not arise; the dhātus grow, and old age reaches slowness.
01.07.49b | ~
01.07.50a | This method has been shown for the non-arising of endogenous (nija) disorders; that of the others will be taught separately.
01.07.50b | ~
01.07.51a | In the exogenous (āgantu) diseases of men that arise from bhūtas, poison, wind, fire, assault and the like, the intellect (prajñā) errs.
01.07.51b | ~
01.07.52a | And the mental disorders, envy, grief, fear, anger, pride, hatred and the like, are also all said to be born of the offence of intellect (prajñāparādha).
01.07.52b | ~
01.07.53a | Giving up of offences of intellect, calming of the senses, memory, knowledge of place, time and self, and following of good conduct (sadvṛtta):
01.07.53b | ~
01.07.54a | this path has been shown for the non-arising of the exogenous (diseases). The wise one should do beforehand that which he would know to be wholesome for himself.
01.07.54b | ~
01.07.55a | Understanding of the teaching of the reliable (āpta) and putting it into practice (pratipatti) are the cause for the non-arising of disorders and for the pacification of those that have arisen.
01.07.55b | ~
01.07.56a | Those of sinful conduct, speech and mind (sattva), informers, lovers of quarrels, mockers at (others') weak points (marma), the greedy, haters of others' prosperity, the deceitful,
01.07.56b | ~
01.07.57a | those delighting in slandering others, the fickle, those who serve enemies, the pitiless, those who have abandoned dharma: these lowest of men are to be avoided.
01.07.57b | ~
01.07.58a | Those advanced in intellect, learning, age, character, steadiness, memory and concentration, who attend on elders, who know the nature (of things), who are free from distress,
01.07.58b | ~
01.07.59a | kindly-faced to all beings, tranquil, of praised vows, teachers of the good path, whose hearing and sight are meritorious: these are to be resorted to.
01.07.59b | ~
01.07.60a | In diet, conduct and activity, the wise one who desires happiness hereafter and here should make the highest effort in the use of the wholesome.
01.07.60b | ~
01.07.61a | One should not eat curd (dadhi) at night, nor without ghee and sugar, nor without mudga yūṣa, nor without honey, nor hot, nor without āmalaka fruits.
01.07.61b | ~
01.07.62a | One fond of curd who abandons the rule may get fever, asṛkpitta, vīsarpa, kuṣṭha, the disease pāṇḍu, giddiness, and severe kāmalā.
01.07.62b | ~
01.07.63a | Here, the verses: the urges, the diseases arising from urges and their medicine; whose urges are to be suppressed, and for what purpose; what is wholesome and unwholesome;
01.07.63b | ~
01.07.63c | ~
01.07.64a | the sequence regarding the habitual unwholesome that is to be avoided and the unaccustomed that is to be used; diet according to constitution (prakṛti); the medicine for diseases of the channels of waste;
01.07.64b | ~
01.07.65a | the medicine which is for the non-arising of future diseases; the persons to be avoided and to be resorted to by the wise one who desires his own happiness;
01.07.65b | ~
01.07.66a | and by which rule and why curd is to be eaten: all this the Atri-born sage told in the chapter 'Na vegān dhāraṇa' (not holding back the urges).
01.07.66b | ~
01.07.P1 | Thus, in the treatise composed by Agniveśa and redacted by Caraka, in the Śloka section (Ślokasthāna), the seventh chapter, named Navegāndhāraṇīya (On not holding back the urges).
