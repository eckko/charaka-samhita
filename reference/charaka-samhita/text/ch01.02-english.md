01.02.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter called "Apāmārgataṇḍulīya".
01.02.2 | Thus spoke the venerable Ātreya.
01.02.3a | The seeds of apāmārga, pippalī and marica, then viḍaṅga, śigru, sarṣapa and tumburu,
01.02.3b | ~
01.02.4a | ajājī and ajagandhā, pīlu, elā, hareṇukā, pṛthvīkā, surasā, śvetā, kuṭheraka and phaṇijjhaka,
01.02.4b | ~
01.02.5a | the seed of śirīṣa, laśuna, the two haridrās, the two salts, jyotiṣmatī and nāgara one should give in head-evacuation (śīrṣavirecana).
01.02.5b | ~
01.02.6a | In heaviness of the head, in pain, in pīnasa, in ardhāvabhedaka (one-sided splitting headache), in worm disease, in apasmāra, in loss of smell and in pramohaka.
01.02.6b | ~
01.02.7a | Madana, madhuka, nimba, jīmūta, kṛtavedhana, pippalī, kuṭaja, ikṣvāku, elā and dhāmārgava,
01.02.7b | ~
01.02.8a | When Kapha (śleṣman) and Pitta have come up, in a disease seated in the āmāśaya, the physician, not harming the body, should employ for emesis (vamana).
01.02.8b | ~
01.02.9a | Trivṛt, triphalā, dantī, nīlinī, saptalā, vacā, kampillaka, gavākṣī, kṣīriṇī and udakīryakā,
01.02.9b | ~
01.02.10a | pīlu, āragvadha, drākṣā, dravantī and nicula one should use for purgation (vireka) when the doṣa has gone to the pakvāśaya.
01.02.10b | ~
01.02.11a | Pāṭalā and agnimantha, bilva and also śyonāka, kāśmarya, śālaparṇī, pṛśniparṇī and nidigdhikā,
01.02.11b | ~
01.02.12a | balā, śvadaṃṣṭrā, bṛhatī, eraṇḍa together with punarnavā, yava, kulattha, kola, guḍūcī and madana,
01.02.12b | ~
01.02.13a | palāśa, kattṛṇa, and also unctuous substances (sneha) and salts, one should use in udāvarta, in obstructions (vibandha) and in decoction enemas (āsthāpana).
01.02.13b | ~
01.02.14a | From this very group of medicines an unctuous enema (anuvāsana) that destroys Vāta (māruta) is to be devised. Thus the collection relating to the five procedures (pañcakarma) has been declared.
01.02.14b | ~
01.02.15a | Those five procedures (pañcakarma) one should perform for persons whose doṣas have come up, with preparation by oleation and sweating (sneha, sveda), considering dose and time.
01.02.15b | ~
01.02.16a | Rational planning (yukti) rests on dose and time; success is established in yukti. The knower of yukti always stands above those who have knowledge of substances (dravya).
01.02.16b | ~
01.02.17a | After this I shall declare gruels (yavāgū) with various medicines, for the cessation of various disorders curable by them.
01.02.17b | ~
01.02.18a | Gruel prepared with pippalī, pippalīmūla, cavya, citraka and nāgara stimulates digestion (dīpanīya) and destroys colic pain (śūla).
01.02.18b | ~
01.02.19a | Prepared with dadhittha, bilva, cāṅgerī, buttermilk (takra) and dāḍima, it is digesting (pācana) and binding (grāhin); when Vāta is involved, a peyā made with the five roots (pāñcamūlikī).
01.02.19b | ~
01.02.20a | Peyā prepared with śālaparṇī, balā and bilva and with pṛśniparṇī, soured with dāḍima, is beneficial for those with diarrhoea (atisāra) of Pitta and Kapha (śleṣman).
01.02.20b | ~
01.02.21a | Peyā prepared in goat's milk with half water, with hrīvera, utpala and nāgara and with pṛśniparṇī, destroys bloody diarrhoea (raktātisāra).
01.02.21b | ~
01.02.22a | When there is āma (undigested matter), one should give peyā with ativiṣā, soured and with nāgara; in painful urination (mūtrakṛcchra), with śvadaṃṣṭrā and kaṇṭakārī, with phāṇita.
01.02.22b | ~
01.02.23a | Gruel cooked in buttermilk (takra) with viḍaṅga, pippalīmūla and śigru and with marica, with suvarcikā, destroys worms (krimi).
01.02.23b | ~
01.02.24a | With mṛdvīkā, sārivā, lāja (parched grain), pippalī, honey (madhu) and nāgara it destroys thirst; and cooked with somarājī it destroys poison.
01.02.24b | ~
01.02.25a | Gruel cooked in boar soup (varāha-niryūha) is considered nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa); that of roasted gavedhukā, with honey (mākṣika), is reducing (karśana).
01.02.25b | ~
01.02.26a | Containing ghee, with much sesame and with salt, it is oleating (snehana); that of śyāmāka in the decoction of kuśa and āmalaka is drying (virūkṣaṇa).
01.02.26b | ~
01.02.27a | Boiled with the ten roots (daśamūlī), it removes cough (kāsa), hiccup, dyspnoea (śvāsa) and Kapha; cooked with madirā in yamaka, it removes pain of the pakvāśaya.
01.02.27b | ~
01.02.28a | Cooked with vegetables (śāka), meats, sesame (tila) and māṣa, it expels faeces (varcas); with the stones of jambū and āmra, sour dadhittha and bilva, it is considered binding (sāṃgrāhika).
01.02.28b | ~
01.02.29a | With alkali (kṣāra), citraka, hiṅgu and amlavetasa it is considered breaking (bhedin); with abhayā, pippalīmūla and viśva, it moves Vāta downward (vātānulomana).
01.02.29b | ~
01.02.30a | Gruel cooked in buttermilk (takra) destroys the ill effects of ghee (ghṛta-vyāpatti); in the ill effects of oil, that prepared with buttermilk and oil-cake (piṇyāka) is recommended.
01.02.30b | ~
01.02.31a | With meat-juice (māṃsarasa) of cattle, soured, it destroys irregular fever (viṣamajvara); that of yava in yamaka, boiled with pippalī and āmalaka, is good for the throat.
01.02.31b | ~
01.02.32a | Cooked in the meat-juice of the cock (tāmracūḍa), it removes pain of the passage of semen; with split māṣa, prepared with ghee and milk, it is aphrodisiac (vṛṣya).
01.02.32b | ~
01.02.33a | Cooked with upodikā and curd (dadhi), it destroys intoxication (mada); boiled with apāmārga, milk and the meat-juice of godhā, it destroys hunger.
01.02.33b | ~
01.02.34a | Here is a verse: Thus these twenty-eight gruels have been proclaimed, and a collection of medicines has been declared on the basis of the five procedures (pañcakarma).
01.02.34b | ~
01.02.34c | ~
01.02.35a | The medicine that was stated earlier for the sake of knowing roots and fruits has been proclaimed again for the sake of knowledge based on the five procedures (pañcakarma).
01.02.35b | ~
01.02.36a | The physician who has memory, knows causes and rational planning (hetu, yukti), is self-controlled and has presence of mind, is fit to carry out treatment with combinations of medicines.
01.02.36b | ~
01.02.P1 | Thus, in the Śloka section (Ślokasthāna) of the treatise composed by Agniveśa and redacted by Caraka, the second chapter, named Apāmārgataṇḍulīya.
