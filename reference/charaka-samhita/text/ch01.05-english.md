01.05.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter Mātrāśitīya (On the Measure of Eating).
01.05.2 | Thus spoke the venerable Ātreya.
01.05.3 | One should be an eater in measure (mātrāśī).
01.05.P1 | And the measure of food depends on the strength of the digestive fire (agni).
01.05.4 | For as much food eaten by him as, without harming his natural state (prakṛti), goes to digestion in due time, so much is to be known as the measure of his quantity.
01.05.5 | In this matter, food substances such as śāli rice, ṣaṣṭika rice, mudga, lāva, kapiñjala, eṇa, hare (śaśa), śarabha and śambara, although light by nature, depend on measure.
01.05.P2 | Likewise food substances such as flour (preparations), sugar-cane, products of milk, tila, māṣa, and the meat of marsh (ānūpa) and water (audaka) animals, although heavy by nature, depend on measure itself.
01.05.6 | And when this has been said, one should not think that heaviness and lightness in a substance are of no account; for light substances abound in the qualities of air (vāyu) and fire (agni), and the others abound in the qualities of earth and soma. Therefore light substances, even by their own quality, are by nature kindlers of the fire, and are said to be of little harm even when taken to satiety; heavy substances, however, are not by nature kindlers of the fire, because of dissimilarity, and hence, taken to satiety, they are excessively harmful, except where there is strength from exercise and strength of the fire. This is the measure that depends on the strength of the fire.
01.05.7 | And it is not that (the measure) does not depend on the substance: with regard to the substance, a third part of satiety or half satiety is prescribed for heavy substances, and for light ones too not excessive satiety, for the sake of the right ordering (yukti) of the fire.
01.05.8 | For food eaten in measure, without harming the natural state (prakṛti), certainly endows the one who takes it with strength, complexion, happiness and life.
01.05.9a | And here there are (verses): therefore one who has eaten should never eat what is heavy and made of flour, nor rice grains (taṇḍula) and flattened rice (pṛthuka); when hungry, one should eat in measure.
