01.14.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on sweating (sveda).
01.14.2 | Thus said the venerable Ātreya.
01.14.3a | Now the sweating procedures will be expounded, by which, when properly applied, the diseases curable by sweating, which are of the nature of Vāta and Kapha, subside.
01.14.3b | ~
01.14.4a | When Vāta (anila) has been subdued by sweating applied after oleation, faeces, urine and semen do not get stuck in any way at all.
01.14.4b | ~
01.14.5a | For even dry pieces of wood are bent properly by the application of oleation and sweating; how much more living men!
01.14.5b | ~
01.14.6a | Sweating that has regard to the disease, the season and the patient, is neither too hot nor too mild, is provided with substances and is applied at the region, is held to be effective.
01.14.6b | ~
01.14.7a | When the disease is cold and the body is of great strength, strong sweating is beneficial; when weak, weak sweating; when moderate, moderate sweating.
01.14.7b | ~
01.14.8a | In Vāta-Kapha (śleṣman), in Vāta, or in Kapha, sweating is desired that is prepared both unctuous and dry, likewise unctuous, and dry.
01.14.8b | ~
01.14.9a | When Vāta is situated in the āmāśaya, and when Kapha rests in the pakvāśaya, sweating preceded by drying is beneficial, and likewise preceded by oleation.
01.14.9b | ~
01.14.10a | One should sweat the testicles, the heart and the eyes mildly or not at all; the groins moderately; the remaining parts of the body as desired.
01.14.10b | ~
01.14.11a | Having covered the eyes with very clean cloths, or with a lump of wheat, or with leaves of lotus (padma) and water-lily (utpala), one is to be sweated.
01.14.11b | ~
01.14.12a | One should touch the heart of the one being sweated with cool strings of pearls, with cool vessels too, with water-born things (jalaja) wet with water, and with hands.
01.14.12b | ~
01.14.13a | When cold and pain (śūla) have ceased, when stiffness and heaviness are checked, and when softness and sweat have arisen, cessation of sweating is approved.
01.14.13b | ~
01.14.14a | Aggravation of Pitta, fainting, weariness of the body, thirst, burning, and weakness of voice and limbs are the sign of one over-sweated.
01.14.14b | ~
01.14.15a | The summer regimen which is stated in full in the Tasyāśitīya, sweet, unctuous and cool, is to be applied for one over-sweated.
01.14.15b | ~
01.14.16a | Of those habitually taking kaṣāya and wine, of a pregnant woman, of those with raktapitta, of those afflicted by Pitta, of those with diarrhoea, of the dry, of those with madhumeha,
01.14.16b | ~
01.14.17a | of those whose bradhna is inflamed and displaced, of those with disorders from poison or wine, of the fatigued, of those who have lost consciousness, of the obese, of those with Pitta-type meha,
01.14.17b | ~
01.14.18a | of the thirsty, the hungry, the angry and the grieving too, of those with kāmalā and udara, of the injured (kṣata), of those with āḍhyaroga,
01.14.18b | ~
01.14.19a | of the weak and the very emaciated, likewise of those whose ojas (vital essence) is depleted, and of those with timira, the physician should not apply sweating.
01.14.19b | ~
01.14.20a | In coryza (pratiśyāya), in cough, in hiccup and dyspnoea, in heaviness, in pain of the ear, nape and head, in hoarseness, in seizure of the throat,
01.14.20b | ~
01.14.21a | in ardita, in (disorder of) one limb or of the whole body, in pakṣāghāta, in vināmaka (bending), in distension (ānāha) and constipation of the bowel, in obstruction of urine (mūtrāghāta), in vijṛmbhaka (yawning),
01.14.21b | ~
01.14.22a | in stiffness of the flanks, back, waist and belly, in gṛdhrasī, in mūtrakṛcchra, in enlargement of the testicles, in body-ache,
01.14.22b | ~
01.14.23a | in pain and stiffness of the feet, knees, thighs and calves, in swelling too, in khallī, in āma conditions, in cold, in tremor, in vātakaṇṭaka,
01.14.23b | ~
01.14.24a | in contraction, extension and pain, in stiffness, heaviness and numbness, and in disorders affecting the whole body, sweating is said to be beneficial.
01.14.24b | ~
01.14.25a | One should apply bolus sweating (piṇḍasveda) with sesame, māṣa, kulattha, sour (substances), ghee, oil, meat (āmiṣa) and boiled rice, and with pāyasa, kṛśarā and meats.
01.14.25b | ~
01.14.26a | With the dung of cow, donkey, camel, pig and horse, with barley with its husks, with packets of sand, dust, stone, dry dung (karīṣa) and iron,
01.14.26b | ~
01.14.27a | one should sweat persons of Kapha type with the former, and treat persons of Vāta type. These substances are also commended, each as appropriate, in spread-beds (prastara).
01.14.27b | ~
01.14.28a | In underground rooms, in jentākas, and in warm inner rooms heated by smokeless embers, one who is well anointed sweats comfortably.
01.14.28b | ~
01.14.29a | Meat of domestic, marsh and aquatic animals, milk, likewise goat's head, the middle part, bile and blood of the pig, and unctuous sesame and rice grains:
01.14.29b | ~
01.14.30a | Having boiled these together, the best physician, knowing the distinctions of place and time and attentive to rational planning (yukti), should apply tube sweating (nāḍīsveda).
01.14.30b | ~
01.14.31a | With vāruṇa, amṛtaka, eraṇḍa, śigru, mūlaka and sarṣapa, with the leaves of vāsā, vaṃśa, karañja and arka, and of aśmantaka,
01.14.31b | ~
01.14.32a | with the leaves of śobhāñjanaka, saireya, mālatī, surasā and arjaka, having boiled water, one should apply tube sweating.
01.14.32b | ~
01.14.33a | With bhūtīka and the pañcamūla (five roots), with surā, with whey of curd (dadhimastu), and with urines and sour (liquids) together with fat, one should apply tube sweating.
01.14.33b | ~
01.14.34a | And these same decoctions (niryūha) are to be used in a water-tub (jalakoṣṭhaka); for sweating one should also have tubs of ghee, milk and oil made.
01.14.34b | ~
01.14.35a | A poultice (upanāha) with pieces of wheat and with powders of barley, mixed with sour (substance) and with fat, kiṇva (ferment) and salt, is commended.
01.14.35b | ~
01.14.36a | And one should apply a poultice with fragrant substances, with the ferment (kiṇva) of surā, with jīvantī, with śatapuṣpā, and with umā combined with kuṣṭha and oil.
01.14.36b | ~
01.14.37a | And it is to be bound on with skins that have hair, are not putrid and are of hot potency (uṣṇavīrya); but in their absence, with silk and woollen cloths.
01.14.37b | ~
01.14.38a | What is tied at night one should release by day, and what is applied by day one should release at night, to avoid burning (vidāha); but in cold there may be a prolongation.
01.14.38b | ~
01.14.39a | The mixed bolus (saṅkara), the spread (prastara), the tube (nāḍī), affusion (pariṣeka), immersion (avagāhana), the jentāka, the stone mass (aśmaghana), the trench (karṣū), the hut (kuṭī), the ground (bhū) and the pitcher (kumbhikā),
01.14.39b | ~
01.14.40a | the pit (kūpa) and the holāka: these thirteen cause sweating. I shall explain them all properly in due order.
01.14.40b | ~
01.14.41 | Among these, sweating with the boluses (piṇḍa) described, wrapped in cloth or not wrapped in cloth, one should know as the saṅkara sweat.
01.14.42 | Sweating of one whose whole body is well anointed, lying on a spread bed (prastara) of awned grains (śūkadhānya), pulses (śamīdhānya) and shrivelled grain (pulāka), or of veśavāra, pāyasa, kṛśarā, utkārikā and the like, with an upper sheet of silk or wool, or with a sheet of pañcāṅgula, urubūka or arka leaves, one should know as the prastara sweat.
01.14.43 | Further, when sweating substances, namely roots, fruits, leaves, shoots (śuṅga) and the like, or the flesh, heads, feet and the like of animals and birds, of hot nature, or urine, milk and the like combined as suitable with sour things, salt and fat, have been boiled in a pitcher (kumbhī) that does not let out the vapour, one whose body is anointed with fat cooked with Vāta-removing drugs should take in the vapour through a tube (nāḍī) made of any one of reed (śara), iṣīkā, split bamboo, karañja leaf or arka leaf, shaped like the tip of an elephant's trunk, one vyāma long or half a vyāma long, whose opening has a circumference of a quarter of a vyāma at the base and an eighth at the tip, whose holes are closed all round with Vāta-removing leaves, and which is bent twice or thrice. For the vapour, travelling not straight and with its fierce force broken, causes sweating comfortably without burning the skin. This is the tube sweat (nāḍīsveda).
01.14.44 | Further, having filled sprinkling pitchers (varṣaṇikā kumbhī) or pipes (pranāḍī) with comfortably warm decoctions of roots and the like, for those of Vāta and of predominant Vāta (vātika, uttaravātika), one should pour them over one whose body is anointed with suitably prepared fat and who is covered with cloth. This is affusion (pariṣeka).
01.14.45 | Immersion in a tub (koṣṭhaka) of Vāta-removing decoction, milk, oil, ghee, meat-juice or hot water, just as described, is the immersion (avagāha).
01.14.46 | Now one who wishes to make a jentāka should examine the ground: there, in the east or the north, on a plot of land with good qualities and commended, with black, sweet soil or with golden soil, on the southern or western bank of any one of the water bodies such as reservoirs (parīvāpa) and lotus ponds, on a level, well-divided plot of land with a good landing place, moving seven or eight aratnis away from the water, he should have built a pavilion (kūṭāgāra) facing east or north, facing the landing place, at most sixteen aratnis in height and width, well rounded on all sides, finished with clay work and with many windows. Inside this pavilion, all round along the wall, he should have made a platform (piṇḍikā) one aratni in width and height, up to the door; and in the middle of this pavilion he should have made a pillar-shaped chamber for coals, measuring four kiṣkus, of a man's height, made of clay, shaped like a kandu, with many fine holes and with a lid; and having filled it with logs of khadira or aśvakarṇa and the like, he should kindle it. When he knows that the logs are well burnt and their smoke is gone, and that the fire-house is wholly heated by the fire and endowed with heat fit for sweating, then, making this man, whose body is anointed with Vāta-removing (fat) and who is covered with cloth, enter there, he should instruct him: "O gentle one, enter for welfare and for health. Having entered and mounted this platform, you should lie as is comfortable on one side and the other. And even when you are overcome by sweat and fainting, this platform is not to be left by you as long as there is breath; for falling from this platform space, not reaching the door, you might at once lose your life from being overcome by sweat and fainting. Therefore you should not leave this platform in any way at all. When you know yourself free from clogging (abhiṣyanda), with sweat and slime (picchā) well flowed out, freed in all channels (srotas), become light and free from obstruction, stiffness, numbness, pain and heaviness, then, following that platform, you should reach the door; and having come out, you should not touch cold water suddenly, for the protection of the eyes; but when heat and exhaustion have gone, after a muhūrta, having been duly bathed with comfortably warm water, you should eat." This is the jentāka sweat.

01.14.47b | ~

01.14.48b | ~

01.14.50b | ~
01.14.51a | Then he should fill that trench with glowing, smokeless coals; lying on a bed above it, a man sweats comfortably.
01.14.51b | ~
01.14.52a | Having made a hut (kuṭī) not too high or wide, round in shape, without windows and with thick walls, one should plaster it with kuṣṭha and the like.
01.14.52b | ~

01.14.53b | ~

01.14.57b | ~

01.14.63a | This is the holāka sweat, a pleasant one, declared by the great sage. Thus sweating based on the quality of fire is of thirteen kinds.
01.14.63b | ~

01.14.66b | ~
