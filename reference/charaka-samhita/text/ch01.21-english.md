01.21.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on the eight censured persons (Aṣṭauninditīya).
01.21.2 | Thus said the venerable Ātreya.
01.21.3 | Here, with regard to the body, eight persons are censured, namely: the excessively tall, the excessively short, the excessively hairy, the hairless, the excessively dark, the excessively fair, the excessively obese and the excessively lean.
01.21.4 | Among them, in the excessively obese and the excessively lean there are, further, other special censurable features.
01.21.P1 | Of the excessively obese, first, there are these eight defects (doṣa): shortening of life-span, impediment of speed, difficulty in sexual intercourse, weakness, bad odour, trouble from sweat, excessive hunger and excessive thirst.
01.21.P2 | That excessive obesity arises from over-filling, from the use of heavy, sweet, cold and unctuous things, from lack of exercise, from lack of sexual intercourse, from day-sleep, from constant cheerfulness, from absence of mental exertion, and from the nature of the seed.
01.21.P3 | For in him, the excessively fat person, fat alone accumulates, not the other dhātus likewise; therefore his life-span is shortened. From the looseness, delicacy and heaviness of fat there is impediment of speed; from scantiness of semen (śukra) and from his pathways being covered by fat there is difficulty in sexual intercourse; weakness, from the imbalance of the dhātus; bad odour, from the defect of fat, from the nature of fat and from its producing sweat; trouble from sweat, from the association of fat with Kapha (śleṣman), its oozing nature, its abundance, its heaviness and its inability to bear exercise; and excessive hunger and excessive thirst, from a sharp digestive fire (agni) and abundant Vāta (vāyu) in the bowel (koṣṭha).
01.21.5a | And here there are: Because its pathways are covered by fat, Vāta (vāyu), moving especially in the bowel (koṣṭha), kindles the digestive fire (agni) and also dries up the food.

01.21.5c | ~
01.21.6a | Therefore he digests food quickly and craves it excessively, and, when the time is transgressed, he incurs some terrible disorders.
01.21.6b | ~
01.21.7a | These two, fire (agni) and Vāta (māruta), especially cause complications; for these two burn the obese one as a forest fire burns the forest.
01.21.7b | ~
01.21.8a | When fat has increased greatly, Vāta (anila) and the others suddenly produce dreadful disorders and quickly destroy life.
01.21.8b | ~
01.21.9a | A man who, from excessive increase of fat and flesh, has pendulous buttocks, belly and breasts, and whose energy does not match his growth, is called excessively obese.
01.21.9b | ~
01.21.10a | Thus the defects (doṣa), causes and symptoms (rūpa) of the fat person have been stated; after this, what is to be said about extreme leanness will be stated.
01.21.10b | ~
01.21.11a | The use of dry food and drink, lightening (laṅghana), scanty eating, excessive use of procedures (kriyā), grief, suppression of urges (vega) and of sleep,
01.21.11b | ~
01.21.12a | rubbing (udvartana) with dry substance, habitual bathing, constitution (prakṛti), old age, lingering of disorders, and anger make a man very lean.
01.21.12b | ~
01.21.13a | The lean man does not tolerate exercise, over-satiety (sauhitya), hunger, thirst, disease and medicine; likewise excessive cold, heat and sexual intercourse.
01.21.13b | ~
01.21.14a | Plīhā (spleen disease), cough (kāsa), wasting (kṣaya), dyspnoea (śvāsa), gulma, arśas, udara diseases and the diseases located in the grahaṇī mostly assail the lean man.
01.21.14b | ~
01.21.15a | A man with dried-up buttocks, belly and neck, covered with a network of vessels (dhamanī), left with skin and bone, with prominent joints, is considered very lean.
01.21.15b | ~
01.21.16a | These two men, the excessively obese and the excessively lean, are always diseased, and they are always to be treated with reducing (karśana) and nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa) measures.
01.21.16b | ~
01.21.17a | Between obesity and leanness, leanness is better, for the two are equal in means (upakaraṇa); if disease should come upon both, it is the obese one that it would afflict severely.
01.21.17b | ~
01.21.18a | But a man with a balanced measure of flesh and balanced compactness of body (saṃhanana), with firm senses, is not overcome by the force of disorders.
01.21.18b | ~
01.21.19a | He is considered to endure hunger, thirst and sun, to endure cold and exercise, to digest evenly, to assimilate evenly and to have a balanced accumulation of flesh.
01.21.19b | ~
01.21.20a | For reducing (karśana) the obese, what is heavy and non-replenishing (atarpaṇa) is desired; and for nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa) the lean, what is light and replenishing (saṃtarpaṇa).
01.21.20b | ~
01.21.21a | Vāta-destroying foods and drinks that also remove Kapha (śleṣman) and fat; dry, hot and sharp enemas (basti); and dry rubbings (udvartana);
01.21.21b | ~
01.21.22a | the administration of guḍūcī and bhadramustā, likewise the administration of triphalā (traiphala), administrations of buttermilk (takra) and ariṣṭa, and the administration of mākṣika;
01.21.22b | ~
01.21.23a | viḍaṅga, dry ginger (nāgara), alkali (kṣāra), powder of black metal (kālaloha), honey (madhu), and powder of barley and āmalaka; administration combined with honey (kṣaudra);
01.21.23b | ~
01.21.24a | the administration of the five roots beginning with bilva (bilvādipañcamūla) combined with honey (kṣaudra), the administration of śilājatu, and the excellent juice of agnimantha;
01.21.24b | ~
01.21.25a | praśātikā, priyaṅgu, śyāmāka, yavaka, barley (yava), jūrṇāhva, kodrava, mudga, kulattha, cakramudgaka,
01.21.25b | ~
01.21.26a | and the seeds of āḍhakī, together with paṭola and āmalaka, are to be used as food; and, as a drink afterwards, honey-water.
01.21.26b | ~
01.21.27a | And for destroying excessive obesity one should apportion and administer, as after-drink (anupāna), ariṣṭas that remove fat, flesh and Kapha.
01.21.27b | ~
01.21.28a | One who wishes to give up obesity should gradually increase wakefulness, sexual intercourse, exercise and mental exertion.
01.21.28b | ~
01.21.29a | Sleep, joy, a comfortable bed, contentment of mind, calm, cessation of worry, sexual intercourse and exercise, the sight of what is dear,
01.21.29b | ~
01.21.30a | new grains, new wine (madya), the meat-juices (rasa) of domestic, marsh and aquatic animals, prepared meats, curd, ghee (sarpis) and milk,
01.21.30b | ~
01.21.31a | sugarcane, śāli rice, māṣa, wheat, preparations of jaggery (guḍa), unctuous and sweet enemas (basti), and oil massage (abhyaṅga) at all times,
01.21.31b | ~
01.21.32a | unctuous rubbing (udvartana), bathing, the use of perfumes and garlands, white clothing, the drawing out of the doṣas at the proper time,
01.21.32b | ~
01.21.33a | and the use of rejuvenative (rasāyana) and aphrodisiac applications: this destroys excessive leanness and produces the greatest increase of body (upacaya) in men.
01.21.33b | ~
01.21.34a | From not worrying about tasks, from replenishing therapy (saṃtarpaṇa), and from indulgence in sleep a man certainly grows plump like a boar.
01.21.34b | ~
01.21.35a | But when the mind is exhausted, and the karmātmans (those whose nature is action), overcome by exhaustion (klama), withdraw from their objects, then a person sleeps.
01.21.35b | ~
01.21.36a | Happiness and misery, nourishment and leanness, strength and weakness, virility and impotence, knowledge and ignorance, life and its absence depend on sleep.
01.21.36b | ~
01.21.37a | Sleep indulged in at the wrong time, in excess, or not at all takes away happiness and life like another night of doom (kālarātri).
01.21.37b | ~
01.21.38a | That same sleep, properly used, on the other hand endows the body with happiness and life, as true insight (buddhi), once it has come, endows a yogin with accomplishment (siddhi).
01.21.38b | ~
01.21.39a | Those worn out by singing, study, wine, women, work, carrying loads and travelling; those with indigestion, the wounded, the wasted, the old, children and likewise the weak;
01.21.39b | ~
01.21.40a | those afflicted by thirst, diarrhoea (atīsāra) and pain (śūla); those with dyspnoea (śvāsa), those with hiccup (hikkā), the lean; those who have fallen, the injured, the insane; those exhausted by riding and by keeping awake;
01.21.40b | ~
01.21.41a | those exhausted by anger, grief and fear, and those who are accustomed to day-sleep: all these may take day-sleep in every season.
01.21.41b | ~
01.21.42a | For in this way balance of the dhātus and strength arise in them; Kapha (śleṣman) nourishes the limbs, and there is stability of life.
01.21.42b | ~
01.21.43a | But in Grīṣma, for those made dry by ādāna (taking up), when Vāta (māruta) is increasing and because the nights are very short, day-sleep is commended.
01.21.43b | ~
01.21.44a | In the seasons other than Grīṣma, Kapha (śleṣman) and Pitta become aggravated by day-sleep; therefore day-sleep is not commended in them.
01.21.44b | ~
01.21.45a | The fat, those who constantly take fat (sneha), the Kapha-predominant, those with Kapha diseases, and those afflicted by dūṣīviṣa (vitiating poison) should never sleep by day.
01.21.45b | ~
01.21.46a | halīmaka, pain in the head (śiraḥśūla), staimitya (torpor), heaviness of the limbs, body-ache (aṅgamarda), loss of the digestive fire (agni), a coating of the heart (hṛdaya),
01.21.46b | ~
01.21.47a | swelling (śopha), loss of taste (arocaka), nausea (hṛllāsa), pīnasa, ardhāvabhedaka, koṭha, sores (arus), eruptions (piḍakā), itching (kaṇḍū), drowsiness (tandrā), cough (kāsa), diseases of the throat,
01.21.47b | ~
01.21.48a | confusion of memory and intellect, obstruction of the channels (srotas), fever (jvara), incapacity of the senses, and the setting in motion of the course (vega) of poison
01.21.48b | ~
01.21.49a | These arise in men from indulging in unwholesome day-sleep; therefore the wise man, having understood sleep as wholesome and unwholesome, should sleep happily.
01.21.49b | ~
01.21.50a | Waking at night is drying (rūkṣa); sleeping by day is unctuous (snigdha); but dozing while seated is neither drying nor channel-clogging.
01.21.50b | ~
01.21.51a | As food is in the maintenance of the body, so sleep is held to be conducive to well-being; and obesity and leanness arise especially from sleep and food.
01.21.51b | ~
01.21.52a | Oil massage (abhyaṅga) and rubbing with unguents (utsādana), bathing, the meat-juices (rasa) of domestic, marsh (ānūpa) and aquatic (audaka) animals, a meal of śāli rice with curd, milk, fat (sneha), wine, ease of mind,
01.21.52b | ~
01.21.53a | smells and sounds agreeable to the mind, massages (saṃvāhana), tarpaṇa of the eyes, a paste (lepa) on the head and face,
01.21.53b | ~
01.21.54a | a well-spread bed, a comfortable house and the accustomed time: these quickly bring on sleep that has been lost through some cause.
01.21.54b | ~
01.21.55a | Evacuation (vireka) of the body and of the head, emesis (chardana), fear, worry, anger, likewise medicated smoking (dhūma), exercise, bloodletting,
01.21.55b | ~
01.21.56a | fasting, an uncomfortable bed, nobility of mind (sattva) and the conquest of tamas: these check unwholesome excessive sleep that has arisen.
01.21.56b | ~
01.21.57a | These same are to be known as the causes of loss of sleep, and also work, time (kāla), disorder (vikāra), nature (prakṛti) and Vāta (vāyu).
01.21.57b | ~
01.21.58a | Sleep is born of tamas, arises from Kapha (śleṣman), arises from fatigue of the mind and the body, is exogenous (āgantukī), follows a disease, and arises from the nature of night.
01.21.58b | ~
01.21.59a | The sleep that is held to arise from the nature of night, those who know it call the nurse of beings (bhūtadhātrī); the one born of tamas they call the root of evil (agha); the rest, again, they assign to diseases.
