01.16.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on the cikitsāprābhṛta (Cikitsāprābhṛtīya).
01.16.2 | Thus said the venerable Ātreya.
01.16.3a | The man whom the cikitsāprābhṛta, intelligent, versed in the science and devoted to practice, purges obtains happiness through proper application (yoga).
01.16.3b | ~
01.16.4a | But the man whom an ignorant one who fancies himself a physician (vaidyamānin) purges, that man obtains suffering from excessive application (atiyoga) and deficient application (ayoga).
01.16.4b | ~
01.16.5a | Weakness, lightness, languor, lessening of the diseases, relish (for food), purity of heart and complexion, hunger and thirst, the coming on of the urges (vega) at the proper time,
01.16.5b | ~
01.16.6a | purity of intellect, senses and mind, downward movement of Vāta (māruta), and the normal functioning of the digestive fire of the body: these are the signs of one properly purged.
01.16.6b | ~
01.16.7a | Spitting, impurity of the heart, nausea (utkleśa) of Kapha (śleṣman) and Pitta, distension, loss of taste, vomiting, absence of weakness, absence of lightness,
01.16.7b | ~
01.16.8a | weariness of the shanks and thighs, drowsiness, staimitya, onset of pīnasa, and suppression of Vāta (māruta): these are the signs of the unpurged.
01.16.8b | ~
01.16.9a | When faeces, Pitta, Kapha and Vāta have come out in sequence, the blood that flows after that, resembling water of fat and flesh,
01.16.9b | ~
01.16.10a | or water free of Kapha (śleṣman) and Pitta, or black blood, in one who is thirsty, afflicted by Vāta (māruta) and fainting: that is excessive application (atiyoga).
01.16.10b | ~
01.16.11a | For when emesis is overdone these same signs occur; and upward-moving Vāta diseases and, in addition, obstruction of speech may occur.
01.16.11b | ~
01.16.12a | Therefore a man should approach as refuge the cikitsāprābhṛta who would join him to the utmost with life and with happiness.
01.16.12b | ~
01.16.13a | Indigestion, loss of taste, corpulence, pallor, heaviness, exhaustion, the arising of pimples (piḍakā), koṭha and itching, and discontent (arati),
01.16.13b | ~
01.16.14a | laziness, fatigue and weakness, foul smell, lassitude (avasādaka), agitation (samutkleśa) of Kapha (śleṣman) and Pitta, loss of sleep, excessive sleep,
01.16.14b | ~
01.16.15a | drowsiness, impotence, lack of intellect, seeing inauspicious dreams, and loss of strength and complexion in one who is thirsty, even with nourishing measures,
01.16.15b | ~
01.16.16a | these are the signs of one with much doṣa. For him purification, both upward and downward, according to the doṣa and according to strength, is beneficial.
01.16.16b | ~
01.16.17a | Thus, in one whose bowel (koṣṭha) is purified the digestive fire of the body increases, the diseases subside and the normal state (prakṛti) continues.
01.16.17b | ~
01.16.18a | His senses, mind and intellect, and complexion become clear; and strength, nourishment, offspring and virility arise in him.
01.16.18b | ~
01.16.19a | He reaches old age with difficulty and, free from disease, lives long. Therefore a man should drink purification at the proper time, applied with rational planning (yukti).
01.16.19b | ~
01.16.20a | Doṣas subdued by lightening and digesting measures sometimes become aggravated; but those that are subdued by purifications do not arise again.
01.16.20b | ~
01.16.21a | With doṣas and with trees, when the root is not destroyed, the return of diseases and of shoots that have gone is certain.
01.16.21b | ~
01.16.22a | In one wasted by medicine, nourishing by foods alone is wholesome, foods accompanied by ghee, meat-juice, milk and pleasing yūṣa,
01.16.22b | ~
01.16.23a | and by oil massage, rubbing with unguents, baths, and decoction enemas together with unctuous enemas; thus he obtains well-being and is joined with life for long.
01.16.23b | ~
01.16.24a | For those afflicted by excessive application (atiyoga), the drinking of ghee is praised, or oil prepared with sweet drugs (madhuraka), or else an unctuous enema.
01.16.24b | ~
01.16.25a | But one in whom there is deficient application (ayoga), that man, once oleated, one should purify again, taking account of measure, time and strength, and remembering the previous sequence.
01.16.25b | ~
01.16.26a | The diseases that arise when oleation, sweating, purification and the graduated diet (saṃsarjana) are done improperly: the means of their cure is in the siddhis (successes).
01.16.26b | ~
01.16.27a | From imbalance of causes the dhātus of the body become unbalanced, from balance of causes balanced; their cessation by their own nature (svabhāvoparama) is constant.
01.16.27b | ~
01.16.28a | There is a cause for the arising of entities; for their cessation there is no cause. Some think that even there the non-continuation of the cause is the cause.
01.16.28b | ~
01.16.29a | To the teacher who had thus stated the matter, Agniveśa said: "If cessation is by nature, what is the action of the cikitsāprābhṛta?
01.16.29b | ~
01.16.30a | Which unbalanced dhātus does the physician make balanced with medicines? Or what is treatment, O venerable one, or for what purpose is it applied?"
01.16.30b | ~
01.16.31a | Having heard that word of the disciple, Punarvasu spoke: "Hear, O gentle one, the reasoning (yukti) on this that has been seen by the great sages.
01.16.31b | ~
01.16.32a | Because there is no cause of destruction, the cause of the destruction of entities is not known, as with the cause of the passing of ever-moving time.
01.16.32b | ~
01.16.33a | Because of its swift going, as an entity has come into being, so it perishes; for its cessation there is no cause, nor any acting otherwise.
01.16.33b | ~
01.16.34a | The actions by which the dhātus in the body become balanced, that is the treatment of disorders; that is held to be the action of physicians.
01.16.34b | ~
01.16.35a | "How may there be no imbalance of the dhātus in the body, and continuance of the balanced ones?": for this purpose action is performed.
01.16.35b | ~
01.16.36a | By giving up the causes of imbalance and by using balanced ones, the unbalanced (dhātus) do not persist; the dhātus become balanced.
01.16.36b | ~
01.16.37a | Since by balanced causes he would produce balanced dhātus, therefore the cikitsāprābhṛta is the giver of body, happiness and life.
01.16.37b | ~
01.16.38a | By the giving of body, happiness and life, the physician becomes the giver of dharma, wealth (artha), pleasure (kāma), and of both worlds of men.
01.16.38b | ~
01.16.39a | Here, the verses: the quality of the cikitsāprābhṛta, and the fault that rests in the other; the sign of proper, deficient and excessive application (yoga, ayoga, atiyoga) relating to purification;
01.16.39b | ~
01.16.39c | ~
01.16.40a | the signs of one with much doṣa, and the qualities of purification; and the bare outline of treatment relating to success and ill effects;
01.16.40b | ~
01.16.41a | and what reasoning (yukti) there is in treatment, and what purpose the physician accomplishes: all that the sage stated in the chapter on the cikitsāprābhṛta.
01.16.41b | ~
01.16.P1 | In the treatise composed by Agniveśa and redacted by Caraka, in the Śloka section (Ślokasthāna), the sixteenth chapter named Cikitsāprābhṛtīya.
01.16.P2 | The tetrad on preparation (kalpanā): completed.
