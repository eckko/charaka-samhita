01.04.1 | Now, therefore, we shall explain the chapter "Ṣaḍvirecanaśatāśritīya" (On the six hundred evacuatives).
01.04.2 | Thus spoke the venerable Ātreya.
01.04.3 | Here there are six hundred evacuatives (virecana), six sources of evacuatives, five sources (yoni) of kaṣāya (medicinal extract), a fivefold preparation of kaṣāya, fifty great kaṣāyas (mahākaṣāya) and five hundred kaṣāyas: this is the summary.
01.04.4 | "Six hundred evacuatives", as was stated: having cited this here in brief, we shall explain it in detail in the Kalpopaniṣad. There, one hundred and thirty-three applications (yoga) are set forth among the phala (fruit), thirty-nine applications in jīmūtaka, forty-five in ikṣvāku; dhāmārgava is joined in applications sixty ways; kuṭaja goes to application eighteen ways; kṛtavedhana is joined in applications sixty ways; for śyāmā and trivṛt a hundred applications are set forth, and here there are ten others; caturaṅgula goes to application twelve ways; lodhra, in the procedure, is joined with sixteen applications; mahāvṛkṣa is joined with twenty applications; thirty-nine applications of saptalā and śaṅkhinī; forty-eight of dantī and dravantī: thus the six hundred evacuatives.
01.04.5 | "Six sources of evacuatives": latex (kṣīra), root, bark, leaf, flower and fruit.
01.04.6 | "Five sources of kaṣāya": sweet kaṣāya, sour kaṣāya, pungent kaṣāya, bitter kaṣāya and astringent kaṣāya: this is the designation in the treatise.
01.04.7 | "Fivefold preparation of kaṣāya": namely, expressed juice (svarasa), paste (kalka), decoction (śṛta), cold infusion (śīta) and hot infusion (phāṇṭa): thus kaṣāya.
01.04.P1 | The juice from a substance pressed in a press (yantra) is called svarasa (expressed juice).
01.04.P2 | The lump of things ground with liquid is declared kalka (paste).
01.04.P3 | A substance boiled on the fire, physicians call śṛta (decoction).
01.04.P4 | From a substance crushed and kept at night in heated water,
01.04.P5 | the extract (kaṣāya) that comes out is declared śīta (cold infusion).
01.04.P6 | That which, having been thrown into hot water, is mashed is declared phāṇṭa (hot infusion). Of these, the strength is greater in each one listed earlier; hence the preparation of kaṣāya depends on the strength of the disease and of the patient; but it is not so that all are useful everywhere.
01.04.8 | What was stated, we shall explain in order; namely: jīvanīya (vitalizing), bṛṃhaṇīya (nourishing), lekhanīya (reducing), bhedanīya (breaking up), sandhānīya (uniting) and dīpanīya (stimulating digestion): this is the group of six kaṣāyas; balya (strengthening), varṇya (good for the complexion), kaṇṭhya (good for the throat) and hṛdya (pleasing to the heart): the group of four; tṛptighna (removing satiety), arśoghna (removing piles), kuṣṭhaghna (removing kuṣṭha), kaṇḍūghna (removing itching), krimighna (removing worms) and viṣaghna (removing poison): the group of six; stanyajanana (producing breast-milk), stanyaśodhana (purifying breast-milk), śukrajanana (producing semen) and śukraśodhana (purifying semen): the group of four; snehopaga (aiding oleation), svedopaga (aiding sweating), vamanopaga (aiding emesis), virecanopaga (aiding purgation), āsthāpanopaga (aiding decoction enema), anuvāsanopaga (aiding unctuous enema) and śirovirecanopaga (aiding head-evacuation): the group of seven; chardinigrahaṇa (checking vomiting), tṛṣṇānigrahaṇa (checking thirst) and hikkānigrahaṇa (checking hiccup): the group of three; purīṣasaṃgrahaṇīya (binding the faeces), purīṣavirajanīya (correcting the colour of faeces), mūtrasaṃgrahaṇīya (binding urine), mūtravirajanīya (correcting the colour of urine) and mūtravirecanīya (promoting urine flow): the group of five; kāsahara (removing cough), śvāsahara (removing dyspnoea), śothahara (removing swelling), jvarahara (removing fever) and śramahara (removing fatigue): the group of five; dāhapraśamana (allaying burning), śītapraśamana (allaying cold), udardapraśamana (allaying udarda), aṅgamardapraśamana (allaying body-ache) and śūlapraśamana (allaying pain): the group of five; śoṇitasthāpana (stabilizing the blood), vedanāsthāpana (arresting pain), saṃjñāsthāpana (restoring consciousness), prajāsthāpana (establishing progeny) and vayaḥsthāpana (stabilizing age): the group of five. Thus the fifty great kaṣāyas are explained, and for the definition and illustration of the great kaṣāyas.
01.04.P7 | In each one of those great kaṣāyas we shall explain in order ten constituent kaṣāyas each; these are the five hundred kaṣāyas.
01.04.9 | Namely: jīvaka, ṛṣabhaka, medā, mahāmedā, kākolī, kṣīrakākolī, mudgaparṇī, māṣaparṇī, jīvantī and madhuka: these ten are jīvanīya (vitalizing) (1). Kṣīriṇī, rājakṣavaka, aśvagandhā, kākolī, kṣīrakākolī, vāṭhyāyanī, bhadraudanī, bhāradvājī, payasyā and ṛṣyagandhā: these ten are bṛṃhaṇīya (nourishing) (2). Musta, kuṣṭha, haridrā, dāruharidrā, vacā, ativiṣā, kaṭurohiṇī, citraka, cirabilva and haimavatī: these ten are lekhanīya (reducing) (3). Suvahā, arka, urubuka, agnimukhī, citrā, citraka, cirabilva, śaṅkhinī, śakulādanī and svarṇakṣīriṇī: these ten are bhedanīya (breaking up) (4). Madhuka, madhuparṇī, pṛśniparṇī, ambaṣṭhakī, samaṅgā, mocarasa, dhātakī, lodhra, priyaṅgu and kaṭphala: these ten are sandhānīya (uniting) (5). Pippalī, pippalīmūla, cavya, citraka, śṛṅgavera, amlavetasa, marica, ajamodā, bhallātakāsthi and hiṅguniryāsa: these ten are dīpanīya (stimulating digestion) (6). This is the group of six kaṣāyas.
01.04.10 | Aindrī, ṛṣabhī, atirasā, ṛṣyaproktā, payasyā, aśvagandhā, sthirā, rohiṇī, balā and atibalā: these ten are balya (strengthening) (7). Candana, tuṅga, padmaka, uśīra, madhuka, mañjiṣṭhā, sārivā, payasyā, sitā and latā: these ten are varṇya (good for the complexion) (8). Sārivā, ikṣumūla, madhuka, pippalī, drākṣā, vidārī, kaiṭarya, haṃsapādī, bṛhatī and kaṇṭakārikā: these ten are kaṇṭhya (good for the throat) (9). Āmra, āmrātaka, likuca, karamarda, vṛkṣāmla, amlavetasa, kuvala, badara, dāḍima and mātuluṅga: these ten are hṛdya (pleasing to the heart) (10). This is the group of four kaṣāyas.
01.04.11 | Nāgara, cavya, citraka, viḍaṅga, mūrvā, guḍūcī, vacā, musta, pippalī and paṭola: these ten are tṛptighna (removing satiety) (11). Kuṭaja, bilva, citraka, nāgara, ativiṣā, abhayā, dhanvayāsaka, dāruharidrā, vacā and cavya: these ten are arśoghna (removing piles) (12). Khadira, abhayā, āmalaka, haridrā, aruṣkara, saptaparṇa, āragvadha, karavīra, viḍaṅga and jātīpravāla: these ten are kuṣṭhaghna (removing kuṣṭha) (13). Candana, nalada, kṛtamāla, naktamāla, nimba, kuṭaja, sarṣapa, madhuka, dāruharidrā and musta: these ten are kaṇḍūghna (removing itching) (14). Akṣīva, marica, gaṇḍīra, kebuka, viḍaṅga, nirguṇḍī, kiṇihī, śvadaṃṣṭrā, vṛṣaparṇikā and ākhuparṇikā: these ten are krimighna (removing worms) (15). Haridrā, mañjiṣṭhā, suvahā, sūkṣmailā, pālindī, candana, kataka, śirīṣa, sindhuvāra and śleṣmātaka: these ten are viṣaghna (removing poison) (16). This is the group of six kaṣāyas.
01.04.12 | Roots of vīraṇa, śāli, ṣaṣṭika, ikṣuvālikā, darbha, kuśa, kāśa, gundra, itkaṭa and kattṛṇa: these ten are stanyajanana (producing breast-milk) (17). Pāṭhā, mahauṣadha, suradāru, musta, mūrvā, guḍūcī, vatsakaphala, kirātatiktaka, kaṭurohiṇī and sārivā: these ten are stanyaśodhana (purifying breast-milk) (18). Jīvaka, ṛṣabhaka, kākolī, kṣīrakākolī, mudgaparṇī, māṣaparṇī, medā, vṛddharuhā, jaṭilā and kuliṅgā: these ten are śukrajanana (producing semen) (19). Kuṣṭha, elavāluka, kaṭphala, samudraphena, kadambaniryāsa, ikṣu, kāṇḍekṣu, ikṣuraka, vasuka and uśīra: these ten are śukraśodhana (purifying semen) (20). This is the group of four kaṣāyas.
01.04.13 | Mṛdvīkā, madhuka, madhuparṇī, medā, vidārī, kākolī, kṣīrakākolī, jīvaka, jīvantī and śālaparṇī: these ten are snehopaga (aiding oleation) (21). Śobhāñjanaka, eraṇḍa, arka, vṛścīra, punarnavā, yava, tila, kulattha, māṣa and badara: these ten are svedopaga (aiding sweating) (22). Madhu, madhuka, kovidāra, karbudāra, nīpa, vidula, bimbī, śaṇapuṣpī, sadāpuṣpā and pratyakpuṣpā: these ten are vamanopaga (aiding emesis) (23). Drākṣā, kāśmarya, parūṣaka, abhayā, āmalaka, bibhītaka, kuvala, badara, karkandhu and pīlu: these ten are virecanopaga (aiding purgation) (24). Trivṛt, bilva, pippalī, kuṣṭha, sarṣapa, vacā, vatsakaphala, śatapuṣpā, madhuka and madanaphala: these ten are āsthāpanopaga (aiding decoction enema) (25). Rāsnā, suradāru, bilva, madana, śatapuṣpā, vṛścīra, punarnavā, śvadaṃṣṭrā, agnimantha and śyonāka: these ten are anuvāsanopaga (aiding unctuous enema) (26). Jyotiṣmatī, kṣavaka, marica, pippalī, viḍaṅga, śigru, sarṣapa, apāmārgataṇḍula, śvetā and mahāśvetā: these ten are śirovirecanopaga (aiding head-evacuation) (27). This is the group of seven kaṣāyas.
01.04.14 | Sprouts of jambu and āmra, mātuluṅga, amlabadara, dāḍima, yava, yaṣṭikā, uśīra, earth (mṛt) and lājā: these ten are chardinigrahaṇa (checking vomiting) (28). Nāgara, dhanvayavāsaka, musta, parpaṭaka, candana, kirātatiktaka, guḍūcī, hrīvera, dhānyaka and paṭola: these ten are tṛṣṇānigrahaṇa (checking thirst) (29). Śaṭī, puṣkaramūla, badarabīja, kaṇṭakārikā, bṛhatī, vṛkṣaruhā, abhayā, pippalī, durālabhā and kulīraśṛṅgī: these ten are hikkānigrahaṇa (checking hiccup) (30). This is the group of three kaṣāyas.
01.04.15 | Priyaṅgu, anantā, āmrāsthi, kaṭvaṅga, lodhra, mocarasa, samaṅgā, dhātakīpuṣpa, padmā and padmakeśara: these ten are purīṣasaṃgrahaṇīya (binding the faeces) (31). Bark of jambu and śallakī, kacchurā, madhūka, śālmalī, śrīveṣṭaka, roasted earth (bhṛṣṭamṛt), payasyā, utpala and tilakaṇa: these ten are purīṣavirajanīya (correcting the colour of faeces) (32). Jambu, āmra, plakṣa, vaṭa, kapītana, uḍumbara, aśvattha, bhallātaka, aśmantaka and somavalka: these ten are mūtrasaṃgrahaṇīya (binding urine) (33). Padma, utpala, nalina, kumuda, saugandhika, puṇḍarīka, śatapatra, madhuka, priyaṅgu and dhātakīpuṣpa: these ten are mūtravirajanīya (correcting the colour of urine) (34). Roots of vṛkṣādanī, śvadaṃṣṭrā, vasuka, vaśira, pāṣāṇabheda, darbha, kuśa, kāśa, gundra and itkaṭa: these ten are mūtravirecanīya (promoting urine flow) (35). This is the group of five kaṣāyas.
01.04.16 | Drākṣā, abhayā, āmalaka, pippalī, durālabhā, śṛṅgī, kaṇṭakārikā, vṛścīra, punarnavā and tāmalakī: these ten are kāsahara (removing cough) (36). Śaṭī, puṣkaramūla, amlavetasa, elā, hiṅgu, aguru, surasā, tāmalakī, jīvantī and caṇḍā: these ten are śvāsahara (removing dyspnoea) (37). Pāṭalā, agnimantha, śyonāka, bilva, kāśmarya, kaṇṭakārikā, bṛhatī, śālaparṇī, pṛśniparṇī and gokṣuraka: these ten are śvayathuhara (removing swelling) (38). Sārivā, śarkarā, pāṭhā, mañjiṣṭhā, drākṣā, pīlu, parūṣaka, abhayā, āmalaka and bibhītaka: these ten are jvarahara (removing fever) (39). Drākṣā, kharjūra, priyāla, badara, dāḍima, phalgu, paruṣaka, ikṣu, yava and ṣaṣṭikā: these ten are śramahara (removing fatigue) (40). This is the group of five kaṣāyas.
01.04.17 | Lājā, candana, kāśmaryaphala, madhūka, śarkarā, nīlotpala, uśīra, sārivā, guḍūcī and hribera: these ten are dāhapraśamana (allaying burning) (41). Tagara, aguru, dhānyaka, śṛṅgavera, bhūtīka, vacā, kaṇṭakārī, agnimantha, śyonāka and pippalī: these ten are śītapraśamana (allaying cold) (42). Tinduka, priyāla, badara, khadira, kadara, saptaparṇa, aśvakarṇa, arjuna, asana and arimeda: these ten are udardapraśamana (allaying udarda) (43). Vidārīgandhā, pṛśniparṇī, bṛhatī, kaṇṭakārikā, eraṇḍa, kākolī, candana, uśīra, elā and madhuka: these ten are aṅgamardapraśamana (allaying body-ache) (44). Pippalī, pippalīmūla, cavya, citraka, śṛṅgavera, marica, ajamodā, ajagandhā, ajājī and gaṇḍīra: these ten are śūlapraśamana (allaying pain) (45). This is the group of five kaṣāyas.
01.04.18 | Madhu, madhuka, rudhira, mocarasa, mṛtkapāla, lodhra, gairika, priyaṅgu, śarkarā and lājā: these ten are śoṇitasthāpana (stabilizing the blood) (46). Śāla, kaṭphala, kadamba, padmaka, tumba, mocarasa, śirīṣa, vañjula, elavāluka and aśoka: these ten are vedanāsthāpana (arresting pain) (47). Hiṅgu, kaiṭarya, arimedā, vacā, coraka, vayasthā, golomī, jaṭilā, palaṅkaṣā and aśokarohiṇī: these ten are saṃjñāsthāpana (restoring consciousness) (48). Aindrī, brāhmī, śatavīryā, sahasravīryā, amoghā, avyathā, śivā, ariṣṭā, vāṭyapuṣpī and viṣvaksenakāntā: these ten are prajāsthāpana (establishing progeny) (49). Amṛtā, abhayā, dhātrī, muktā, śvetā, jīvantī, atirasā, maṇḍūkaparṇī, sthirā and punarnavā: these ten are vayaḥsthāpana (stabilizing age) (50). This is the group of five kaṣāyas.
01.04.19 | Thus, by combining the five hundred kaṣāyas, the fifty great kaṣāyas are explained, and for the definition and illustration of the great kaṣāyas.
01.04.20 | For there is no limit to elaboration, and excessive brevity does not serve the capability of those of little intellect; therefore they have been taught neither too briefly nor too elaborately.
01.04.P8 | For this many are sufficient for the practical use of those of little intellect, and, for the intelligent who are skilled in svālakṣaṇya (own characteristics), inference and rational planning (yukti), for knowing what is not stated.
01.04.21 | To the venerable Ātreya, who was speaking thus, Agniveśa said: "O venerable one, these five hundred kaṣāyas do not come to the full number, for those same members recur in these various great kaṣāyas."
01.04.22 | The venerable Ātreya said to him: "This should not be seen in that way by an intelligent person, O Agniveśa."
01.04.P9 | For even one thing, performing different actions, obtains many names; for example, a man is capable of doing many actions; whatever action he does, he obtains for each such action a particular secondary name connected with its agent, instrument and effect; the medicinal substance too should be viewed in the same way.
01.04.P10 | And if we found some one substance endowed with such qualities that it would be capable of performing all actions, who would wish to consider, or to teach to pupils, anything other than that?
