01.25.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter Yajjaḥpuruṣīya ('born of what is the person').
01.25.2 | Thus said the venerable Ātreya.
01.25.3a | Long ago, among the great sages who had perceived dharma directly and had assembled around the venerable Punarvasu, this discussion arose
01.25.3b | ~
01.25.4a | concerning the determination of the prior origin of this aggregate of self, senses, mind and sense-objects which is called the person (puruṣa), and of its diseases.
01.25.4b | ~
01.25.5a | Thereupon Vāmaka, lord of Kāśi, a knower of meaning, having approached the assembly of sages and saluted it, uttered these words:
01.25.5b | ~
01.25.6a | 'Sirs, are the diseases of the person held to be born of that of which the person is born, or not?' When the king had said this, Punarvasu spoke to the sages:
01.25.6b | ~
01.25.7a | All of you, whose doubts have been cut away by boundless knowledge and specialised knowledge (vijñāna), are able to cut away the doubt of the king of Kāśi.
01.25.7b | ~
01.25.8a | Pārīkṣi Maudgalya, having first examined it, spoke these words: 'The person is born of the self (ātman), and the diseases are born of the self; for it is the cause.'
01.25.8b | ~
01.25.9a | It accumulates action (karma) and experiences the fruits of action; for without the conscious element (cetanādhātu) there is no occurrence of pleasure and pain.
01.25.9b | ~
01.25.10a | But Śaraloman said 'No': for the self, which hates sorrow, would never by itself join itself with diseases, which are sorrows.
01.25.10b | ~
01.25.11a | Rather, the mind, called sattva, pervaded by rajas and tamas, is the cause in the origin of the body and of disorders.
01.25.11b | ~
01.25.12a | But Vāryovida said 'No': for the mind alone is not the cause; without the body there are no bodily diseases, nor any abiding of the mind.
01.25.12b | ~
01.25.13a | Rather, beings are born of rasa, and so are diseases of various kinds; for the waters, those possessing rasa, are held to be the causes of their coming into being.
01.25.13b | ~
01.25.14a | But Hiraṇyākṣa said 'No': for the self is not held to be born of rasa, nor is the mind, which is beyond the senses; and likewise there are diseases born of sound and the rest.
01.25.14b | ~
01.25.15a | Rather, the person is born of six dhātus, and the diseases likewise are born of six dhātus; for this aggregate has been proclaimed by the early Sāṃkhyas to be born of six dhātus.
01.25.15b | ~
01.25.16a | To Kuśika, speaking thus, Kauṣika said 'That is not so: how indeed could one born of six dhātus come into being without mother and father?'
01.25.16b | ~
01.25.17a | Man is born from man, a cow from a cow, a horse from a horse; and diseases such as meha are spoken of as hereditary; those two (mother and father) are the cause here.
01.25.17b | ~
01.25.18a | But Bhadrakāpya said 'No': for a blind man is not born from a blind man; and, for you, a prior origin in the mother and father too is not tenable.
01.25.18b | ~
01.25.19a | Rather, the person is held to be born of action (karma), and his diseases are born of action; for without action there is no birth of diseases or of the person.
01.25.19b | ~
01.25.20a | But Bharadvāja said 'No': for the doer comes before the action; and no action that has not been done is seen, of which the person could be the fruit.
01.25.20b | ~
01.25.21a | Rather, own nature (svabhāva) is the cause of the existence of diseases and of the person, just as roughness, fluidity, mobility and heat are of those ending with fire (tejas).
01.25.21b | ~
01.25.22a | But Kāṅkāyana said 'No': for (then) there would be no fruit of undertaking; the failure and the success of things would come just from their own nature.
01.25.22b | ~
01.25.23a | Rather, Prajāpati, of boundless resolve, the offspring of Brahmā, is the creator of the pleasure and pain of this world, sentient and insentient.
01.25.23b | ~
01.25.24a | The mendicant (bhikṣu) Ātreya said 'That is not so': for Prajāpati, who always wishes the welfare of creatures, would not, like a wicked person, yoke (his) offspring with sorrows.
01.25.24b | ~
01.25.25a | Rather, the person is born just of time, and his diseases are born of time; the whole world is under the sway of time; time is the cause everywhere.
01.25.25b | ~
01.25.26a | As the sages were thus disputing, Punarvasu said this: 'Do not speak so; for the truth is hard to attain by resorting to a side.'
01.25.26b | ~
01.25.27a | For those who state arguments together with counter-arguments as if they were settled never reach the end of their side, like a sesame-presser in his movement.
01.25.27b | ~
01.25.28a | Having thus left aside the clash of arguments, let the inner self (adhyātma) be reflected upon; while the mass of darkness has not been shaken off, knowledge does not proceed toward the knowable.
01.25.28b | ~
01.25.29a | For the failure (vipad) of the very things whose excellence (sampad) would produce a man would bring forth diseases of various kinds.
01.25.29b | ~
01.25.30 | Then, having heard the words of the venerable Ātreya, Vāmaka, lord of Kāśi, again said to the venerable Ātreya: 'Venerable one, what is the cause of growth of the person, who is born of excellence (sampad) as cause, and of the diseases, which are born of failure (vipad) as cause?'
01.25.31 | The venerable Ātreya said to him: 'The use of wholesome food alone causes the growth of the person; the use of unwholesome food, on the other hand, is the cause of disease.'
01.25.32 | To the venerable Ātreya, speaking thus, Agniveśa said: 'Venerable one, how here can we know a characteristic, free from exception, of the wholesome and unwholesome kinds of food? We observe that the kinds of food declared wholesome and those declared unwholesome act contrarily under differences of measure (mātrā), time, processing (kriyā), place (bhūmi), body, doṣa, person and condition.'
01.25.33 | The venerable Ātreya said to him: "O Agniveśa, the kind of food that keeps the balanced dhātus of the body in their normal state and makes the imbalanced ones balanced, know that to be wholesome; the opposite, however, is unwholesome." This is the definition of the wholesome and the unwholesome, without exception.
01.25.34 | And Agniveśa said to the venerable Ātreya, who was speaking thus: "Venerable sir, this, taught in this way, will not, however, be understood by most physicians."
01.25.35 | The venerable Ātreya said to him: "O Agniveśa, those to whom the truth about food is known by quality, by substance, by action and in all its parts, and (to whom) the factors beginning with measure (are known), they are able to understand this when it is taught in this way."
01.25.P1 | But we shall teach this in such a way that most physicians will understand it when taught, without citing the factors beginning with measure; for these have gradations of many kinds.
01.25.P2 | But we shall explain in order the particulars of the kinds of food, both by their characteristics and by their parts.
01.25.36 | Namely: the nature of food as food is of one kind, because its purpose does not differ. Again, it has two sources, because it is of the nature of the immobile and the mobile; it has two kinds of effect (prabhāva), because of the distinction of wholesome and unwholesome outcome; it has four modes of use, because it is used as drink, as food to be eaten, as food to be chewed and as food to be licked; it has six tastes, because it is sixfold by the difference of taste (rasa); it has twenty qualities, because it possesses heavy, light, cold, hot, unctuous, dry, dull, sharp, stable, mobile, soft, hard, non-slimy, slimy, smooth, rough, subtle, gross, dense and liquid; and it has countless gradations, because of the multitude of combinations and preparations of substances.
01.25.37 | Whichever items among its products are mostly used, and are by their very nature most wholesome and most unwholesome for most people, each of those we shall teach properly.
01.25.38 | Namely: red śāli rice is the very best among awned grains (śūkadhānya) in being most wholesome; mudga among pod grains (śamīdhānya); rain-water among waters; saindhava among salts; jīvantī greens among leafy vegetables; eṇa meat among game meats; lāva among birds; godhā among burrow-dwellers; rohita among fish; cow's ghee among ghees; cow's milk among milks; sesame (tila) oil among fats born of plants; boar's muscle-fat among the muscle-fats of marsh animals; culukī fat among fish fats; pākahaṃsa fat among the fats of water-birds; fowl's fat among the fats of scratching birds (viṣkira); goat's fat (medas) among the fats of browsing animals (śākhāda); śṛṅgavera among tubers; mṛdvīkā among fruits; sugar (śarkarā) among sugarcane products. Thus the substances of the food products that are most wholesome by their very nature have been explained by way of pre-eminence.
01.25.39 | We shall also teach the most unwholesome: yavaka is foremost among awned grains in being most unwholesome; māṣa among pod grains; river water of the Varṣā season among waters; ūṣara salt among salts; sarṣapa greens among leafy vegetables; beef among game meats; kāṇakapota among birds; the frog among burrow-dwellers; cilicima among fish; sheep's ghee among ghees; sheep's milk among milks; kusumbha oil among plant fats; buffalo's muscle-fat among the muscle-fats of marsh animals; kumbhīra fat among fish fats; kākamadgu fat among the fats of water-birds; caṭaka fat among the fats of scratching birds; elephant's fat (medas) among the fats of browsing animals; nikuca among fruits; āluka among tubers; phāṇita among sugarcane products. Thus the foremost substances among the food products that are most unwholesome by their very nature have been explained. The wholesome and unwholesome items of the food products have been explained.
01.25.40 | Hereafter we shall further explain in order, by pre-eminence, the substances of actions and medicines too, together with their connections.

01.25.48 | Having heard those words of the venerable Ātreya, Agniveśa again spoke to the venerable Ātreya: "This whole subject has been set forth by the venerable one according to the enumeration, and we have heard it."
01.25.P4 | "Now we wish to hear the characteristic of the substances for āsavas (fermented preparations), without exception, taught not too briefly."
01.25.49 | The venerable Ātreya said to him: "O Agniveśa, in summary, the sources of āsavas are eight: grains, fruits, roots, heartwood, flowers, stalks, leaves and barks; with sugar as the ninth."
01.25.P5 | Among those very sources, which become countless through the combination and preparation of substances, learn the eighty-four āsavas, the most wholesome as appropriate (yathā).
01.25.P6 | Namely: surā, sauvīra, tuṣodaka, maireya, medaka and dhānyāmla are the six grain āsavas; the fruit āsavas of mṛdvīkā, kharjūra, kāśmarya, dhanvana, rājādana, tṛṇaśūnya, parūṣaka, abhayā, āmalaka, mṛgaliṇḍikā, jāmbava, kapittha, kuvala, badara, karkandhū, pīlu, priyāla, panasa, nyagrodha, aśvattha, plakṣa, kapītana, udumbara, ajamoda, śṛṅgāṭaka and śaṅkhinī are twenty-six; with the roots of vidārigandhā, aśvagandhā, kṛṣṇagandhā, śatāvarī, śyāmā, trivṛt, dantī, dravantī, bilva, urubūka and citraka there are eleven root āsavas; with śāla, priyaka, aśvakarṇa, candana, syandana, khadira, kadara, saptaparṇa, arjuna, asana, arimeda, tinduka, kiṇihī, śamī, śukti, śiṃśapā, śirīṣa, vañjala, dhanvana and madhūka there are twenty heartwood āsavas; with the flowers of padma, utpala, nali, kumuda, saugandhika, puṇḍarīka, śatapatra, madhūka, priyaṅgu and dhātakī there are ten flower āsavas; the stalk āsavas are (four), with ikṣu, kāṇḍekṣu, ikṣuvālikā and puṇḍraka as the fourth; the leaf āsavas of paṭola and tāḍaka are two; the bark āsavas are (four), with tilvaka, lodhra, elavāluka and kramuka as the fourth; and the sugar āsava is just one.
01.25.P7 | Thus the eighty-four of these āsavas, (made) from āsava substances not mixed with one another, have been indicated.
01.25.P8 | These have the name āsava because they are steeped (āsuta).
01.25.P9 | But the detailed division of their combinations of substances, and their processing, are of many kinds.
01.25.P10 | For āsavas, prepared each in its own way by combination and processing, perform their own action.
01.25.P11 | And the factors of each of those āsavas, beginning with combination, processing, place, time and measure, are taught each for its case, after considering the particular effect (to be produced).
01.25.50a | And here there is: This (number), eighty increased by four, has been stated of the excellent āsavas that give strength to the mind, body and digestive fire, destroy sleeplessness, grief and loss of taste, and gladden.
