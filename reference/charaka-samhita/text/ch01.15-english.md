01.15.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on what is to be prepared (Upakalpanīya).
01.15.2 | Thus said the venerable Ātreya.
01.15.3 | Here, the physician who wishes to make a king, or one equal to a king, or another person of abundant wealth drink emesis or purgation is to have the requisites prepared even before the drinking of the medicine; for even when the medicine goes on properly they serve for attendant use (pratibhoga); for when the time is pressing and an emergency has arisen, the proper collecting of medicines is not easy to do quickly, even when buying is possible.
01.15.4 | To the venerable Ātreya, speaking thus, Agniveśa said: "Surely, O venerable one, one who has knowledge should make arrangements at the very outset in such a way that, the arrangements made, the medicine would surely succeed invariably; for the success of all procedures is held to be caused by proper administration, and ill effect by improper administration. Now if a procedure undertaken properly or improperly succeeds or fails without any rule, knowledge is equal to ignorance."
01.15.5 | The venerable Ātreya said to him: "It is possible, O Agniveśa, for us or also for those like us to make arrangements in such a way that, the arrangements made, the medicine would surely succeed invariably, and to teach that excellence of administration properly; for there is no one who would be able to grasp this when thus taught, or, having grasped it, to carry it out so or to apply it. For subtle are the different states of doṣa, medicine, place, time, strength, body, diet, suitability (sātmya), mind (sattva), constitution (prakṛti) and age, which, when thought over, would confuse the intellect even of one whose intellect is clear and vast, how much more of one of little intellect. Therefore we shall teach both of these properly: the proper administration of medicines, and, later in the Siddhi (section), the remedies for the ill effects of those that have gone wrong."
01.15.6 | Now first we shall teach in brief the requisites, various though they are. Namely: one skilled in the science of building should first of all prepare a commendable house that is sturdy, sheltered from wind, with one part open to the wind, easy to move about in, not at the foot of a hill, not to be reached by smoke, sun, water and dust, nor by unwelcome sounds, touches, tastes, sights and smells, and having a well, mortar, pestle, latrine, bathing place and kitchen.
01.15.7 | Then he should prepare: attendants endowed with good character, purity, good conduct, attachment, dexterity and courtesy, skilled in nursing care, well versed in all tasks, cooks of soup and rice, bathers, masseurs, lifters, those who lay the patient down and grinders of medicine, not unwilling in any task; likewise companions skilled in song, instrumental music, ullāpaka, verses, gāthās, tales (ākhyāyikā), itihāsa and purāṇa, knowing intentions, approved and knowing place and time; likewise lāva birds, kapiñjala birds, hares, hariṇa deer, eṇa deer, kālapucchaka deer, mṛgamātṛka deer and rams; a cow yielding milk, gentle, healthy, with a living calf, well provided with grass, shelter and drinking water; dishes, rinsing vessels, water-tubs, maṇika jars, pots, piṭhara pans, paryoga vessels, kumbhī pots, jars, basins, saucers, ladles, mats, buckets, cooking pans, churning sticks, leather, cloth, thread, cotton, wool and the like; and beds, seats and the like, with ewers and receptacles placed near, with well-arranged spreads, upper covers and pillows, with back-rests, convenient for use in the acts of lying down, sitting, oleation, sweating, oil massage, plaster, affusion, anointing, emesis, purgation, decoction enema, unctuous enema, head-evacuation, urination and defecation; grinding stones, very smooth, rough and medium, with well-washed upper stones; knives serving as equipment, a smoking pipe, an enema nozzle, an uttarabasti nozzle, a handful of kuśa grass, a balance and a measuring vessel; ghee, oil, muscle-fat, marrow, honey (kṣaudra), phāṇita, salt, fuel, water, madhu, sīdhu, surā, sauvīraka, tuṣodaka, maireya, medaka, curd, curd-scum, udasvit, sour gruel (dhānyāmla) and urines; likewise śāli rice, ṣaṣṭika rice, mudga, māṣa, barley, sesame, kulattha, badara, grapes, kāśmarya, parūṣaka, abhayā, āmalaka and bibhītaka, and substances of various kinds serving as means of oleation and sweating; and likewise medicines that remove upward, move downward, and act both ways, and those named binding, stimulating digestion, digesting, pacifying, Vāta-removing and so on; and whatever other equipment he would know of for counteracting ill effects, having reckoned them up, and whatever serves for attendant use: each such thing he should prepare.
01.15.8 | Then he should treat that person, as is fitting, with oleation and sweating as described. If in this interval some more severe disease, mental or bodily, should suddenly come upon him, he should first strive to turn back just that disease of his; then, having turned it back, he should treat him for just as long a time with just such a procedure.
01.15.9 | Then, having observed that person to be furnished with oleation and sweating, of unimpaired mind, to have passed the night comfortably, his food well digested, bathed including the head, his limbs anointed, garlanded, clad in unsoiled clothes, and to have worshipped the gods, fire, the twice-born, teachers, elders and physicians, he should, on a desired asterism, lunar day, karaṇa and muhūrta, having had brahmins perform the recitation of blessings (svasti), make him drink a measure of madanaphala extract (kaṣāya), consecrated with benedictions pronounced, and mixed with honey, madhuka, rock salt and phāṇita.
01.15.10 | But the size of the measure of madanaphala extract, and the sizes of the measures of all purifying drugs, are to be considered for each person; for as much purifying drug as, when drunk by someone, suffices for removing the morbid doṣas and not for excessive or deficient action, that much is to be known as the size of his measure.
01.15.11 | And when he has drunk, one should wait a muhūrta. When he knows that his doṣa is undergoing liquefaction, by the appearance of sweat; is dislodged from its sites, by horripilation; has reached the abdomen, by the distension of the abdomen; and has turned upward, by nausea and flow from the mouth: then he should give him, to sit on, a seat knee-high, uncramped, with a well-arranged spread, upper cover and pillow, and with a back-rest, and should have receptacles brought near. Approved friends before whom he need not feel ashamed should exert themselves in holding his forehead, supporting his sides, pressing his navel and rubbing his back.
01.15.12 | Then he should instruct him: "With lips, palate and throat opened, with not very great effort, urging on the urges that have arisen, bending the neck and upper body a little, setting going at each approach of an urge those that do not flow, touching the throat with two fingers whose nails are well pared, or with stalks of utpala, kumuda or saugandhika, let them flow comfortably." He should do so. Then, attentive, one should observe his urges that have come into the receptacle; for from seeing the particulars of the urges the skilled one would perceive the particulars of proper, deficient and excessive action; and one who sees the particulars of the urges would, moreover, understand from the signs what is to be done, as is fitting. Therefore, attentive, he should observe the urges.
01.15.13 | There, these are the means of knowing the particulars of deficient, proper and excessive action. Namely: no flow from anywhere, or the coming out of the medicine alone, and obstruction of the urges, are the signs of deficient action. Flow in time, distress that is not very great, removal of the doṣas in due sequence, and stopping by itself, are the signs of proper action. Through proper action, by the particular quantity of doṣa, the division into strong, mild and medium is to be known. By excess of action, the coming of frothy blood and candrikās: these are the signs of excessive action.
01.15.P1 | There he should know these complications caused by excessive or deficient action: distension (ādhmāna), cutting pain (parikartikā), oozing (parisrāva), approach to the heart (hṛdayopasaraṇa), stiffness of the limbs, jīvādāna (literally "taking away of life"), displacement (vibhraṃśa), rigidity and exhaustion (klama): these are the complications.
01.15.14 | And having observed that he has vomited with proper action, his hands, feet and face well washed, having let him rest a muhūrta, and having made him drink any one of the unctuous, evacuative and pacifying smokings according to suitability, he should again have him rinse with water.
01.15.15 | And having made him, who has rinsed with water, enter a windless room and lie down, he should instruct him: "Not indulging even with the mind in these things: loud speech, over-eating, excessive standing, excessive walking, anger, grief, cold, sun, dew and strong wind, going in vehicles, sexual intercourse (grāmyadharma), not sleeping at night, sleep by day, incompatible eating, eating during indigestion, unsuitable, untimely, limited, very deficient, heavy and irregular eating, and suppression and forcing of urges, pass the whole day."
01.15.P2 | He should do so.
01.15.16 | Then, in the evening or on the next day, having bathed him with pleasant water, he should make him drink gruel of old red śāli rice grains, well softened, preceded by its maṇḍa, comfortably warm, having observed the strength of his digestive fire. So at the second and the third mealtime; but at the fourth mealtime he should feed him a well-boiled thick gruel (vilepī) of the same kind of śāli rice grains, accompanied by hot water, without fat and salt or with little fat and salt. So at the fifth and the sixth mealtime; but at the seventh mealtime he should feed him well-boiled rice of the same kind of śāli, measuring two prasṛtas, with hot water as after-drink, with thin mudga soup (yūṣa) furnished with a little fat and salt. So at the eighth and the ninth mealtime; but at the tenth mealtime he should feed him with the meat-juice of any one of lāva, kapiñjala and the like, made with water and salt and not very thick, with hot water as after-drink. So at the eleventh and the twelfth mealtime. From then onward, gradually enjoying the qualities of food, he should come to his normal diet in seven nights.
01.15.17 | Then, having again treated him with oleation and sweating, and having observed him to be of unimpaired mind, to have passed the night comfortably, his food well digested, and to have performed oblation, offering, auspicious rites, muttered prayer and expiation, on a desired lunar day, asterism, karaṇa and muhūrta, having had brahmins recite blessings (svasti), he should make him drink a paste (kalka) of trivṛt, of the measure of one akṣa, prepared with a suitable liquid for stirring, having carefully considered the different states of doṣa, medicine, place, time, strength, body, diet, suitability, mind, constitution and age, and the disorders. And him, properly purged, he should treat by the procedure stated for emesis, smoking excepted, until he regains strength, complexion and his normal state. And having observed him endowed with strength and complexion, of unimpaired mind, having passed the night comfortably, his food well digested, bathed including the head, his limbs anointed, garlanded, clad in unsoiled clothes and adorned with fitting ornaments, having shown him to his friends he should show him to his kinsmen. Then he should release him to his pleasures.
