01.13.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on unctuous substances (sneha).
01.13.2 | Thus said the venerable Ātreya.
01.13.3a | Vahniveśa asked Punarvasu, who was seated with the Sāṃkhyas who had enumerated what is to be enumerated, about his own doubt, for the welfare of the world.
01.13.3b | ~
01.13.4a | Of what sources are the unctuous substances (sneha), how many are they, and what are the qualities of the snehas separately? What are the time and the after-drink (anupāna) of which (sneha), and how many and which are the variant modes of use (vicāraṇā)?
01.13.4b | ~
01.13.5a | How many are the measures (mātrā), of what amount are they, and which is prescribed for whom? Which sneha is beneficial for whom, and what is the utmost limit in oleation (snehana)?
01.13.5b | ~
01.13.6a | Who are to be oleated and who are not, and what are the signs of the properly oleated, the unoleated and the over-oleated? What is wholesome and what unwholesome before drinking, when it has been drunk, and when it is digested?
01.13.6b | ~
01.13.7a | Who have a soft and who a hard (krūra) bowel (koṣṭha)? What are the ill effects (vyāpad) and what are the successes (siddhi)? And what regimen is prescribed in the case of plain sneha and of purifying sneha?
01.13.7b | ~
01.13.8a | For whom are the vicāraṇās to be applied, and by which procedure? O lord, you of boundless knowledge, I wish to know that knowledge of sneha.
01.13.8b | ~
01.13.9a | Then Punarvasu, the cutter of his doubt, replied: The source of the snehas, O gentle one, is twofold: immobile and mobile.
01.13.9b | ~
01.13.10a | Tila, priyāla and abhiṣuka, bibhītaka, citrā, abhayā, eraṇḍa, madhūka, sarṣapa, kusumbha, bilva, āruka, mūlaka, atasī, nikocaka, akṣoḍa, karañja and śigruka,
01.13.10b | ~
01.13.11a | these are the repositories of sneha termed immobile. Likewise the mobile ones would be fish and animals (mṛga), together with birds; of them, curd, milk, ghee, flesh, muscle-fat and marrow are likewise taught among the snehas.
01.13.11b | ~
01.13.12a | Of all kinds of oil, tila oil excels; it is foremost in oleation for strength, but castor oil (airaṇḍa) in purgation. Castor oil is pungent, hot, removes Vāta and Kapha (śleṣman), and is heavy; combined with astringent, sweet and bitter (substances) it destroys Pitta also.
01.13.12b | ~
01.13.12c | ~
01.13.12d | ~
01.13.13a | Ghee, oil, muscle-fat and marrow are considered the best of all snehas; and among these ghee is the best, because it follows the processing (saṃskāra).
01.13.13b | ~
01.13.14a | Ghee removes Pitta and Vāta (anila), is beneficial for rasa, semen (śukra) and ojas (vital essence), is cooling, softening, and clarifies the voice and complexion.
01.13.14b | ~
01.13.15a | Oil destroys Vāta (māruta) and does not increase Kapha (śleṣman); it increases strength, is good for the skin, hot, gives firmness, and purifies the yoni (female genital passage).
01.13.15b | ~
01.13.16a | Muscle-fat (vasā) is prescribed for pierced wounds, fractures, blows, displaced yoni, and pain of ear and head, for increase of virility, in oleation and in exercise.
01.13.16b | ~
01.13.17a | Marrow increases strength, semen (śukra), rasa, Kapha (śleṣman), fat (medas) and marrow; it especially gives strength to the bones and is beneficial in oleation.
01.13.17b | ~
01.13.18a | Ghee is to be drunk in Śarad, muscle-fat and marrow in Mādhava, oil in Prāvṛṭ; a man should drink sneha when it is neither excessively hot nor cold.
01.13.18b | ~
01.13.19a | A man with predominant Vāta and Pitta should drink at night, and also in hot weather; one with predominant Kapha should drink by day, in cold weather, and when the sun is clear.
01.13.19b | ~
01.13.20a | If drunk in excessive heat or by day by one with predominant Vāta and Pitta, it may bring about fainting, thirst, insanity (unmāda) or kāmalā.
01.13.20b | ~
01.13.21a | A man with predominant Kapha, too, who drinks sneha in cold weather or at night incurs distension (ānāha), loss of taste, pain or pallor (pāṇḍutā).
01.13.21b | ~
01.13.22a | With ghee hot water is to be drunk; after oil, yūṣa is recommended; for muscle-fat and marrow it should be maṇḍa; or else, with all of them, hot water.
01.13.22b | ~
01.13.23a | Boiled rice (odana) and vilepī (thick gruel), rasa (juice), meat, milk, curd, gruel (yavāgū), sūpa (pulse-soup) and vegetables, yūṣa, kāmbalika, khaḍa,
01.13.23b | ~
01.13.24a | saktu (roasted-grain flour), tila paste, and wine (madya), and likewise linctuses (leha), eatables (bhakṣya), anointing (abhyañjana), enema (basti) and also uttarabastis,
01.13.24b | ~
01.13.25a | gaṇḍūṣa (liquid held in the mouth), ear-oil, and tarpaṇa through the nose, ears and eyes: thus these are the twenty-four variant modes of use (pravicāraṇā) of sneha.
01.13.25b | ~
01.13.26a | But the sneha which is to be drunk plain (accha) they do not call a vicāraṇā; that, recognised by physicians, is the primary mode (kalpa) of sneha.
01.13.26b | ~
01.13.27a | Sneha combined with the six tastes (rasa), joined collectively and singly, attains a number of sixty-three kinds, and one is plain.
01.13.27b | ~
01.13.28a | Thus these are the sixty-four variant modes of use (pravicāraṇā) of the snehas; they should be applied by one who knows, with regard to habit (okas), season, disease and the person.
01.13.28b | ~
01.13.29a | The major, medium and small measure of sneha waits, for its digestion, a day and night, a whole day, and half a day respectively.
01.13.29b | ~
01.13.30a | Thus three measures of sneha have been set out by quantity; I shall state their administration for each person.
01.13.30b | ~
01.13.31a | Men who are habituated to plentiful sneha, who endure hunger and thirst, whose digestive fire (pāvaka) is of the highest strength, and who are highest in strength,
01.13.31b | ~
01.13.32a | those with gulma, those bitten by snakes, and those who are afflicted by visarpa, the insane, those with difficult urination, and those with hard stools,
01.13.32b | ~
01.13.33a | should drink the highest measure. Hear the benefits of drinking it: properly administered, it quickly pacifies disorders,
01.13.33b | ~
01.13.34a | this measure draws out the doṣas, pervades all the channels, and is strengthening and renewing for body, senses and mind.
01.13.34b | ~
01.13.35a | Those who are afflicted by aruṣkā, blisters (sphoṭa), pustules (piḍakā), itching (kaṇḍū) and pāmā, and those who have kuṣṭha, who urinate profusely (pramīḍha), and who have vātaśoṇita,
01.13.35b | ~
01.13.36a | and those who do not eat very much, and likewise those with a soft bowel (mṛdukoṣṭha), and those who are middling in strength, should drink the medium measure.
01.13.36b | ~
01.13.37a | This measure carries only a slight risk of mishap and does not take away much strength; it oleates easily and is used for the purpose of purifying (śodhana).
01.13.37b | ~
01.13.38a | But those who are old, children, delicate and accustomed to comfort, those for whom an empty bowel is harmful, and those who have weak digestive fire (agni),
01.13.38b | ~
01.13.39a | those whose fever, diarrhoea (atīsāra) and cough are long-standing, and those who are low in strength, should drink the small measure of fat (sneha).
01.13.39b | ~
01.13.40a | This measure is easy as regards restrictions, is oleating and nourishing, aphrodisiac, strengthening and free from trouble, and it also continues for a long time.
01.13.40b | ~
01.13.41a | Those of Vāta or Pitta constitution (prakṛti), those with Vāta and Pitta disorders, those desiring good eyesight, the wounded (kṣata), the wasted (kṣīṇa), the old, children and the weak,
01.13.41b | ~
01.13.42a | those desiring increase of life-span, those seeking strength, complexion and voice, those desiring nourishment, those desiring offspring, and those who seek delicacy of body,
01.13.42b | ~
01.13.43a | those seeking radiance, ojas (vital essence), memory, intelligence (medhā), digestive fire, understanding and strength of the senses, and those afflicted by burning, weapons, poison and fire, should drink ghee.
01.13.43b | ~
01.13.44a | Those whose Kapha (śleṣman) and fat are increased, whose neck and abdomen are flabby and bulky, who are possessed by Vāta diseases, and who are of Vāta constitution,
01.13.44b | ~
01.13.45a | and those embodied beings who desire strength, slimness, lightness, firmness, steadiness of the limbs, and unctuous, smooth and fine skin,
01.13.45b | ~
01.13.46a | those with worms in the bowel, those with a hard bowel (krūrakoṣṭha), those afflicted by nāḍīs, and those who are accustomed to oil, should drink oil in the cold season.
01.13.46b | ~
01.13.47a | Those who endure wind and sun, who are dry, emaciated by carrying loads and by travelling, whose semen and blood are dried up, whose Kapha and fat are used up,
01.13.47b | ~
01.13.48a | those who have great pain in the bones, joints, vessels (sirā), ligaments (snāyu), vital points (marman) and abdomen (koṣṭha), and those whose powerful Vāta (māruta) stays covering the channels (kha),
01.13.48b | ~
01.13.49a | those whose strength of digestive fire is great, and those men to whom muscle-fat is suited (sātmya): for them, when they are to be oleated, drinking of muscle-fat (vasā) is prescribed.
01.13.49b | ~
01.13.50a | Those with kindled digestive fire, who endure hardship, who are voracious, who habitually use fat, who are afflicted by Vāta and who have a hard bowel, being fit to be oleated, should take marrow (majjan).
01.13.50b | ~
01.13.51a | Which fat is beneficial for which persons has been declared. The two utmost limits of oleation are seven nights and three nights.
01.13.51b | ~
01.13.52a | Those who are to be sweated and purified, the dry, those with Vāta disorders, those constantly given to exercise, wine and women, and those who are thinkers should be oleated.
01.13.52b | ~
01.13.53a | Those for whom drying (rūkṣaṇa), apart from purification, will be described: for them, who have abundant Kapha and fat, oleation is not recommended.
01.13.53b | ~
01.13.54a | Those whose mouth and anus are oozing (abhiṣyaṇṇa), those whose digestive fire is constantly weak, those overcome by thirst and fainting, pregnant women, those with dryness of the palate,
01.13.54b | ~
01.13.55a | those averse to food, those vomiting, those afflicted by jaṭhara, āma and gara, the weak, the exhausted, those wearied by fat, and those suffering from intoxication (mada),
01.13.55b | ~
01.13.56a | are not to be oleated, nor while nasal therapy and enema therapy are in progress; from drinking fat, very dreadful diseases arise in them.
01.13.56b | ~
01.13.57a | Knotted, dry stool, Vāta (vāyu) not moving properly, a weak digester, and roughness and dryness of the body: this is the sign of one not oleated.
01.13.57b | ~
01.13.58a | Downward movement of Vāta, kindled digestive fire, unctuous and unformed stool, and softness and unctuousness in the body arise in those who are oleated.
01.13.58b | ~
01.13.59a | Pallor, heaviness, sluggishness, undigested stool, drowsiness, loss of taste and nausea (utkleśa): this is the sign of one over-oleated.
01.13.59b | ~
01.13.60a | By one who wishes to drink fat tomorrow, food that is liquid, warm, not channel-clogging (anabhiṣyandin), not too unctuous and not mixed should be eaten in proper quantity.
01.13.60b | ~
01.13.61a | One should drink the pacifying fat at meal-time, when hungry; but for purification a man should drink it when the night's meal has been digested.
01.13.61b | ~
01.13.62a | He should use warm water, be celibate and sleep at night, and he should not suppress faeces, urine, flatus and belching when they have arisen.
01.13.62b | ~
01.13.63a | He should avoid exercise, loud speaking, anger and grief, cold and sun, and should use a bed and seat sheltered from wind.
01.13.63b | ~
01.13.64a | A man who has drunk fat, and who is indeed partaking of fat in turn: for from wrong management of fat dreadful diseases arise.
01.13.64b | ~
01.13.65a | A man with a soft bowel becomes oleated in three nights by taking fat plain (accha); but one with a hard bowel becomes oleated in seven nights.
01.13.65b | ~
01.13.66a | Jaggery, sugarcane juice, whey (mastu), milk, churned curd, milk-rice (pāyasa), kṛśarā, ghee, the juice of kāśmarya and triphalā,
01.13.66b | ~
01.13.67a | grape (drākṣā) juice, pīlu juice, or hot water, or new wine: having drunk these, one with a soft bowel is purged.
01.13.67b | ~
01.13.68a | These never purge one with a hard bowel; the grahaṇī of one with a hard bowel has excessively strong Vāta (anila).
01.13.68b | ~
01.13.69a | In one with soft bowels (mṛdukoṣṭha) the grahaṇī has excited Pitta, little Kapha and weak Vāta (māruta); therefore that man is regarded as easily purged.
01.13.69b | ~
01.13.70a | He whose grahaṇī has excited Pitta and whose strength of digestive fire (agni) is great: in him the fat (sneha) that is drunk quickly turns to ash by the heat of the fire.
01.13.70b | ~
01.13.71a | That strong fire fed by fat (snehāgni), having consumed that dose of fat and causing ojas (vital essence) to flow away, would bring on extreme thirst with complications.
01.13.71b | ~
01.13.72a | Food, even very heavy, is not sufficient to pacify the (fire) increased by fat; if he does not obtain very cold water, he is burnt, just as a venomous snake lying in the middle of a thicket (is burnt) by the fire of its own venom.
01.13.72b | ~
01.13.72c | ~
01.13.73a | But if thirst arises while the fat is undigested, the physician should make him vomit; then, having drunk cold water and having eaten dry food, (the patient) should vomit.
01.13.73b | ~
01.13.74a | Ghee alone (should) not (be given) in Pitta, especially when it is associated with āma (undigested matter); for it would pervade the whole body and, destroying consciousness, would kill.
01.13.74b | ~
01.13.75a | Drowsiness, distension (ānāha) with nausea (utkleśa), fever, stiffness, loss of consciousness, kuṣṭha diseases, itching, pallor, swelling (śopha), piles (arśas), loss of taste, thirst,
01.13.75b | ~
01.13.76a | abdominal disease (jaṭhara), grahaṇī disorders, sluggish heaviness (staimitya), obstruction of speech, pain (śūla) and disorders of āma arise from the mishandling of sneha.
01.13.76b | ~
01.13.77a | In that case too, vomiting (ullekhana), sweating (sveda), waiting for time, and also laxative purgation (sraṃsana), after judging the strength of the disease in each case, are recommended.
01.13.77b | ~
01.13.78a | The administration of buttermilk (takra) and ariṣṭa, the taking of dry drinks and food, and (the administration) of urines and of triphalā are the medicine for the ill effects of sneha.
01.13.78b | ~
01.13.79a | Sneha used at the wrong time, unwholesome, not applied in (proper) measure, through wrong management, and used in excess, would go wrong (cause ill effects).
01.13.79b | ~
01.13.80a | After oleation (sneha), the person, having paused for three nights, should drink a purgative (praskandana), after eating for three days boiled rice with meat-juice that is liquid, warm and contains fat.
01.13.80b | ~
01.13.81a | Having paused for one day and having eaten likewise, he should drink an emetic. But when the sneha is not meant for purification, the regimen should be as for one who has been purged.
01.13.81b | ~
01.13.82a | Men who dislike fat, who are habituated to fat, who have soft bowels, who cannot bear hardship and who habitually drink wine: for them vicāraṇā (sneha combined with food) is recommended.
01.13.82b | ~
01.13.83a | Meat-juices (rasa) of quail, partridge, peacock, swan, boar, cock, cow, goat, sheep and fish are beneficial in oleation.
01.13.83b | ~
01.13.84a | Yava, kola and kulattha, fats with jaggery (guḍa) and sugar, dāḍima, and curd with vyoṣa: this is the summary of combinations (saṃyoga) with meat-juice.
01.13.84b | ~
01.13.85a | Tila seeds eaten beforehand with fat and phāṇita (treacle) oleate, and so do kṛśarā with much fat and tila-kāmbalikā.
01.13.85b | ~
01.13.86a | A dry person should drink phāṇita, śṛṅgavera and oil with surā (wine); and when it is digested he should eat a meal with prepared meats.
01.13.86b | ~
01.13.87a | A man of Vāta nature (vātika) becomes oleated by drinking oil with the clear upper part (maṇḍa) of surā, or muscle-fat or marrow, or milk with phāṇita.
01.13.87b | ~
01.13.88a | A man becomes oleated by drinking milk warm from the milking, mixed with fat and with sugar, or by drinking the upper layer (sara) of curd with phāṇita.
01.13.88b | ~
01.13.89a | Thin gruel (peyā) of five prasṛtas, and milk-rice (pāyasa) mixed with māṣa, cooked in milk and with much fat, would quickly oleate a man.
01.13.89b | ~
01.13.90a | Thin gruel of five prasṛtas, cooked with prasṛtas of ghee, oil, muscle-fat, marrow and rice, is to be drunk by one who desires oleation. Or unctuous pork meat-juice combined with ghee and salt, drunk carefully twice in a day, would quickly oleate a man.
01.13.90b | ~
01.13.90c | ~
01.13.90d | ~
01.13.91a | One with kuṣṭha, with swelling (śotha) or with prameha should not use, in oleation, the meat of domestic, marsh and aquatic animals, jaggery (guḍa), curd, milk or tila seeds.
01.13.91b | ~
01.13.92a | He should oleate them, as appropriate, with fats that cause no disorder, prepared with pippalī and harītakī, or also prepared with triphalā.
01.13.92b | ~
01.13.93a | The physician should prepare fat with vyoṣa inside it, with the juices (yūṣa) of drākṣā and āmalaka and with sour curd; drinking it, a man becomes oleated.
01.13.93b | ~
01.13.94a | The juices of yava, kola and kulattha, alkali (kṣāra), surā, curd, and milk and ghee: that (ghee) prepared (with these) is the best ghee for oleation.
01.13.94b | ~
01.13.95a | In disorders of the yoni (female genital tract) and of semen, one should prepare oil, marrow, muscle-fat and ghee with the juices of badara and triphalā, and use (them).
01.13.95b | ~
01.13.96a | As cloth takes in water and lets the excess flow out, so fat is digested in proportion to the digestive fire, and the excess flows out.
01.13.96b | ~
01.13.97a | Or, as water poured hastily on a lump of clay without moistening it runs off, so fat taken hastily slips out.
01.13.97b | ~
01.13.98a | Fats combined with salt oleate a man quickly, for it (salt) is abhiṣyandin (causing moistening), not dry, subtle, hot and vyavāyin (spreading through the body).
01.13.98b | ~
01.13.99a | One should apply oleation (sneha) first, then, immediately after, sweating (sveda); for one who has undergone oleation and sweating, purification (saṃśodhana), then the other (measure).
01.13.99b | ~
01.13.P1 | Here, the verse:
01.13.100a | The fats, the method of oleation, and the complete success (siddhi) over ill effects together with medicines: (all this) was declared, according to the questions, by the venerable Cāndrabhāgi.
01.13.100b | ~
01.13.P2 | Thus, in the Śloka section (Ślokasthāna) of the treatise composed by Agniveśa and redacted by Caraka, the thirteenth chapter, named Snehādhyāya (Chapter on Sneha).
