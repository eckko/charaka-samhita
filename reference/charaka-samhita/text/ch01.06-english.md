01.06.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter called "Tasyāśitīya".
01.06.2 | Thus spoke the venerable Ātreya.
01.06.3a | The strength and complexion of him who knows the seasonal suitability (ṛtusātmya) that rests on conduct and diet increase from food eaten and the rest.
01.06.3b | ~
01.06.4 | Here one should know the year as having six parts by its division into seasons.
01.06.P1 | Of these, one should determine the northward course of the sun and ādāna (taking up) to be the three seasons beginning with Śiśira and ending with Grīṣma; and, again, those beginning with Varṣā and ending with Hemanta to be the southward course and visarga (release).
01.06.5 | In visarga the winds blow not too dry; in ādāna, however, the others. And the moon, its strength unimpaired, filling the world with its cool rays, nourishes it continually; hence visarga is saumya (of the moon's nature).
01.06.P2 | Ādāna, however, is āgneya (of fire's nature). These two, the sun and the wind, and the moon, governed by time, their own nature and their course, are taught to be the causes of the production of time, season, taste (rasa), doṣa and bodily strength.
01.06.6 | There, the sun, taking up with its rays the moisture (sneha) of the world, and the winds, sharp and dry, drying it up, producing dryness in succession in Śiśira, Vasanta and Grīṣma and increasing the dry tastes, bitter, astringent and pungent, bring weakness to humans.
01.06.7 | But in Varṣā, Śarad and Hemanta, when the sun faces the south and its heat is struck down by time, its course, clouds, wind and rain, when the moon is of unimpaired strength, and when the heat of the world is calmed by the water of Mahendra, the non-dry tastes, sour, salty and sweet, increase in succession; then the strength of humans grows.
01.06.8a | And here there is: One should declare weakness of humans at the beginning and the end of visarga and ādāna, middling strength in the middle, and the best at the end and at the start.

01.06.9a | In the cold Hemanta, the digester, confined by the touch of cold wind, becomes strong in the strong, able to bear what is heavy in quantity and in substance.
01.06.9b | ~
01.06.10a | When it does not get suitable fuel, it then harms the body-born rasa; therefore Vāta (vāyu), being cold, is aggravated in the cold.
01.06.10b | ~
01.06.11a | Therefore, in the season of frost, one should use the unctuous, sour and salty rasas of the fat meats of aquatic and marsh animals.
01.06.11b | ~
01.06.12a | A man should eat the meats of burrow-dwellers and the roasted (meats) of prasaha animals, and drink after them madirā, śīdhu and madhu.
01.06.12b | ~
01.06.13a | The life-span of one who regularly takes, in Hemanta, milk products, sugarcane preparations, muscle-fat (vasā), oil, new cooked rice and warm water does not diminish.
01.06.13b | ~
01.06.14a | One should resort to oil massage (abhyaṅga), rubbing with unguents (utsādana), oil on the head, the jentāka (sweat-chamber), sunshine, and a warm underground house, and likewise a warm inner chamber.
01.06.14b | ~
01.06.15a | In the cold seasons, a covered vehicle, bed and seat should be used, spread with cloaks (prāvāra), hides (ajina), silk (kauṣeya), praveṇī and kuthaka.
01.06.15b | ~
01.06.16a | Wearing heavy, warm clothes, his limbs always smeared with heavy aguru, in bed, a plump young woman with broad, full breasts
01.06.16b | ~
01.06.17a | ...embracing her, whose limbs are smeared with aguru, he should sleep, full of intoxication and passion; and at the coming of the cold (śiśira) he should engage freely in sexual intercourse.
01.06.17b | ~
01.06.18a | At the coming of the cold (hima) one should avoid foods and drinks that are Vāta-increasing and light, strong wind, a scanty diet, and udamantha.
01.06.18b | ~
01.06.19a | Hemanta and Śiśira are alike; in Śiśira there is a slight difference: dryness born of ādāna, and cold born of clouds, wind and rain.
01.06.19b | ~
01.06.20a | Therefore the whole regimen of Hemanta is prescribed in Śiśira; but in Śiśira one should resort to a house that is windless and warmer.
01.06.20b | ~
01.06.21a | In Śiśira one should avoid foods and drinks that are pungent, bitter and astringent, Vāta-increasing, light and cold.
01.06.21b | ~
01.06.22a | In Vasanta, the accumulated Kapha (śleṣman), stirred by the rays of the day-maker, impairs the digestive fire of the body (kāyāgni) and then produces many diseases.
01.06.22b | ~
01.06.23a | Therefore in Vasanta one should have the procedures beginning with emesis (vamana) performed, and avoid what is heavy, sour, unctuous and sweet, and day-sleep.
01.06.23b | ~
01.06.24a | At the coming of flowers one should practise exercise, rubbing (udvartana), medicated smoking (dhūma), holding a mouthful moved in the mouth (kavalagraha), collyrium (añjana), and the practice of cleansing with pleasant water.
01.06.24b | ~
01.06.25a | With limbs smeared with sandal and aguru, eating barley and wheat, ... the meat of the śarabha, the hare, the eṇa deer, and of lāva and kapiñjala birds
01.06.25b | ~
01.06.26a | ...he should eat; he should drink harmless sīdhu, or just mādhvīka; in Vasanta he should enjoy the youth of women and of forests.
01.06.26b | ~
01.06.27a | In Grīṣma the sun drinks up again and again with its rays the moisture of the world; then food and drink that is sweet, cold, liquid and unctuous is wholesome.
01.06.27b | ~
01.06.28a | Taking cold mantha with sugar, animals and birds of dry land (jāṅgala), ghee, and milk with śāli rice, one does not suffer in Grīṣma.
01.06.28b | ~
01.06.29a | Liquor should be drunk a little, or not at all, or else with very much water; one should avoid salty, sour, pungent and hot things, and exercise.
01.06.29b | ~
01.06.30a | By day one should take sleep in a cool house; at night, attended by things cool with the moon's rays, adorned with pearls and gems, one should resort to sitting at rest (āsyā).
01.06.30b | ~
01.06.31a | Attended with fans and touches of hands, cool with sandal-water, adorned with pearls and gems, one should resort to sitting at rest (āsyā).
01.06.31b | ~
01.06.32a | In the season of Grīṣma a man, abstaining from sexual intercourse, should enjoy cool forests, waters and flowers.
01.06.32b | ~
01.06.33a | When the body is weakened by ādāna, the digester becomes weak; in the rains it is further impaired by the vitiations of Vāta (anila) and the others.
01.06.33b | ~
01.06.34a | From the vapour of the earth, the downpour of the clouds and the sour transformation (pāka) of water, in the rains, when the strength of the digestive fire (agni) is diminished, Vāta (pavana) and the others are aggravated.
01.06.34b | ~
01.06.35a | Therefore in the rains the whole general regimen is commended. Udamantha, day-sleep, dew, river water,
01.06.35b | ~
01.06.36a | ...exercise, sunshine and sexual intercourse one should avoid here; one should take drinks, foods and preparations mostly mixed with honey (kṣaudra).
01.06.36b | ~
01.06.37a | In the rains, on a day troubled by wind and rain and especially cold, food with distinct sour, salt and fat should be eaten, for pacifying Vāta (anila).
01.06.37b | ~
01.06.38a | By one intent on protecting the digestive fire (agni), old barley, wheat and śāli rice should be eaten with the meats of dry-land animals and with well-prepared soups (yūṣa).
01.06.38b | ~
01.06.39a | One should drink a little mādhvīka and ariṣṭa mixed with honey, or water: māhendra water boiled and cooled, or well water, or lake water.
01.06.39b | ~
01.06.40a | One should be devoted to friction (pragharṣa), rubbing (udvartana), bathing, perfumes and garlands; wearing light, clean clothes, one should resort to a dwelling for the rainy season, free from dampness.
01.06.40b | ~
01.06.41a | In those whose limbs are used to the cold of the rains and who are suddenly heated by the sun's rays, the accumulated Pitta is generally aggravated in Śarad.
01.06.41b | ~
01.06.42a | Then food and drink that is sweet, light, cold, with bitter, and pacifying Pitta, should be taken in due measure by those with keen desire for it.
01.06.42b | ~
01.06.43a | At the passing of the clouds, they say, lāva and kapiñjala birds, eṇa deer, rams (urabhra), śarabhas, hares, and śāli rice with barley and wheat are to be taken.
01.06.43b | ~
01.06.44a | At the passing of the rain-bearers, drinking of bitter ghee, purgation (vireka), blood-letting and avoidance of sunshine should be done.
01.06.44b | ~
01.06.45a | Here one should avoid muscle-fat (vasā), oil, dew, the meat of aquatic and marsh animals, alkali (kṣāra), curd, day-sleep and the east wind.
01.06.45b | ~
01.06.46a | Heated by the sun's rays by day, cooled by the moon's rays at night, matured by time, free of faults, made free of poison by Agastya,
01.06.46b | ~
01.06.47a | ...the autumnal water, clear and pure, known as "haṃsodaka", is wholesome like amṛta for bathing, drinking and immersion.
01.06.47b | ~
01.06.48a | In the season of Śarad, autumnal garlands and clean clothes are commended, and in the early night the rays of the moon.
01.06.48b | ~
01.06.49a | Thus has been stated the seasonal suitability (ṛtusātmya) which rests on conduct and diet. What suits through habituation is called okaḥsātmya (suitability by habit).
01.06.49b | ~
01.06.50a | Those who know suitability regard as suitable (sātmya) both conduct and food that are opposite in qualities to the qualities of regions and diseases.
01.06.50b | ~
01.06.51a | Here, the verse: Whatever is to be used and not to be used by humans in each season, with its reasons, and also suitability (sātmya), has been set out in the Tasyāśitīya.
