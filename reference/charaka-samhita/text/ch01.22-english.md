01.22.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter named Laṅghanabṛṃhaṇīya (on lightening and nourishing).
01.22.2 | Thus indeed spoke the venerable Ātreya.
01.22.3a | Urging them on, Ātreya spoke to his six best disciples, headed by Agniveśa, who were devoted to austerity and self-study:
01.22.3b | ~
01.22.4a | He who knows lightening (laṅghana), nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa), drying (rūkṣaṇa), oleation (snehana), and also sweating (svedana) and checking (stambhana), at the proper time, he truly is a physician.
01.22.4b | ~
01.22.5 | To Ātreya, who had spoken, Agniveśa said:
01.22.6a | O venerable one, what is lightening, and of what kind are those to be lightened? (What are) nourishing and those to be nourished, those to be dried and drying?
01.22.6b | ~
01.22.7a | Which are the unctuous substances and who are those to be oleated; which are considered the sweating procedures and who are those to be sweated; checking and those to be checked — that you should tell, O teacher!
01.22.7b | ~
01.22.8a | And you should tell in brief the sign of these six, beginning with lightening, when done, not done and overdone.
01.22.8b | ~
01.22.9a | Having heard those words of Agniveśa, the teacher said: Whatever produces lightness in the body is known as lightening (laṅghana).
01.22.9b | ~
01.22.10a | And that which would produce largeness of the body is nourishing (bṛṃhaṇa); that which would cause dryness, roughness and non-sliminess is indeed drying (rūkṣaṇa).
01.22.10b | ~
01.22.11a | Oleation (snehana) is what produces unctuousness, liquefaction (viṣyanda), softness and moisture; sweating (svedana) is what removes stiffness, heaviness and cold and produces sweat.
01.22.11b | ~
01.22.12a | Checking (stambhana) is that which certainly arrests what is moving and mobile. A substance that is light (laghu), hot, sharp, non-slimy, dry, subtle, rough, flowing (sara),
01.22.12b | ~
01.22.13a | and hard, is generally known as lightening. A substance that is heavy (guru), cold, soft, unctuous, thick, gross, slimy,
01.22.13b | ~
01.22.14a | mostly slow, stable and smooth — such a substance is called nourishing. A substance that is dry, light, rough, sharp, hot, stable, non-slimy,
01.22.14b | ~
01.22.15a | and mostly hard — that substance is indeed drying. A substance that is liquid, subtle, flowing, unctuous, slimy, heavy, cold, mostly slow and soft is considered oleating (snehana).
01.22.15b | ~
01.22.15c | ~
01.22.16a | A substance that is mostly hot, sharp, flowing, unctuous, dry, subtle, liquid, stable and heavy is indeed called sweating (svedana).
01.22.16b | ~
01.22.17a | A substance described as cold, slow, soft, smooth, dry, subtle, liquid, stable and light is generally known as checking (stambhana).
01.22.17b | ~
01.22.18a | Purification of four kinds, thirst, wind and sun, digestives (pācana), fasting and exercise — this is lightening.
01.22.18b | ~
01.22.19a | Those who have abundant Kapha (śleṣman), Pitta, blood and waste, whose Vāta (māruta) is combined (with these), who are large-bodied and strong, are to be lightened by purifications.
01.22.19b | ~
01.22.20a | Those whose diseases arisen from Kapha and Pitta are of medium strength — vomiting, diarrhoea (atīsāra), heart disease, visūcī, alasaka, fever,
01.22.20b | ~
01.22.21a | obstruction (vibandha), heaviness, belching, nausea (hṛllāsa), loss of taste (arocaka) and the like — them the wise physician should generally treat at first with digestives (pācana).
01.22.21b | ~
01.22.22a | Those in whom these very diseases, as described, are of slight strength — their diseases one should overcome by suppression of thirst and by fasts.
01.22.22b | ~
01.22.23a | One should overcome diseases of medium strength by exercise, sun and wind; what then (need be said) of the strong whose diseases are of low strength?
01.22.23b | ~
01.22.24a | For those with skin disorders, those who have urinated much (pramīḍha), those taking unctuous, channel-clogging (abhiṣyandin) and nourishing (things), lightening is commended in Śiśira — even for those with Vāta disorders.
01.22.24b | ~
01.22.25a | The meat of wild animals (mṛga), fish and birds that move in their suitable (habitat) — shot with a weapon that is not smeared, not exhausted, and of the right age — is called nourishing.
01.22.25b | ~
01.22.26a | The depleted, the injured, the emaciated, the old, the weak, those constantly travelling, and those constantly given to women and wine are known as men to be nourished, and in Grīṣma.
01.22.26b | ~
01.22.27a | And for those who are emaciated by the diseases śoṣa (wasting), arśas and grahaṇī disorder, light juices of the flesh of carnivorous animals are nourishing.
01.22.27b | ~
01.22.28a | Bathing, rubbing with unguents (utsādana), sleep, sweet unctuous enemas, sugar, milk and ghee — know (these) as nourishing for all.
01.22.28b | ~
01.22.29a | Use of pungent, bitter and astringent (tastes), lack of restraint with women, and (use) of khali, oil-cake (piṇyāka), buttermilk, honey and the like is drying.
01.22.29b | ~
01.22.30a | Great doṣas that are clogged (abhiṣyaṇṇa), and those diseases located in the vital points, beginning with ūrustambha (stiffness of the thighs), are indicated as to be dried.
01.22.30b | ~
01.22.31a | The unctuous substances and the men to be oleated, the sweating procedures and the men to be sweated — these have been stated by me in detail in the chapter on unctuous substances and in the one named for sweating.
01.22.31b | ~
01.22.32a | Whatever medicine is liquid, thin, non-flowing, cooling, sweet, bitter and astringent — all that indeed is checking.
01.22.32b | ~
01.22.33a | Those burnt by Pitta, alkali or fire, those afflicted with vomiting and diarrhoea, and those suffering from poison or from excessive sweating are indicated as to be checked.
01.22.33b | ~
01.22.34a | When there is release of wind, urine and faeces, lightness of the limbs, cleanness of the heart, belching, throat and mouth, when drowsiness and exhaustion have gone,
01.22.34b | ~
01.22.35a | when sweat has arisen, and relish (for food), when hunger and thirst arise together, and when the inner self is free from distress, lightening should be declared (properly) done.
01.22.35b | ~
01.22.36a | Breaking pain in the joints, body-ache (aṅgamarda), cough, dryness of the mouth, loss of hunger, loss of taste (aruci), thirst, weakness of the ears and eyes,
01.22.36b | ~
01.22.37a | frequent confusion of the mind, upward wind (ūrdhvavāta), darkness in the heart, and loss of body, digestive fire (agni) and strength would occur when lightening is overdone.
01.22.37b | ~
01.22.38a | Strength, gaining of plumpness and freedom from the defects of emaciation are the sign in one (properly) nourished; and excessive corpulence (sthaulya) in one excessively nourished.
01.22.38b | ~
01.22.39a | The sign of (properly) done and overdone that holds in one lightened holds indeed in one dried. One would be (properly) checked when strength has been gained and the diseases stated have been overcome.
01.22.39b | ~
01.22.40a | Duskiness, stiffness of the limbs, agitation, hanusaṃgraha, and binding of the heart and of the faeces would be the sign of one over-checked.
01.22.40b | ~
01.22.41a | And in brief, the sign of these six when not done would be the non-subsidence, and indeed the increase, of the dhātus for which these are the remedies.
01.22.41b | ~
01.22.42a | Thus the six measures of treatment (upakrama) for all diseases have been properly declared; conforming to measure (mātrā) and time, they are effective in curing the curable.
01.22.42b | ~
01.22.43a | And here there is: Because of the manifold combinations of the doṣas the measures of treatment become mixed; but they do not go beyond six, just as Vāta and the others do not go beyond three.
01.22.43b | ~
01.22.43c | ~
01.22.44a | Here, the verses: Thus in this chapter on lightening the venerable one has expounded, according to the questions, the six measures of treatment by which treatment proceeds.
01.22.44b | ~
01.22.44c | ~
01.22.P1 | Thus, in the treatise composed by Agniveśa and redacted by Caraka, in the Śloka section (Ślokasthāna), the twenty-second chapter named Laṅghanabṛṃhaṇīya.
