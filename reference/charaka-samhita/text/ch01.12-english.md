01.12.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on the aspects and non-aspects of Vāta (Vātakalākalīya).
01.12.2 | Thus indeed spoke the venerable Ātreya.
01.12.3 | Concerning the knowledge of the aspects and non-aspects of Vāta, the great sages, wishing to know one another's views, sat together and asked one another: Of what qualities is Vāta (vāyu)? What aggravates it, or what pacifies it? And how do the aggravating and pacifying factors aggravate or pacify it, which is non-compact and unstable, without reaching it? And what are the actions of it, aggravated or unaggravated, moving in the body and outside the body, as it moves in bodies or outside bodies?
01.12.4 | On this Kuśa Sāṅkṛtyāyana said: Dry, light, cold, harsh, rough and non-slimy (viśada): these six are the qualities of Vāta.
01.12.5 | Having heard that statement, Kumāraśiras Bharadvāja said: It is so, as the venerable one has said; these indeed are the qualities of Vāta. But that Vāta (vāyu) becomes aggravated by actions of such qualities, such substances and such effects (prabhāva) when they are repeatedly practised; for repeated use of similar qualities is the cause of increase of the dhātus.
01.12.6 | Having heard that statement, Kāṅkāyana, the physician from Bāhlīka, said: It is so, as the venerable one has said; these indeed are the aggravators of Vāta, and their opposites are the pacifiers of Vāta; for the reverse of the aggravators is the cause of pacification of the dhātus.
01.12.7 | Having heard that statement, Baḍiśa Dhāmārgava said: It is so, as the venerable one has said; these indeed are the aggravators and pacifiers of Vāta.
01.12.P1 | How the aggravating and pacifying factors aggravate or pacify it, which is non-compact and unstable, without reaching it, we shall now explain. The aggravators of Vāta produce in bodies dryness, lightness, cold, harshness, roughness, non-sliminess and porosity; in bodies of that kind Vāta (vāyu), having found a lodging and growing, becomes aggravated. The pacifiers of Vāta, on the other hand, produce in bodies unctuousness, heaviness, heat, smoothness, softness, sliminess and compactness; in bodies of that kind Vāta, moving without getting attached, becomes calm.
01.12.8 | Having heard that true statement of Baḍiśa, approved by the groups of sages, the royal sage Vāryovida said: All this is so, beyond reproach, as the venerable one has said.
01.12.P2 | Now the actions of Vāta (vāyu), aggravated or unaggravated, moving in the body and outside it, which take place as it moves in bodies or outside bodies: having established their details by perception, inference and instruction, and having bowed to Vāyu, we shall declare them as far as we are able. Vāta, when unaggravated, upholds the frame and mechanism (of the body); it has prāṇa, udāna, samāna, vyāna and apāna as its self; it initiates movements of all kinds; it controls and directs the mind; it stimulates all the senses; it conveys all sense-objects; it arranges all the dhātus of the body; it holds the body together; it prompts speech; it is the source of touch and sound, the root of hearing and touch, the origin of joy and enthusiasm; it kindles the digestive fire (agni); it dries up the doṣas; it expels wastes (mala) outward; it opens the large and minute channels; it makes the forms of the fetus; and it is the basis of the continuance of life.
01.12.P3 | But when aggravated in the body, it afflicts the body with disorders of various kinds, to the impairment of strength, complexion, happiness and life; it agitates the mind, impairs all the senses, destroys fetuses, brings them to deformity or retains them beyond the due time; it produces fear, grief, confusion, dejection and excessive talk; and it obstructs the vital breaths (prāṇa).
01.12.P4 | The actions of it in its normal state, moving in the world, are these: upholding the earth, kindling fire, regulating the continuous course of the hosts of the sun, moon, stars and planets, creation of clouds, release of waters, setting streams in flow, producing flowers and fruits, sprouting of plants, division of the seasons, division of the elements (dhātu), manifestation of the measure and shape of the elements, preparation of seeds, growth of crops, freedom from moistness and drying, and change that is not abnormal.
01.12.P5 | The actions of it when aggravated, moving in the worlds, are these: crushing the peaks of mountains, uprooting trees, making the oceans surge, making lakes overflow, making rivers flow back, shaking the earth, blowing the clouds about, showering mist, thunder, dust, sand, fish, frogs, snakes, alkali, blood, stones and thunderbolts, deranging the six seasons, failure of crops to form, affliction of beings, bringing things to non-existence, and releasing the clouds, suns, fires and winds that bring the end of the four ages. For he is the Lord, the origin and the imperishable, the cause of the existence and non-existence of beings, the dispenser of happiness and unhappiness; he is Death, Yama, the controller, Prajāpati, Aditi, Viśvakarman, the one of all forms, the all-pervading, the ordainer of all systems, the subtle among things, the mighty one, Viṣṇu, the strider; wind (vāyu) itself is the Lord of the worlds.
01.12.9 | Having heard Vāryovida's statement, Marīci said: Even if this is so, what use is there for the science of medicine in stating or knowing this matter? This discussion was begun concerning the science of medicine.
01.12.10 | Vāryovida said: If the physician were not to study closely Vāta (pavana), which is very strong, very harsh, acting very fast and dangerous, how could he, unhurried and very careful, give instruction in advance to protect (the patient) from it when aggravated, beforehand, out of fear of mortal danger? Even a true praise of Vāta (vāyu) serves for health, for the increase of strength and complexion, for lustre, for bodily growth, for the attainment of knowledge and for the greatest extension of life.
01.12.11 | Marīci said: It is fire (agni) alone, situated within Pitta in the body, that, aggravated or unaggravated, produces good and bad (effects), such as digestion and indigestion, vision and loss of vision, normal and abnormal measure of heat, normal and abnormal complexion, courage and fear, anger and joy, confusion and clarity, and other such pairs.
01.12.12 | Having heard Marīci's statement, Kāpya said: It is soma alone, situated within Kapha (śleṣman) in the body, that, aggravated or unaggravated, produces good and bad (effects), such as firmness and looseness, bulk and leanness, enthusiasm and laziness, virility and impotence, knowledge and ignorance, understanding and confusion, and other such pairs.
01.12.13 | Having heard Kāpya's statement, the venerable Punarvasu Ātreya said: All of you have spoken rightly, except for the one-sided statement. Surely all of them, Vāta, Pitta and Kapha (śleṣman), in the normal state, endow a person who has unimpaired senses and is endowed with strength, complexion and happiness with a long life, just as dharma, wealth and pleasure, properly practised, endow a person with the highest good in this world and in the other; but when deranged they endow him with the great reverse; the three seasons indeed, fallen into derangement, (endow) the world with misfortune at a time of destruction.
01.12.14 | All the sages approved that statement of the venerable Ātreya and welcomed it.
