01.18.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on the three swellings (Triśothīya).
01.18.2 | Thus said the venerable Ātreya.
01.18.3 | There are three swellings (śotha), caused by Vāta, Pitta and Kapha (śleṣman); they, again, are of two kinds by the division into endogenous (nija) and exogenous (āgantu).
01.18.4 | Among these, the exogenous swellings arise from cutting, splitting, wounding, breaking, crushing, pounding, striking, beating (vadha), binding, wrapping, piercing, pressing and the like; or from contact with the flower, fruit and juice of bhallātaka, the bristles of ātmaguptā, bristly worms (krimiśūka), and harmful leaves, creepers and shrubs; or from the sweat, crawling over and urinating of poisonous creatures; or from the strokes of the fangs, teeth, horns and nails of venomous creatures; or from contact with sea-poison, wind, frost and fire.
01.18.5 | They, again, are recognised at the outset by their respective causes and signs, which differ in part from the signs of the endogenous [swellings]; and, being treated with measures such as bandaging, incantations, antidotes (agada), smearing (pralepa), heating and cooling (nirvāpaṇa), they subside.
01.18.6 | The endogenous ones, again, arise from improper administration of oleation, sweating, emesis, purgation, decoction enema, unctuous enema and head-evacuation, or from a faulty graded diet (saṃsarjana); or from excessive emaciation due to the disorders vomiting, alasaka, visūcikā, dyspnoea, cough, diarrhoea, wasting (śoṣa), pāṇḍu disease, udara, fever, pradara, bhagandara and arśas; or from kuṣṭha, itching, pimples (piḍakā) and the like; or from suppression of the urges of vomiting, sneezing, belching, semen, flatus, urine and faeces; or, in one emaciated by action (karma), disease, fasting or travel, from the sudden excessive use of heavy, sour and salty things, flour-foods, fruits, vegetables, rāga, curd, green herbs, wine, mandaka, sprouted grain, new awned grain and pod grain, and the flesh of marsh and aquatic animals; from eating clay, mud or clods; from eating too much salt; from pressure of the foetus; from the falling of an unformed foetus; and, in women who have given birth, from wrong care and from the aggravated state of the doṣas. Thus the general cause has been stated.
01.18.7-1 | But here is the specific feature: Vāta (vāyu), aggravated by cold, dry, light and non-slimy things, fatigue, fasting, excessive reduction, depletion and the like, overpowers the skin, flesh, blood and the rest and produces a swelling. It is quick to arise and quick to subside; it is dark or reddish in colour, or of natural colour; mobile, throbbing, with rough, harsh and split skin and hair; it is as if cut, as if split, as if pressed, as if pricked with needles, as if crept over by ants; as if smeared with mustard paste it tingles; it contracts and is as if stretched: this is the Vāta swelling.
01.18.7-2 | Pitta, aggravated by hot, sharp, pungent, alkaline, salty and sour foods and by eating during indigestion, and by the heat of fire and sun, overpowers the skin, flesh and blood and produces a swelling. It is quick to arise and quick to subside; it has a black, yellow, blue or coppery hue, is hot and soft, with tawny or coppery hair; it burns, smarts, feels as if fumigated, gives off heat, sweats and becomes moist, and does not tolerate touch or heat: this is the Pitta swelling.
01.18.7-3 | Kapha (śleṣman), aggravated by heavy, sweet, cold and unctuous things and by excessive sleep, lack of exercise and the like, overpowers the skin, flesh, blood and the rest and produces a swelling. It arises and subsides with difficulty; it has a pale or white hue, is heavy, unctuous, smooth, firm and dense, with white-tipped hair, and tolerates touch and heat: this is the Kapha swelling.
01.18.7 | From the combination of their respective causes and forms, there are three swellings born of two doṣas; from the coming together of all their respective causes and forms, there is one sānnipātika [swelling] (from all three doṣas together); thus the division is sevenfold.
01.18.8 | Being divided according to these and those basic factors (prakṛti), swelling is found to be twofold, threefold, fourfold, sevenfold and eightfold; and again it is one alone, on account of the common feature of elevation (utsedha).
