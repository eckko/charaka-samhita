01.01.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter called "Dīrghaṃjīvitīya" (On Long Life).
01.01.2 | Thus spoke the venerable Ātreya.
01.01.3a | Seeking long life, Bharadvāja, of fierce austerity, approached Indra, knowing him to be the lord of the immortals who gives refuge.
01.01.3b | ~
01.01.4a | For Prajāpati first received Āyurveda (the science of life) in full, just as Brahmā had declared it; and from him, in turn, the two Aśvins (received it).
01.01.4b | ~
01.01.5a | From the Aśvins the lord Śakra (Indra) received it in its entirety; therefore Bharadvāja, urged by the sages, approached Śakra.
01.01.5b | ~
01.01.6a | When diseases arose among embodied beings, becoming obstacles to their austerity, fasting, study, celibacy, vows and life,
01.01.6b | ~
01.01.7a | then the great sages of virtuous deeds, putting compassion for living beings first, assembled on an auspicious slope of the Himālaya.
01.01.7b | ~
01.01.8a | Aṅgiras, Jamadagni, Vasiṣṭha, Kaśyapa, Bhṛgu, Ātreya, Gautama, Sāṅkhya, Pulastya, Nārada, Asita,
01.01.8b | ~
01.01.9a | Agastya, Vāmadeva, Mārkaṇḍeya and Āśvalāyana, Pārikṣi, Bhikṣu Ātreya, Bharadvāja, Kapiñjala,
01.01.9b | ~
01.01.10a | Viśvāmitra and Āśmarathya, Cyavana the Bhārgava, Abhijit, Gārgya, Śāṇḍilya and Kauṇḍilya, Vārkṣi, Devala and Gālava,
01.01.10b | ~
01.01.11a | Sāṅkṛtya, Baijavāpi, Kuśika, Bādarāyaṇa, Baḍiśa, Śaraloman, and both Kāpya and Kātyāyana,
01.01.11b | ~
01.01.12a | Kāṅkāyana, Kaikaśeya, Dhaumya, Mārīca and Kāśyapa, Śarkarākṣa, Hiraṇyākṣa, Lokākṣa, and Paiṅgi too,
01.01.12b | ~
01.01.13a | Śaunaka, Śākuneya, Maitreya, Maimatāyani, the Vaikhānasas, the Vālakhilyas, and likewise other great sages,
01.01.13b | ~
01.01.14a | treasuries of the knowledge of brahman, of self-control and of discipline, blazing with the radiance of austerity like fires receiving oblations,
01.01.14b | ~
01.01.15a | Seated at ease there, they held this sacred discussion: "Health is the supreme root of dharma, wealth (artha), pleasure (kāma) and liberation (mokṣa).
01.01.15b | ~
01.01.16a | Diseases are the destroyers of this (health), of well-being and of life. This great obstacle has arisen for human beings.
01.01.16b | ~
01.01.17a | What could be the means of allaying them?" Having said this, they entered into meditation; then, with the eye of meditation, they saw Śakra as their refuge.
01.01.17b | ~
01.01.18a | "He, the lord of the immortals, will properly tell the means of allaying them. Who should go to the abode of the thousand-eyed one to ask the husband of Śacī?"
01.01.18b | ~
01.01.19a | "Let me be appointed for this task," was the first word, spoken by Bharadvāja; therefore he was appointed by the sages.
01.01.19b | ~
01.01.20a | Having gone to Śakra's abode, he saw the slayer of Bala, in the midst of the hosts of gods and sages, blazing like fire.
01.01.20b | ~
01.01.21a | He approached, greeted the lord of the gods with blessings of victory, and, being wise, humbly delivered the sages' excellent message:
01.01.21b | ~
01.01.22a | "Diseases have arisen that terrify all living beings; so tell me properly the means of allaying them, O lord of the immortals."
01.01.22b | ~
01.01.23a | To him, the supreme sage, the lord Śatakratu (Indra), perceiving his vast intellect, taught Āyurveda in few words.
01.01.23b | ~
01.01.24a | It is the knowledge of cause (hetu), sign (liṅga) and remedy (auṣadha), the support of both the healthy and the sick, the eternal and sacred threefold teaching (trisūtra), which the Grandsire (Brahmā) had understood.
01.01.24b | ~
01.01.25a | That sage of great intellect, his mind intent on it, soon understood properly the whole of Āyurveda, limitless in extent and having three branches (triskandha).
01.01.25b | ~
01.01.26a | Through it Bharadvāja obtained a boundless life filled with happiness; and he taught it to the sages, adding nothing and leaving nothing out.
01.01.26b | ~
01.01.27a | And the sages, wishing to secure long life, received from Bharadvāja that science (veda), beneficial to creatures and the promoter of life.
01.01.27b | ~
01.01.28a | Those great sages saw properly, with the eye of knowledge, the general (sāmānya) and the particular (viśeṣa), qualities (guṇa), substances (dravya) and action (karma),
01.01.28b | ~
01.01.29a | and inherence (samavāya); knowing these and following the regimen stated in the treatise, they obtained the highest well-being and a life that does not soon pass away.
01.01.29b | ~
01.01.30a | Then Punarvasu, devoted to friendliness, out of compassion for all beings gave the sacred Āyurveda to six disciples.
01.01.30b | ~
01.01.31a | Agniveśa, Bhela, Jatūkarṇa, Parāśara, Hārīta and Kṣārapāṇi received the sage's teaching.
01.01.31b | ~
01.01.32a | The difference among them lay in their intellect, not in any difference in the sage's teaching; that is why Agniveśa became the first author of a treatise (tantra).
01.01.32b | ~
01.01.33a | Then Bhela and the others each composed his own treatise, and these intelligent ones recited their compositions to Ātreya together with the assembly of sages.
01.01.33b | ~
01.01.34a | Hearing the arrangement of the topics in sūtras by those of virtuous deeds, the sages were delighted and approved of them, saying, "It is properly set out in sūtras."
01.01.34b | ~
01.01.35a | All of them praised those well-wishers of all beings, and cried aloud together, "Well done, compassion for living beings!"
01.01.35b | ~
01.01.36a | The divine sages abiding in heaven, together with the gods, heard that holy cry of the supreme sages, and hearing it they rejoiced greatly.
01.01.36b | ~
01.01.37a | "Ah, well done!" — this cry, smooth and deep, raised in the sky by beings out of joy, resounded through the three worlds.
01.01.37b | ~
01.01.38a | An auspicious wind blew, all the directions shone open with radiance, and heavenly showers of flowers, together with water, fell.
01.01.38b | ~
01.01.39a | Then the deities of knowledge — Intellect (buddhi), Accomplishment (siddhi), Memory (smṛti), Retention (medhā), Steadfastness (dhṛti), Fame (kīrti), Forbearance (kṣamā) and Compassion (dayā) — entered Agniveśa and the others headed by him.
01.01.39b | ~
01.01.40a | And those treatises (tantra) of theirs, approved by the supreme sages, gained an established place on earth for the welfare of the multitudes of beings.
01.01.40b | ~
01.01.41a | That in which wholesome and unwholesome life, happy and unhappy life, what is wholesome and unwholesome for it, its measure, and life itself are described, is called Āyurveda (the science of life).
01.01.41b | ~
01.01.42a | The union of body, senses, mind (sattva) and self (ātman) is life (āyus); it is called by the synonyms dhāri (sustaining), jīvita (living), nityaga (ever-moving) and anubandha (continuity).
01.01.42b | ~
01.01.43a | The science (veda) of that life, regarded by the knowers of the Vedas as the most sacred, which is beneficial to humans in both worlds, will be taught.
01.01.43b | ~
01.01.44a | Sameness (sāmānya) is always the cause of increase of all entities, and difference (viśeṣa) the cause of decrease; but application belongs to both.
01.01.44b | ~
01.01.45a | Sameness (sāmānya) produces oneness, while difference (viśeṣa) produces separateness; sameness is indeed likeness of things, and difference is the opposite.
01.01.45b | ~
01.01.46a | Mind (sattva), self and body — this triad is like a tripod; by their union the world (of living beings) stands, and in it everything is established.
01.01.46b | ~
01.01.47a | That (union) is the person (pumān); it is sentient; and it is declared the subject matter of this science; for its sake this science has been revealed.
01.01.47b | ~
01.01.48a | Ether and the others, the self, mind, time and the directions — this is the group of substances (dravya). A substance with senses is sentient; one without senses is insentient.
01.01.48b | ~
01.01.49a | The qualities (guṇa) are declared to be: the sense objects (artha), together with heaviness and the rest (guru etc.), intellect (buddhi), and para and the rest ending with effort (prayatna). Movement preceded by effort is called action (karma).
01.01.49b | ~
01.01.50a | Inherence (samavāya) is held to be the inseparable existence of earth and the other substances with their qualities; it is eternal, for where there is a substance, quality is not unfixed there.
01.01.50b | ~
01.01.51a | That in which actions and qualities reside and which is the inherent (samavāyin) cause is substance (dravya); quality (guṇa), however, is an inherent but motionless cause.
01.01.51b | ~
01.01.52a | That which, residing in a substance, is the cause of conjunction and disjunction, and is the activity of what is to be done, is action (karma); action depends on nothing else.
01.01.52b | ~
01.01.53a | Thus the cause has been stated. Here the effect (kārya) is said to be the equilibrium of the dhātus, and the act of bringing about that equilibrium is declared the purpose of this treatise.
01.01.53b | ~
01.01.54a | Wrong, deficient (non-) and excessive conjunction of time, intellect and sense objects — this is the threefold summary of the causes of diseases, which have two seats.
01.01.54b | ~
01.01.55a | The body and that which is called sattva (the mind) are held to be the seat of diseases, and likewise of well-being (sukha); the cause of well-being is balanced conjunction.
01.01.55b | ~
01.01.56a | The self, however, is changeless and supreme; together with the mind, the qualities of the elements and the senses, it is the cause of consciousness; it is eternal and the seer, for it sees actions.
01.01.56b | ~
01.01.57a | Vāyu (Vāta), Pitta and Kapha are stated to be the group of bodily doṣas; the mental group, on the other hand, is indicated as Rajas and Tamas.
01.01.57b | ~
01.01.58a | The former (the bodily group) is pacified by remedies based on the divine (daiva-vyapāśraya) and on reasoning (yukti-vyapāśraya); the mental group by knowledge, understanding, fortitude, memory and concentration (samādhi).
01.01.58b | ~
01.01.59a | Vāta (māruta, wind) is dry, cold, light, subtle, mobile, non-slimy (viśada) and rough; it is pacified by substances with opposite qualities.
01.01.59b | ~
01.01.60a | Pitta is unctuous (sasneha), hot, sharp, liquid, sour, flowing (sara) and pungent; it is quickly pacified by substances with opposite qualities.
01.01.60b | ~
01.01.61a | The qualities of Kapha (śleṣman) — heavy, cold, soft, unctuous, sweet, stable and slimy — are pacified by opposite qualities.
01.01.61b | ~
01.01.62a | Disorders regarded as curable are reversed by medicines of opposite qualities, administered with due regard to place, dose and time.
01.01.62b | ~
01.01.63a | But treatment of incurable diseases is not taught. Further, from here on, the qualities and actions will be stated substance by substance.
01.01.63b | ~
01.01.64a | Taste (rasa) is the object of the tongue; its substratum is water and also earth; in its formation and its differentiation the three beginning with ether are the causes.
01.01.64b | ~
01.01.65a | Sweet, sour, salty, pungent, bitter and astringent — this set of six is held to be the summary of the tastes (rasa).
01.01.65b | ~
01.01.66a | Sweet, sour and salty tastes overcome Vāyu (Vāta); astringent, sweet and bitter overcome Pitta; astringent, pungent and bitter overcome Kapha.
01.01.66b | ~
01.01.66.1a | Pungent, sour and salty tastes aggravate Pitta; sweet, sour and salty aggravate Kapha; and pungent, bitter and astringent aggravate Vāta (samīraṇa).
01.01.66.1b | ~
01.01.67a | Some substances pacify the doṣas, some vitiate the dhātus, and some are approved for maintaining health — thus substance (dravya) is said to be threefold.
01.01.67b | ~
01.01.68a | That (substance) again is declared threefold: animal (jaṅgama), plant-born (audbhida) and earthly (pārthiva). Honeys, cow products, bile, fat, marrow, blood, flesh,
01.01.68b | ~
01.01.69a | faeces, urine, skin, semen, bone, sinew, horn, nails, hooves, head hair, body hair and rocanā are the things used from animals.
01.01.69b | ~
01.01.70a | Gold, the five metals (loha) with their dross (mala), along with sand, lime (sudhā), realgar (manaḥśilā) and orpiment (āla), gems, salt, red ochre (gairika) and añjana (collyrium mineral) —
01.01.70b | ~
01.01.71a | — are indicated as earthly medicine. The plant-born, however, is fourfold: vanaspati, vīrudh, vānaspatya and oṣadhi.
01.01.71b | ~
01.01.72a | Vanaspati is known by its fruits, vānaspatya by flowers and also fruits; oṣadhis are those whose life ends with the ripening of their fruit; and vīrudhs are known by their spreading runners.
01.01.72b | ~
01.01.73a | Root, bark, heartwood, exudate, stalk, expressed juice, tender leaves, alkalis, milky latex, fruit, flower, ash, oils, thorns,
01.01.73b | ~
01.01.74a | leaves, leaf-buds, bulbs and shoots: this is the plant (audbhida) group. The root-drugs (mūlinī) are declared to be sixteen and the fruit-drugs (phalinī) nineteen.
01.01.74b | ~
01.01.75a | There are four great fats (mahāsneha) and just five salts; eight urines are enumerated, and likewise eight milks.
01.01.75b | ~
01.01.76a | And six trees for purification (śodhana) have been pointed out by Punarvasu. He who knows how to apply these in disorders is a knower of the Veda.
01.01.76b | ~
01.01.77a | Hastidantī, haimavatī, śyāmā, trivṛt, adhoguḍā, saptalā, the one named śvetā, pratyakśreṇī and also gavākṣī,
01.01.77b | ~
01.01.78a | jyotiṣmatī, bimbī, śaṇapuṣpī, viṣāṇikā, ajagandhā, dravantī, and kṣīriṇī, the sixteenth here.
01.01.78b | ~
01.01.79a | Śaṇapuṣpī, bimbī and also haimavatī are to be used in emesis (vamana), and śvetā and jyotiṣmatī in head-evacuation (śīrṣavirecana, errhine therapy).
01.01.79b | ~
01.01.80a | The eleven that remain are to be used in purgation (virecana). Thus the root-drugs have been described by name and action; now hear the fruit-drugs.
01.01.80b | ~
01.01.81a | Śaṅkhinī, then viḍaṅga, trapuṣa and madana; dhāmārgava, then ikṣvāku, jīmūta and kṛtavedhana; and klītaka, declared to be of two kinds, marsh-grown and land-grown,
01.01.81b | ~
01.01.81c | ~
01.01.82a | prakīryā and udakīryā, pratyakpuṣpā and likewise abhayā, antaḥkoṭarapuṣpī, and the autumn-gathered fruit of hastiparṇī,
01.01.82b | ~
01.01.83a | the fruit of kampillaka and of āragvadha, and that of kuṭaja. Dhāmārgava, then ikṣvāku, jīmūta, kṛtavedhana,
01.01.83b | ~
01.01.84a | madana, kuṭaja, trapuṣa and hastiparṇinī: these are to be used in emesis (vamana) and in decoction enemas (āsthāpana).
01.01.84b | ~
01.01.85a | Pratyakpuṣpā is prescribed for evacuation through the nose. The ten that remain are stated for purgation (virecana).
01.01.85b | ~
01.01.86a | The nineteen fruits have been described by name and action. Ghee, oil, muscle fat and marrow: fat (sneha) is taught as fourfold,
01.01.86b | ~
01.01.87a | for drinking, massage, enema and nasal medication, according to application. They are oleating, vitalising, good for the complexion, and increase strength and bodily bulk.
01.01.87b | ~
01.01.88a | These fats are prescribed as removers of Vāta, Pitta and Kapha. Sauvarcala, saindhava (rock salt), viḍa and audbhida as well,
01.01.88b | ~
01.01.89a | together with sea salt (sāmudra), these are the five salts. They are unctuous, hot, sharp and the most kindling of the digestive fire.
01.01.89b | ~
01.01.90a | They are used for plasters, likewise in the procedures of oleation and sudation, in downward and upward evacuation, in decoction enemas (nirūha) and in unctuous enema (anuvāsana),
01.01.90b | ~
01.01.91a | in massage, for food, in evacuation of the head, in surgical operations, for suppositories (varti), and in collyria and unctions,
01.01.91b | ~
01.01.92a | and in indigestion and distension (ānāha), in Vāta disorder, in gulma (abdominal lump), colic and udara (abdominal enlargement). The salts have been described; next learn from me the eight urines,
01.01.92b | ~
01.01.93a | the principal ones, all of which are taught in the teaching of Ātreya: sheep urine, goat urine, cow urine, and that of the buffalo,
01.01.93b | ~
01.01.94a | elephant urine, and that of the camel, the horse and the donkey. (Urine is) hot, sharp, not dry, pungent and accompanied by salt taste.
01.01.94b | ~
01.01.95a | Urine is used in unction, used in plasters, used in decoction enema (āsthāpana), and used also in purgation,
01.01.95b | ~
01.01.96a | it is used also in sudations, in distension and in antidotes, in abdominal enlargements (udara) and in piles, and for those suffering from gulma, skin disease (kuṣṭha) and kilāsa (white patches of skin, leucoderma);
01.01.96b | ~
01.01.97a | it is used in poultices (upanāha) and likewise in affusion (pariṣeka). It is taught to be kindling of the digestive fire, destroying poison and destroying worms.
01.01.97b | ~
01.01.98a | and it is said to be the best relief for those afflicted with pāṇḍu disease (pallor, anaemia). When drunk it pacifies Kapha (śleṣman) and moves wind (māruta, Vāta) in its proper direction,
01.01.98b | ~
01.01.99a | and draws Pitta out downwards. Thus the summary of its properties has been told by me in general terms; it will now be described separately.
01.01.99b | ~
01.01.100a | Sheep urine is slightly bitter, unctuous and not hostile to Pitta. Goat urine is astringent and sweet, wholesome, and destroys the doṣas.
01.01.100b | ~
01.01.101a | Cow urine has some sweetness, is somewhat doṣa-destroying, and dispels worms and skin disease (kuṣṭha). When drunk it relieves itching and is properly beneficial in udara (abdominal enlargement) caused by the doṣas.
01.01.101b | ~
01.01.102a | Buffalo urine destroys piles, swelling (śopha) and udara, is alkaline and laxative (sara). Elephant urine is salty and beneficial for those suffering from worms and skin disease.
01.01.102b | ~
01.01.103a | It (elephant urine) is recommended for obstruction of faeces and urine, poison, Kapha disorders and piles. Camel urine is said to be slightly bitter and to destroy dyspnoea (śvāsa), cough and piles.
01.01.103b | ~
01.01.104a | That of horses is bitter and pungent and removes skin disease, wounds and poison. Donkey urine destroys epilepsy (apasmāra), insanity (unmāda) and seizure by spirits (graha).
01.01.104b | ~
01.01.105a | Thus the urines have been described here according to their efficacy and application. Next the milks will be described, with their action and their properties.
01.01.105b | ~
01.01.106a | Sheep milk, goat milk, cow milk and that of the buffalo; then that of she-camels, of she-elephants, of the mare and likewise of woman.
01.01.106b | ~
01.01.107a | Milk is generally held to be sweet, unctuous, cold and promoting of breast milk; it is nourishing, bulk-promoting (bṛṃhaṇa), aphrodisiac, good for the intellect, strengthening and invigorating to the mind,
01.01.107b | ~
01.01.108a | vitalising, removing fatigue, removing dyspnoea and cough; it destroys śoṇitapitta (bleeding disorder) and is a uniter of what is injured,
01.01.108b | ~
01.01.109a | (Milk) is wholesome (sātmya) for all living beings, both pacifying (śamana) and purifying (śodhana), thirst-quenching and appetising (dīpanīya), and the best thing for the wasted and the injured (kṣīṇa-kṣata).
01.01.109b | ~
01.01.110a | It is especially (beneficial) in anaemia (pāṇḍu-roga), acid dyspepsia (amlapitta), wasting (śoṣa), abdominal lump (gulma) and abdominal enlargement (udara), and in diarrhoea (atīsāra), fever, burning sensation and swelling (śvayathu).
01.01.110b | ~
01.01.111a | In disorders of the female genital tract (yoni) and of semen (śukra), in scanty urine, and in hard, knotted faeces, it is wholesome for those suffering from Vāta and Pitta disorders.
01.01.111b | ~
01.01.112a | In nasal therapy (nasya), plasters, immersion baths, emesis (vamana), decoction enemas (āsthāpana), purgation (virecana) and oleation (snehana): milk is used everywhere.
01.01.112b | ~
01.01.113a | The properties of the milk of each (animal), one by one and in due order, I shall describe again in full in the chapter beginning with 'annapāna' (food and drink).
01.01.113b | ~
01.01.114a | Now there are three other trees, separate from the fruit- and root-yielding plants: snuhī, arka and aśmantaka. Their action, one by one, is as follows.
01.01.114b | ~
01.01.115a | One should know aśmantaka (as used) for emesis and the latex of snuhī for purgation; the latex of arka is to be known (as used) for emesis together with purgation.
01.01.115b | ~
01.01.116a | They name these three other trees whose barks are beneficial: pūtīka, kṛṣṇagandhā and likewise the tree tilvaka.
01.01.116b | ~
01.01.117a | Pūtīka and likewise tilvaka are to be used for purgation; kṛṣṇagandhā is prescribed in spreading skin disease (parīsarpa), in swellings and in piles (arśas),
01.01.117b | ~
01.01.118a | and in ringworm (dadru), abscess (vidradhi), glandular swellings (gaṇḍa), skin diseases (kuṣṭha) and alajī eruptions. The discerning (physician) should also know these six trees as purifying (śodhana).
01.01.118b | ~
01.01.119a | Thus have been stated the fruit- and root-yielding plants, the unctuous substances (sneha), the salts, urine, the milks, and the six trees whose latex and bark were indicated.
01.01.119b | ~
01.01.120a | Goatherds, shepherds, cowherds and the other forest-dwellers know the plants in the forest by their names and forms.
01.01.120b | ~
01.01.121a | No one can know the highest mastery of plants by mere knowledge of their names, or again by knowledge of their forms.
01.01.121b | ~
01.01.122a | But one who knows their application (yoga), even without knowing their form, is called a knower of their true nature; how much more, then, the physician who knows the plants in every respect!
01.01.122b | ~
01.01.123a | He who knows their application, adapted to place and time, after examining each person individually, is to be known as the best of physicians.
01.01.123b | ~
01.01.124a | Like poison, like a weapon, like fire, like a thunderbolt is a medicine that is not understood; a medicine well understood is like nectar (amṛta).
01.01.124b | ~
01.01.125a | For a medicine not recognised by the three, name, form and properties, and even one that is known but wrongly applied, leads to harm.
01.01.125b | ~
01.01.126a | Through proper application even a potent poison may become an excellent medicine, and a medicine wrongly applied becomes a potent poison.
01.01.126b | ~
01.01.127a | Therefore a wise person who desires life and health should not accept any medicine administered by a physician who is devoid of rational planning (yukti).
01.01.127b | ~
01.01.128a | Vāsava's (Indra's) thunderbolt falling on the head might still leave something remaining; but a medicine prescribed by an ignorant person would not leave the patient with anything remaining.
01.01.128b | ~
01.01.129a | He who, thinking himself wise, gives a medicine without knowing it to a suffering, bed-ridden and trusting patient,
01.01.129b | ~
01.01.130a | with that man, who has abandoned dharma, sinful, death incarnate, evil-minded, a person would fall into hell even by conversing.
01.01.130b | ~
01.01.131a | Better to drink a serpent's venom or boiled (molten) copper, or even to swallow iron balls heated in fierce fire,
01.01.131b | ~
