01.19.1 | Now, therefore, we shall expound the chapter on the eight udaras (Aṣṭodarīya).
01.19.2 | Thus said the venerable Ātreya.
01.19.3 | Here, indeed, [there are] eight udaras (abdominal diseases), eight mūtrāghātas (obstructions of urine), eight defects of milk, eight defects of semen; seven kuṣṭhas, seven eruptions (piḍakā), seven visarpas; six diarrhoeas (atisāra), six udāvartas, five gulmas, five disorders of the spleen, five coughs, five dyspnoeas, five hiccups (hikkā), five morbid thirsts (tṛṣṇā), five vomitings, five grounds for not eating food, five diseases of the head, five heart diseases, five pāṇḍu diseases, five insanities; four apasmāras, four eye diseases, four ear diseases, four coryzas (pratiśyāya), four mouth diseases, four grahaṇī disorders, four intoxications (mada), four faintings, four wastings (śoṣa), four impotences (klaibya); three swellings, three kilāsas, lohitapitta of three kinds; two fevers, two wounds (vraṇa), two āyāmas, two gṛdhrasīs, two kāmalās, āma of two kinds, vātarakta of two kinds, arśas of two kinds; one ūrustambha (stiffness of the thighs), one saṃnyāsa, one mahāgada (great disease); twenty kinds of worms, twenty pramehas, twenty disorders of the female genital tract (yonivyāpad). Thus forty-eight headings of diseases have been enumerated in this compendium.
01.19.4-1 | These we shall specify in the order of the enumeration. 'Eight udaras': the udaras of Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, sannipāta (all three combined), the spleen, obstruction (baddha), perforation (chidra) and water (daka). 'Eight obstructions of urine': those born of Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, sannipāta, stone (aśmarī), gravel (śarkarā), semen and blood. 'Eight defects of milk': discoloration, bad smell, bad taste, sliminess, massing of froth, roughness, heaviness and excess of unctuousness. 'Eight defects of semen': [semen that is] thin, dry, frothy, not white, putrid, very slimy, mixed with other dhātus, and sinking.
01.19.4-2 | 'Seven kuṣṭhas': kapāla, udumbara, maṇḍala, ṛṣyajihva, puṇḍarīka, sidhma and kākaṇa. 'Seven eruptions': śarāvikā, kacchapikā, jālinī, sarṣapī, alajī, vinatā and vidradhī. 'Seven visarpas': those named Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, agni (fire), kardamaka, granthi and sannipāta.
01.19.4-3 | 'Six diarrhoeas': those born of Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, sannipāta, fear and grief. 'Six udāvartas': those born of flatus (vāta), urine, faeces, semen, vomiting and sneezing.
01.19.4-4 | 'Five gulmas': those born of Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, sannipāta and blood. 'Five disorders of the spleen' are explained by the gulmas. 'Five coughs': those born of Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, injury (kṣata) and wasting (kṣaya). 'Five dyspnoeas': the great, the upward, the interrupted, tamaka and the minor. 'Five hiccups': the great (mahatī), the deep (gambhīrā), the intermittent (vyapetā), the minor (kṣudrā) and the food-born (annajā). 'Five morbid thirsts': those of the nature of Vāta, Pitta, āma, wasting and secondary affection (upasarga). 'Five vomitings': one born of contact with disliked objects, and those arising from the predominance of Vāta, Pitta, Kapha and sannipāta. 'Five grounds for not eating food': Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, sannipāta and aversion. 'Five diseases of the head': taking together the former enumeration, those born of Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, sannipāta and worms. 'Five heart diseases' are explained by the diseases of the head. 'Five pāṇḍu diseases': those born of Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, sannipāta and eating clay. 'Five insanities': those caused by Vāta, Pitta, Kapha, sannipāta and exogenous factors (āgantu).
01.19.4-5 | 'Four apasmāras': those caused by Vāta, Pitta, Kapha and sannipāta. Four eye diseases, four ear diseases, four coryzas, four mouth diseases, four grahaṇī disorders, four intoxications and four faintings are explained by the apasmāras. 'Four wastings': those born of over-exertion (sāhasa), suppression (sandhāraṇa), depletion (kṣaya) and irregular eating. 'Four impotences': from damage to the seed, from breakdown of the penis (dhvaja), from old age, and from depletion of semen.
01.19.4-6 | 'Three swellings': those caused by Vāta, Pitta and Kapha (śleṣman). 'Three kilāsas': red, coppery and white. 'Lohitapitta of three kinds': going upward, going downward and going both ways.
01.19.4-7 | 'Two fevers': one that desires heat and arises from cold, and one that desires cold and arises from heat. 'Two wounds': the endogenous and the exogenous. 'Two āyāmas': the external and the internal. 'Two gṛdhrasīs': from Vāta, and from Vāta and Kapha. 'Two kāmalās': one based in the trunk (koṣṭha) and one based in the periphery (śākhā). 'Āma of two kinds': alasaka and visūcikā. 'Vātarakta of two kinds': deep and superficial. 'Arśas of two kinds': dry and moist.
01.19.4-8 | 'One ūrustambha': arising from āma and the three doṣas. 'One saṃnyāsa': of the nature of the three doṣas, seated in mind and body. 'One mahāgada (great disease)': atattvābhiniveśa (attachment to what is not true).
01.19.4-9 | 'Twenty kinds of worms': lice (yūkā) and pipīlikās, these two kinds born of external dirt; keśāda, lomāda, lomadvīpa, saurasa, audumbara and jantumātṛ, these six born of blood; antrāda, udarāveṣṭa, hṛdayāda, curu, darbhapuṣpa, saugandhika and mahāguda, these seven born of Kapha; kakeruka, makeruka, leliha, saśūlaka and sausurāda, these five born of faeces. 'Twenty pramehas': udakameha, ikṣubālikārasameha, sāndrameha, sāndraprasādameha, śuklameha, śukrameha, śītameha, śanairmeha, sikatāmeha and lālāmeha, these ten caused by Kapha (śleṣman); kṣārameha, kālameha, nīlameha, lohitameha, mañjiṣṭhāmeha, haridrāmeha, hastimeha and madhumeha, these four caused by Vāta: thus [there are] twenty pramehas. 'Twenty disorders of the female genital tract': the Vātika, Paittika, Śleṣmika and Sānnipātika, these four born of the doṣas; the remaining sixteen are designated by their naming after the combination of doṣa and dūṣya and after their nature, namely: raktayoni, arajaskā, acaraṇā, aticaraṇā, prākcaraṇā, upaplutā, pariplutā, udāvartinī, karṇinī, putraghnī, antarmukhī, sūcīmukhī, śuṣkā, vāminī, ṣaṇḍhayoni and mahāyoni: thus there are twenty disorders of the female genital tract.
01.19.4 | And this entire enumeration has been specified in the order of the enumeration.
01.19.5 | All endogenous disorders indeed arise from nothing other than Vāta, Pitta and Kapha. Just as a bird, though flying about the whole day, does not go beyond its own shadow, so all disorders caused by imbalance of one's own dhātus do not go beyond Vāta, Pitta and Kapha.
01.19.P1 | Further, the intelligent, having carefully observed the particulars of site, form and nature of Vāta, Pitta and Kapha (śleṣman), designate all disorders too, which have these as their nature, as just those [doṣas].
01.19.6a | And here there are two: The many groups of disorders in the body that are born from imbalance of one's own dhātus are not separate from Pitta, Kapha and Vāta (anila); but the exogenous ones are indeed distinct from them.
